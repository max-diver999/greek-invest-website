**Quick answer:** The UAE does not tax personal income, so nothing about a Greek purchase creates a
filing obligation at home. That removes the entire subject most of these pages are about and replaces it
with a different one: Greek anti-money-laundering compliance, which is where UAE-based buyers actually
encounter difficulty. The tax question is simple and the banking question is not.

**The Greek process is covered elsewhere.** Tier thresholds, the certified area test, the letting ban, the AFM and account sequence, and the due diligence that precedes a deposit apply identically here and are set out at [€400,000 and €800,000 tier map](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/), [120 m² certified usable area test](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/), [short-term rental ban on the qualifying asset](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/), [AFM and Greek bank account sequence](/guides/greece-golden-visa-bank-account-afm/) and [cadastre and engineer due diligence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/). This page is about the funding trail and the absence of tax at home.

## No income tax at home means no relief to claim

Greek rental income is taxed in Greece at 15% to 45%, charged on gross rent with only a flat 5% repairs
allowance. For a UAE-resident individual that is the end of it: there is no UAE personal income tax, so
there is no second charge and equally no credit to claim.

That cuts both ways and the second half is the one buyers miss. A German or a Canadian who pays Greek tax
gets relief against tax at home. A UAE resident has no home tax to relieve, so the Greek charge is
absorbed in full. The effective rate on Greek rental income for a Gulf-based owner is simply the Greek
rate, and it should be modelled at the Greek rate rather than at the blended figure an international
adviser might quote.

The exit is the mirror image and it is favourable. Greek capital gains tax on property has been
suspended, and the UAE does not tax individual capital gains, so a disposal inside the suspension window
can be genuinely untaxed on both sides. Confirm the suspension's current end date before relying on it.

## Circular 1/2026 is where the transaction actually gets difficult

The requirement is precise: the purchase price must arrive from abroad through a Greek credit
institution, into an account in the buyer's own name. A Greek compliance officer then has to satisfy
himself about where the money came from, and the shapes that Gulf wealth commonly takes are shapes his
framework handles awkwardly.

| Source of funds | Why it is straightforward | Why a Greek compliance desk may query it |
|---|---|---|
| Salary from a named employer | Payslips and contract are standard evidence | Rarely queried |
| Free-zone company distributions | Legitimate and common in the UAE | The company's own accounts and ownership chain may be requested |
| Proceeds of a Dubai property sale | Documented by the sale contract and the DLD record | Foreign-language title records need certified translation |
| Exchange-house transfers | Ordinary in the Gulf | Non-bank intermediaries sit outside a European compliance officer's normal map |
| Family or gifted funds | Common | The donor's own source of funds is usually requested as well |

None of these is a reason for refusal. All of them are reasons for delay, and delay is the actual cost:
a file that stalls at the bank stalls the deed, and a preliminary agreement with a fixed completion date
does not pause while a compliance query is answered.

## The documentary sequence that avoids the delay

Assemble the pack before the first transfer rather than in response to a query. In practice the file that
clears fastest contains:

1. Passport, Emirates ID and proof of UAE address.
2. A clear statement of the source: employment, business distribution, asset sale or gift.
3. Six to twelve months of bank statements showing the funds accumulating rather than appearing.
4. For business income: the trade licence, the ownership chain to a natural person, and recent accounts.
5. For an asset sale: the contract, the transfer record and the receipt of proceeds.
6. Certified translations of anything not in English or Greek.
7. A single transfer route, bank to bank, in the buyer's own name, with no intermediary.

The last line matters more than the rest combined. A transfer that arrives from a company account, a
relative's account or an exchange house — even where the underlying money is unimpeachable — fails the
Circular's own test and has to be redone.

## Residency, not tax, is usually the actual objective

Most UAE-based buyers already hold UAE residency through employment or an investor visa, and that
residency is conditional on maintaining the underlying position. A Greek permit is bought as a hedge
against that conditionality rather than as a relocation: it is not tied to an employer, it carries no
minimum stay, and it gives Schengen access independent of any Gulf status.

That reframes what the purchase should optimise for. A buyer treating the permit as insurance wants a
clean, low-maintenance asset with a straightforward title and a tenant, not a trophy that needs managing
from another country. It also means the Greek property is frequently held alongside, rather than instead
of, a Dubai portfolio, and the two behave very differently: one is a permit attached to a deed, the other
is an income asset.

## The zero rate belongs to where you live, not to who you are

Every calculation on this page rests on there being no UAE tax to set against the Greek charge. That is a
statement about UAE residence, not about the buyer, and residence is the variable most likely to move
over a Greek holding period measured in years.

Two ways it moves. The first is that UAE residency is conditional and renewable rather than permanent, as
this page has already noted, so it ends when the employment, the investor status or the qualifying holding
behind it ends. The second is that people leave for their own reasons. In either case the person becomes
tax resident somewhere else, and the Greek rental income and any eventual gain arrive in a system that
does tax them — possibly with credit for the Greek tax, possibly on a different base, and certainly with
filing obligations that did not previously exist. Nothing about the Greek property changes. The whole
answer changes.

The practical version: model what this asset looks like in the two or three jurisdictions you might
plausibly be resident in later, not only in the one you are resident in now. For many Gulf-based buyers
that is a country of citizenship they may return to, and for some of them — Americans above all — the
home-country charge never went away in the first place.

There is a second variable on the same theme. The UAE introduced a federal corporate tax that applies to
businesses and to natural persons carrying on business activity above a turnover threshold, while
personal investment income and personal real estate investment held by an individual sit outside its
scope. That distinction is doing real work if a buyer is considering holding the Greek property through a
UAE company or a free-zone entity, because a corporate holder is inside a regime an individual holder is
not, with its own registration, filing and substance considerations.

For a Golden Visa buyer the point is easily settled, because Circular 1/2026 requires the purchase price
to arrive in an account in the buyer's own name and the qualifying asset to sit on a single deed in that
name. Personal ownership is what the permit wants. Where a corporate structure is being proposed for
reasons outside the Golden Visa, get the UAE corporate tax position confirmed before the structure is
built rather than after. The cost of getting this wrong is not a tax adjustment, it is the file. A price
that arrives from a company account rather than from the buyer's own fails the Circular 1/2026 trail
whatever the underlying funds are, and the remedy is to unwind and re-do the transfer, which on an
€800,000 deed means moving the money twice, paying the currency spread twice, and restarting a Ministry
queue that already runs 12 to 18 months. Settle who owns the property before the first euro moves.

## Advantages and disadvantages for a UAE-based buyer

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| No home-country tax on Greek rental income or gains | No home tax also means no credit — the Greek charge is absorbed in full |
| A disposal in the Greek suspension window can be untaxed on both sides | Greek tax on gross rent at up to 45% is the effective rate, not a blended one |
| The permit is independent of any employer or Gulf visa status | Source-of-funds documentation is the real cost, measured in weeks |
| Schengen access with no minimum stay | Free-zone and exchange-house money invites additional queries |

## Risks a UAE-based buyer should price before signing

- **Transferring from the wrong account.** Company, family or exchange-house routes fail Circular 1/2026
  regardless of the underlying legitimacy.
- **Signing a completion deadline before the bank file is cleared.**
- **Modelling Greek tax at a blended international rate** when there is no second jurisdiction to blend
  with.
- **Assuming a Dubai property sale documents itself** to a European standard without certified
  translation.
- **Relying on the capital gains suspension** without confirming its current end date.

## Buyer scenarios that fit

**The salaried professional hedging visa conditionality.** The cleanest file: documented income, a single
transfer, a straightforward apartment let long-term. This is the profile the Greek process handles best.

**The business owner distributing from a free zone.** Workable, and start the compliance file first. Build
the ownership chain to a natural person before approaching a Greek bank rather than after.

**The buyer recycling Dubai sale proceeds.** Perfectly ordinary, and the documentation is the work. Get
the sale record translated and certified in advance, and route the proceeds through your own account
before they move to Greece.

## What the Greek charge actually costs when nothing offsets it

Because there is no UAE tax to relieve, the Greek rate is the effective rate. On an €800,000 Attica
apartment letting at €2,600 a month, gross rent is €31,200 a year and the Greek bill lands near €6,774,
an effective 21.7% of gross before ENFIA, management, vacancy or maintenance. A Gulf-based owner should
read that as final: there is no second jurisdiction to blend it with and no credit to reclaim, so a model
built on a blended international rate overstates the net by several percentage points.

Carry the same
arithmetic down the stack and the gap widens. Management on a long let runs 8% to 15% of rent, ENFIA is
assessed annually on the state's objective value, and a vacancy allowance and maintenance reserve come out
of what is left. On the €31,200 of gross rent above, management at the mid-point takes something over
€3,500 before a single repair is authorised, and none of it is deductible against the Greek charge, which
is computed on gross with only the flat 5% allowance. The practical consequence is that the spread between the gross yield in a
listing and the figure reaching a Dubai account is wider here than the headline percentages suggest. On the €31,200 above, the Greek tax alone has already taken more than a fifth before any
of that is counted, which is why a headline gross yield quoted for Attica and a net figure an owner
actually banks are two different numbers with a wide space between them.

## Why the compliance file is harder here than the tax file

The reason a UAE purchase takes longer than a German or Canadian one has nothing to do with the buyer and
everything to do with how Gulf wealth is held. A European compliance officer is reading for a pattern he
recognises: regular income into a named account, accumulating over time, from an identifiable employer or
a business whose ownership resolves to a person.

Gulf wealth frequently does not present that way, for entirely legitimate reasons.

| What the file shows | Why it is normal in the UAE | What the desk asks next |
|---|---|---|
| A large lump sum arriving recently | Property cycles and business distributions are lumpy | Where was this money six months ago |
| Ownership through a free-zone entity | The standard corporate form for expatriate business | The trade licence and the chain to a natural person |
| Multiple currencies in the trail | Regional business is multi-currency by default | Conversion records and the originating account |
| A transfer routed through an exchange house | Ordinary retail practice across the Gulf | A bank-to-bank replacement, because the Circular requires it |

None of these is an accusation. All of them are questions, and questions take weeks. The buyer who
prepares the answers before the first transfer completes in the ordinary timetable; the one who responds
to queries as they arrive adds a month or more to a deed that may have a fixed completion date.

## The permit as a hedge against visa conditionality

UAE residency for most expatriates is conditional on something: an employer, an investor visa tied to a
business, a property visa tied to holding that property. It is renewable rather than permanent, and it
ends with the underlying condition.

That is the actual purchase rationale for most Gulf-based buyers, and it reframes what the Greek asset
should be. A hedge wants to be low-maintenance, cleanly titled, easily let on a twelve-month lease and
straightforward to hold from abroad. It does not want to be a trophy that needs managing across a time
zone, nor a project that needs a builder.

It also means the Greek property usually sits alongside a Dubai portfolio rather than replacing it, and
the two behave differently. One is a permit attached to a deed and should be underwritten for durability.
The other is an income asset and can be underwritten for yield.
