**Quick answer:** Kallithea is an inner-Athens district organised around Syngrou Avenue, with two
universities, the hospital corridor, an office spine and the Stavros Niarchos Foundation park along or
beside it. That combination produces something most Attica districts do not: a dense, year-round tenant
who does not own a car. Since a Golden Visa asset may only be let on twelve-month residential terms, a
district built around exactly that tenant is worth more to a permit holder than the yield table suggests.

Kallithea is Attica, so the threshold is €800,000 with 120 square metres of certified main usable area.
At Kallithea prices that buys a great deal of floor space, which moves the whole question to what is
actually scarce here.

## Syngrou Avenue is the district's economy, not its edge

Most residential districts are defined by what they are near. Kallithea is defined by what runs through
it. Syngrou is one of the main arteries between central Athens and the coast, and the institutions along
and beside it — Panteion University, the University of West Attica campus, the hospital cluster, the
office buildings, and the Niarchos cultural centre and park — are employers and enrolments rather than
amenities.

That gives the district a tenant profile with three useful properties for a long-let owner:

- **It renews annually.** Student and junior-staff tenancies turn over on a predictable cycle rather than
  unpredictably.
- **It does not depend on tourism.** The short-term-rental ban on a qualifying deed removes nothing this
  district was built on.
- **It is not seasonal.** February demand looks like October demand, which is not true of any coastal
  page in this collection.

## In a grid this dense, parking is the premium

Kallithea sits in one of the tightest street grids in Attica. Buildings run to the plot line, streets are
narrow, and off-street parking is genuinely scarce rather than merely desirable.

For a buyer this reorders the usual checklist. Aspect and quiet matter more than floor level; an interior
courtyard orientation can be worth more in rent than a higher floor on the avenue, because Syngrou is
loud and continuous. A parking space attached to the title is a real and transferable asset, and one of
the few features in this district that reliably shortens a void.

Note that a parking space is not countable toward the 120 square metres of κύρια χρήση, however much it
adds to the rent. The same is true of storage. On stock where the marketed area includes both, the
certified enclosed figure can come back materially lower than the listing, which the
[120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) sets out in full.

## What €800,000 does at inner-Athens prices

Kallithea's entry per square metre is well below the Riviera and below prime central districts, so the
Attica threshold buys size comfortably. The practical effect is that a Golden Visa buyer here is not
hunting for a unit large enough to qualify; they are deciding what to do with the surplus.

Three shapes that surplus usually takes, and what each does to the permit:

| Approach | Works for a Golden Visa file? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| One large apartment on a single deed | Yes | The straightforward route; certify the enclosed area |
| Two smaller apartments in the same building | No | The rule is one property on one title; areas cannot be added |
| A large unit plus a separate parking title | Only on the residential title | The parking deed is a second property and counts for nothing |

The second row is the common error in low-€/m² districts and it is worth stating plainly: buying two
€400,000 flats in Kallithea does not produce a Golden Visa. It produces two flats.

## Older stock, and what an engineer will and will not certify

Much of Kallithea's building stock dates from the 1960s to the 1980s. That is where the value is and it
is also where the paperwork risk sits: enclosed verandas, converted semi-basements, added rooftop rooms
and internal reconfigurations that never reached a permit.

For an ordinary buyer those are negotiating points. For a Golden Visa buyer they are eligibility items,
because an un-legalised alteration is not counted toward main usable area and can complicate the title
altogether. Ask for the Electronic Building Identity and the engineer's certificate before the
preliminary agreement, and treat any discrepancy between the listing area and the certified area as the
real number.

## Where Kallithea sits against its neighbours

Against [Piraeus](/areas/piraeus-property-investment/) at the same tier: both are dense, both let
year-round, and the difference is the employer. Piraeus tenants work around a harbour whose operations
shape the street; Kallithea tenants work in institutions that do not care which way the window faces.

Against the Riviera: [Glyfada](/areas/glyfada-property-investment/) costs two to three times as much per
square metre and buys amenity, sea access and capital position. Kallithea buys floor area and a tenant.
For a permit holder who will not live in the property, the second is the more useful purchase, and it is
the reason inner-Athens districts outperform the Riviera on net yield despite the postcode.

### Risks and what to check before committing

The eligibility risk here is documentary rather than financial. Ask for the Electronic Building Identity
and the engineer's certificate before the preliminary agreement, and treat the certified enclosed figure
as the real area regardless of the listing. Confirm any parking or storage sits on the same title or
understand that it counts for nothing toward the 120 m². And read the building's own regulation, because
in a dense grid the shared costs and the rules on alteration matter more than in a low-density suburb.

## What the acquisition costs, and what the tax does to the rent

An Attica deed at €800,000 carries transfer tax of about €24,720 at 3.09%, notary and registration at
1.5% to 2.0%, legal fees from €2,500, and engineer certification of €800 to €1,500. Golden Visa state
fees add €2,000 per adult and €1,000 per dependent under 21, which puts the all-in figure near €856,000
to €880,000.

The tax on the income matters more here than in most districts, because Kallithea's case rests on rent.
Greek rental income is taxed on gross rent rather than on profit, with only a flat 5% repairs allowance
set against it: 15% on the first €12,000, 25% from €12,001 to €24,000, 35% from €24,001 to €35,000 and
45% above that. A unit letting for €18,000 a year therefore pays 15% on the first €12,000 and 25% on the
balance, before ENFIA, management, vacancy or maintenance are counted at all.

## Kallithea in one paragraph

Kallithea is Attica's middle-ring coastal district: metro-linked to central Athens, tram-connected to the southern coast, and priced well below the Riviera postcodes it borders. For Golden Visa buyers that combination matters, because Kallithea sits inside the €800,000 Attica prime tier under Law 5100/2024 while offering stock that still clears the 120 m² usable-area test on a single deed. The trade-off is honest: you pay the Attica threshold without paying Glyfada or Voula prices, and you accept a tenant profile driven by commuters and students rather than by seasonal demand. Long-term letting is the only option on the qualifying asset, so underwrite twelve-month leases, not summer nightly rates.
