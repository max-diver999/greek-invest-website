**Quick answer:** Crete averaged about €2,329 per square metre in April 2025, up 12.5%
in a year, ranging from about €1,999 in Heraklion Prefecture to about €2,935 in Chania
Prefecture, with the town of Chania itself near €3,690. The island falls in the
€400,000 Golden Visa tier, where the average price clears the 120 square metre area
rule comfortably. What separates Crete from every other Greek island is scale: it is
large enough to function as a region rather than as a destination.

## What the island asks

| Where | Asking price per m² | Source and reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Chania town | about €3,690 | Indomio, August 2026 |
| Chania Prefecture | about €2,935 | Indomio, August 2025 |
| Crete, island average | about €2,329 | Indomio, April 2025 |
| Heraklion Prefecture | about €1,999 | Indomio, August 2025 |

Asking prices across Crete rose 12.5% year on year to April 2025. As everywhere on
this site these are asking prices from Indomio listings rather than recorded
transactions, and the [market overview](/property-for-sale/) sets out why that
distinction matters.

Against the alternatives, Crete sits close to [Rhodes](/property-for-sale/rhodes/) at
about €2,421 and below [Corfu](/property-for-sale/corfu/) at about €2,932 in its main
municipality. It is more expensive than the [Peloponnese](/property-for-sale/peloponnese/)
at about €1,778 and a third of [Mykonos](/property-for-sale/cyclades/) at €7,623.

## The only island large enough to be a region

Crete is not a bigger version of a Greek island. It is a different kind of place, and
that is the argument for it.

It carries two cities. Chania and Heraklion both have hospitals, universities, courts,
year-round commerce and airports with winter service. It has an agricultural economy,
olive oil and wine and produce, that exists independently of tourism and employs people
through the year. It has mountains that hold snow, a south coast with a different
climate from the north, and a permanent population large enough that the island does
not empty in October.

Resort strips empty as they do everywhere. The island does not, and the difference
between those two statements is the whole case.

The practical consequences are the same four that make [Rhodes](/property-for-sale/rhodes/)
work, at greater scale: works can happen in winter, resale is not confined to a summer
window, healthcare is on the island rather than a flight away, and a real long-let
market exists alongside the holiday one.

That is why Crete absorbs more long-term foreign residents than any other Greek island,
and why it appears on almost every shortlist that starts with "somewhere we could
actually live".

## The yield calculation, and its limits

Crete is the only Greek island in this research where both halves of a yield
calculation are published in the same place for the same period, so it is worth doing
carefully and then stating what it is not.

For Chania Prefecture in August 2025, asking rent was about €9.79 per square metre per
month against an asking price of about €2,935 per square metre.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---:|
| Monthly asking rent per m² | €9.79 |
| Annualised | about €117 |
| Asking price per m² | €2,935 |
| Implied gross yield | about 4.0% |

Three caveats, and they matter more than the number.

**It is asking-on-asking.** Neither figure is a transaction. Sellers and landlords
both start high, and the two errors do not necessarily cancel.

**It is gross.** The Greek rental income scale reaches 45% from 2026 with only a flat
5% deduction for repairs against gross rent, so the net figure is considerably lower.
The [rental income guide](/guides/greece-rental-income-tax-non-resident/) sets out the
bands.

**It assumes full-year letting.** On a property let seasonally the annualisation does
not hold.

With those attached, 4.0% is a sane order of magnitude. It sits below the Athens
average of 5.43% and near the national 4.40%, which is what a large island with a
functioning long-let market should look like: better than a seasonal island, worse
than a capital city.

Asking rent in Lasithi Prefecture was about €6.93 per square metre per month over the
same period, the lowest on the island. The corresponding price figure is not published,
so no equivalent yield can be calculated honestly, and this page does not estimate one.

## The prefectures are four different markets

**Chania**, the west. The most expensive, the prettiest, the most internationally
known, and where most foreign buying concentrates. The Venetian harbour town is the
image people have of Crete. Prefecture asking prices near €2,935 and the town itself
near €3,690.

**Rethymno**, between them. A smaller Venetian town, a long beach, and prices between
Chania and Heraklion. Less foreign competition than Chania and more character than
Heraklion.

**Heraklion**, the centre and the island's commercial capital. The cheapest prefecture
at about €1,999 and the most working-city in character. Knossos, the main port, the
biggest airport. Overlooked by foreign buyers precisely because it is a city doing a
job rather than a postcard.

**Lasithi**, the east. Agios Nikolaos and Elounda at the top end, quieter agricultural
country behind, and the lowest asking rents on the island.

The spread between Chania town and Heraklion Prefecture is nearly two to one, and
they are ninety minutes apart on a national road.

## North coast and south coast are different climates

Almost every price figure quoted for Crete describes the north. That is where the two
cities, both airports, the main port and nearly all the foreign buying sit, along the
national road that runs the length of the island.

The south is a separate proposition. The White Mountains and the Psiloritis range cut
the island in half, and the south coast beyond them is hotter, drier, less built and
reached by mountain roads rather than by the national road. Places like Plakias,
Paleochora and Ierapetra are an hour or more over a pass from the north, and in winter
those passes see snow. Prices are lower, the light is different, and the practical
question is not the view but whether a specialist appointment, a builder or a delivery
will make the journey.

Buyers who fall for the south usually do so on a summer visit and underestimate the
drive. Buyers who want the south for what it is, quieter and warmer and further from
everything, get a genuinely different island for less money. Both are reasonable; they
are just not the same purchase, and a price per square metre quoted for the island does
not distinguish between them.

## The distances are real distances

Crete runs about 260 kilometres end to end, and that changes how a purchase works
compared with any other island on this site.

Two international airports, Heraklion and Chania, both with winter service, sit about
140 kilometres apart. A property in Lasithi in the east is roughly three hours by road
from Chania in the west. The lawyer, the notary, the engineer and the tax office are
organised by prefecture, so a purchase in one part of the island is not administered
from another.

The practical consequence is that "Crete" is not a search area. Choosing a prefecture
comes before choosing a property, because it determines which airport visitors use,
which hospital is nearest, which professionals handle the file, and how long the drive
is when something needs attending to. On a small island none of that is a decision. Here
it is the first one.

## Golden Visa: comfortable, except in Chania town

Crete sits in the €400,000 tier throughout, since it is outside Attica and is not
named in the prime zone under Law 5100/2024.

At the island average near €2,329 per square metre, €400,000 corresponds to roughly
172 square metres, which clears the 120 square metre main usable area requirement with
room. In Heraklion Prefecture at about €1,999 it corresponds to roughly 200 square
metres, more comfortably still.

In the town of Chania at about €3,690, the same €400,000 corresponds to roughly 108
square metres, which does not clear the threshold. So the same trap that appears on
[Lefkada](/property-for-sale/lefkada/) and on [Corfu's](/property-for-sale/corfu/)
north-east coast applies to Crete's most expensive town, and only there.

The [tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones.
The tier follows the cadastral parcel rather than the island's name, and the letting
restriction applies as everywhere: twelve-month leases only on a qualifying property.

## What the portals will not answer

Search results for Crete property belong to Rightmove, A Place in the Sun, Spitogatos
and the Cretan agencies, all with live inventory. This page is not competing with a
database and a buyer looking for listings should use them.

What none of them addresses is which prefecture suits which buyer, why Chania costs
nearly twice Heraklion, whether the 12.5% rise is a trend or a listing-mix artefact,
what the yield arithmetic actually supports, or where the Golden Visa area rule bites
on this island. Those are the decisions, and the inventory does not contain them.

## What to check on Crete specifically

The standard [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies,
with three local emphases.

**Building legality on rural stone stock.** Extensions and enclosed terraces built
across generations are common inland, and the
[engineer's certificate](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) is what stops
a sale completing without them being resolved.

**Water on the south coast and inland.** Crete is drier than the Ionian, and water
supply on rural plots is a real question rather than an assumption.

**Archaeology.** Dense across the island, particularly around Heraklion, and capable
of constraining a plot or halting works.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer who wants to actually live in Greece.** Crete is the strongest answer among
the islands, because the services, the healthcare and the year-round economy exist at
scale. Chania for character, Heraklion or Rethymno for value.

**A buyer with €400,000 taking a Golden Visa file.** Comfortable across most of the
island, where that sum clears the area rule with room. Not comfortable in Chania town
itself, where it buys about 108 square metres against a 120 square metre requirement.

**A buyer looking for yield.** Crete is the island where the arithmetic can at least be
checked, at roughly 4.0% gross on asking figures in Chania Prefecture. Heraklion
should do better on the same logic, being cheaper. Neither beats Athens at 5.43%, and
both are gross rather than net.

## Risks and red flags

**Projecting the 12.5% forward.** It measures asking prices over one twelve-month
window and is not a return.

**Reading the island as one market.** Chania town is nearly twice Heraklion Prefecture,
ninety minutes apart.

**Treating the 4.0% as a net yield.** It is gross, asking-on-asking, and assumes
year-round letting.

**Assuming the €400,000 tier is enough everywhere.** In Chania town the area rule binds
first.

**Underestimating water and legality inland.** The two rural questions that most often
turn a cheap Cretan house into an expensive one.
