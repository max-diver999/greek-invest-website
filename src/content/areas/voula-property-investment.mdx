**Quick answer:** Voula is the residential half of the Athenian Riviera's centre. It sits in the
municipality of Vari-Voula-Vouliagmeni rather than in Glyfada, which for a Golden Visa file changes
nothing — both are Attica at €800,000 — and for everything after the permit changes a great deal. Voula
has no commercial high street, a constrained supply of developable coastal land, and a buyer pool made of
people who intend to live there.

At €3,500 to €5,500 per square metre the €800,000 threshold clears 120 square metres without difficulty.
The questions worth spending time on here are supply, noise and the exit.

---

## A different municipality, and why that is not a technicality

Vari-Voula-Vouliagmeni is one municipality; Glyfada is another. They share a coastline and almost nothing
administratively.

| | Voula (Vari-Voula-Vouliagmeni) | [Glyfada](/areas/glyfada-property-investment/) |
|---|---|---|
| Character | Residential, low commercial frontage | The Riviera's commercial centre |
| Local economy | Serves residents | Retail, offices, marina, services |
| Supply pipeline | Constrained; little developable coastal land left | Larger, with redevelopment of older stock |
| Typical buyer | End-user who will occupy | Mixed: occupiers, investors, businesses |
| Evening character | Quiet | Loud, and deliberately so |

Local charges, planning politics and permitting sit with different councils, which matters over a
multi-year hold. So does the pipeline: a municipality with little land left cannot answer a demand
increase with new supply, and that supports price and suppresses transaction volume at the same time.

## An end-user market behaves differently at both ends

Because Voula's buyers mostly intend to live in what they buy, two things follow that a yield table will
not show.

Purchases are slower and more considered. Deals here are less likely to be signed on a spreadsheet and
more likely to hinge on layout, orientation, floor level, a garden or a specific school run. That means a
seller who has an unusual property waits longer for the buyer who wants that property.

And resale liquidity is narrower than Glyfada's, which is narrower again than central Athens. For a
Golden Visa holder that is a planning point rather than a defect: the permit already implies a long hold,
because selling the qualifying asset removes the basis for the permit. What matters is knowing the exit
is a matter of finding the right household, not of clearing a market.

## What €800,000 buys, and what is actually scarce

| Position | Indicative €/m² | What €800,000 buys | Against the 120 m² floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seafront and first line | ~€5,500 | ~145 m² | Clears |
| Central Voula, walkable to the coast | €4,300 to €5,000 | ~160 to 185 m² | Comfortable |
| Voula hillside and set-back streets | €3,500 to €4,200 | ~190 to 228 m² | Comfortable |

Size is not the constraint here, which frees a buyer to spend the budget on the things that actually
differentiate Riviera stock: a quiet orientation away from Poseidonos Avenue, a genuine parking space,
outdoor area that is usable rather than nominal, and floor level.

One caution that applies to the third row in particular. Older set-back stock offers the most square
metres per euro and carries the highest incidence of unpermitted alteration — enclosed verandas,
converted basements, additions. Those do not count toward main usable area and can complicate title, so
the engineer's certificate has to come before the preliminary agreement. The
[120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) sets out what is countable.

## The income the permit allows, against what the district supports

A qualifying deed may only be let on twelve-month residential terms; short-term tourist letting of it is
prohibited nationwide for the life of the permit. Voula supports long lets — families, professionals,
staff at the schools and clinics along the coast — but at Riviera prices the gross yield is thin, in the
same band as Glyfada and for the same reason: the purchase price reflects capital position and the rent
reflects what a household pays.

Buyers who want seasonal income alongside the permit need a second, non-qualifying property registered
under ΑΜΑ. That is the only compliant structure, and on the Riviera it is a substantial second commitment
rather than a marginal one.

## Who Voula suits

A buyer who wants to be on the Riviera, will occupy the property at least part of the year, values quiet
over convenience, and is content with a slow, end-user resale market. The 120 m² rule is easy here and the
supply constraint works in an owner's favour over a long hold.

It suits badly an investor optimising yield, who should be in inner Athens at a third of the entry price,
and badly anyone who wants restaurants and shops on the doorstep — that is one municipality north, and
choosing wrongly between the two is the most common mistake made on this stretch of coast.

## Who this is for, and the risks that come with it

| Buyer profile | Fit | The trade being made |
|---|---|---|
| Occupier wanting the Riviera without the noise | Strong | A slower, end-user resale market |
| Permit-first with €800,000 | Good | 145 to 228 m² certified is achievable; gross yield of 4.5 to 5.5% is not the reason to buy |
| Family with school-age children | Strong | Quiet and services, at a premium over inland Attica |
| Yield investor | Poor | Inner Athens pays similar rent at a third of the entry price |

### Risks and what to check

The municipality runs from the coast up into the pine slopes behind, and the two halves behave differently: seafront and first-line stock trades on aspect and is the tightest supply in the municipality, while the hillside streets above Vouliagmenis Avenue offer more area per euro and depend on a car. A buyer who intends to let long-term should weigh the walk to the coast road and the tram against the extra square metres, because a Voula tenant is usually a household rather than a commuter and values the garden more than the stop.

Supply constraint cuts both ways: it supports price over a long hold and it means little new stock, so a
buyer waits for the right property rather than choosing from many. Older set-back stock carries the
highest incidence of unpermitted alteration in the municipality, and those metres are not certifiable.
Confirm which municipality a listing actually sits in, because agents market the whole coast as
"Glyfada area" and the [cost of buying](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-greece/) and local charges differ
by council. And verify the property class on the permit before assuming a ground-floor unit is
residential.

## What a Voula purchase costs to complete

An €800,000 Attica deed carries transfer tax of about €24,720 at 3.09%, notary and registration at 1.5%
to 2.0% of value, legal fees from €2,500 upward, and an engineer's certificate at €800 to €1,500. Golden
Visa state fees add €2,000 for the main applicant, €150 per adult family member and €16 per residence card, with minors exempt from the application fee. The realistic all-in
figure lands between €856,000 and €880,000 before furnishing.

Two Voula-specific lines are worth adding to that model. ENFIA on Riviera stock sits in the upper municipal
bands, and Vari-Voula-Vouliagmeni sets its own municipal charges separately from Glyfada, so a like-for-like
comparison across the two councils needs the actual assessment rather than a coastal average. And where a
unit is a first delivery from a developer, 24% VAT applies instead of the 3.09% transfer tax — a difference
above €150,000 on a deed this size, and one that belongs in writing from your own lawyer before the
preliminary agreement. Long-let gross yields in Voula sit in the 4.5% to 5.5% band, in line with Glyfada, and the rental income itself is taxed on gross rent rather than profit: 15% on the first €12,000, 25% to €24,000, 35% to €35,000 and 45% above, with only a flat 5% repairs allowance to set against it. Model the tax before the yield, not after. The [120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) covers what the
engineer's certificate must show for the file to stand.
