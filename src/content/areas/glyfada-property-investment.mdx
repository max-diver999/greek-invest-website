**Quick answer:** Glyfada sits in the €800,000 Attica Golden Visa tier, and unlike the premium Cyclades
at the same threshold the 120 square metre requirement is rarely the problem here. At €3,000 to €5,500
per square metre, €800,000 buys roughly 145 square metres on prime marina-adjacent stock and comfortably
more a few streets back. What a buyer is actually choosing is a location with a genuine year-round
economy: a commercial high street, a marina, golf, and a tram that runs into central Athens.

The trade is yield. Long-let gross of 3.8% to 4.5% is thin by Greek standards, and it is thin for a
reason worth understanding rather than working around.

---

## Glyfada is where the Riviera does business, not where it sleeps

The Athenian Riviera is usually described as a single corridor. It is not. Glyfada is its commercial
centre, shops, restaurants, offices, professional services and the marina, while Voula, Vouliagmeni
and Varkiza to the south are predominantly residential and Alimos and Elliniko to the north are in
transition.

That distinction has consequences a price table does not show:

- **The tenant exists in February.** A district with an amenity economy houses people who work in it, so
  the twelve-month lease a Golden Visa deed is restricted to has a natural counterparty.
- **Ground-floor stock is not residential.** Commercial frontage on the main avenues prices differently
  and is not a Golden Visa asset; verify the property class on the permit rather than the marketing.
- **Noise and parking are the local constraints**, not distance to the sea. A quiet orientation and a
  parking space carry a premium here that they do not carry in Vouliagmeni.

Glyfada is also its own municipality, which is worth noting only because
[Voula](/areas/voula-property-investment/) belongs to Vari-Voula-Vouliagmeni and the two therefore run
different local charges, different planning politics and different supply pipelines despite sharing a
coastline.

## The tram is the piece of infrastructure that changes the tenant

The tram line connecting the southern suburbs to central Athens is the reason Glyfada's rental market
does not depend on car ownership. For a foreign owner letting on twelve-month terms, that widens the
tenant pool from people who drive to people who commute, a materially larger group, and one less
exposed to the cost of running a car in Athens.

It also shapes which streets let well. Proximity to a stop matters more to a long-term tenant than
proximity to the water, and the two do not correlate. Buyers underwriting a Golden Visa asset here
should walk the route from the specific building to the nearest stop before treating a coastal address
as a rental advantage.

## Where €800,000 lands on the price curve

| Position | Indicative €/m² | What €800,000 buys | Against the 120 m² floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marina front and prime seafront | ~€5,500 | ~145 m² | Clears |
| Central Glyfada, near the high street | €4,200 to €5,000 | ~160 to 190 m² | Comfortable |
| Residential streets set back | €3,400 to €4,000 | ~200 to 235 m² | Comfortable |
| Older stock needing renovation | ~€3,000 | ~265 m² | Comfortable, but check what is certifiable |

The headroom is the point. On Santorini or Mykonos the €800,000 buyer is choosing between the view and
the permit; in Glyfada both fit inside the budget, and the decision moves to layout, floor level,
parking and orientation.

One caution on the bottom row. Older Riviera stock offers the most square metres per euro and the
highest probability of unpermitted alterations, enclosed verandas, converted basements, added floors, which affect what an engineer can certify as main usable area. Cheap metres are only useful if they are
countable, and the [120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) explains what the
certificate accepts.

## Why the yield is thin, and why that is not a defect

Glyfada's long-let gross of 3.8% to 4.5% sits below working-class Athens districts, where the same rent
is paid against a much lower purchase price. Buyers here are paying for capital position and amenity, and
the rent does not rise to meet the price.

That makes Glyfada a poor choice for a yield-first investor and a reasonable one for a buyer whose
priority is a stable asset with a deep resale market and a permit attached. Short-term letting of the
qualifying property is prohibited nationwide for the life of the permit, so the seasonal income that
partly justifies island pricing is unavailable here too, but unlike the islands, Glyfada was never
structured around it, so the ban removes less.

## What the Ellinikon project does and does not do to this market

The Ellinikon regeneration on the former airport site to the north is the largest development in Attica
and it is changing the northern end of the Riviera corridor. Glyfada is adjacent to it rather than inside
it, which means the effect on Glyfada is second-order: a new neighbourhood selling new units at
substantially higher prices per square metre resets what buyers consider expensive nearby, without adding
supply to Glyfada itself.

Two practical points follow. Glyfada's own stock is largely 1970s to 2000s and is not competing on
specification with new-build. And a buyer weighing the two should read
[Ellinikon](/guides/ellinikon-athens-property-investment/) directly rather than inferring it from Glyfada
pricing, because the products, the delivery timelines and the risks are different in kind.

## The high street is an asset and a boundary

Glyfada's commercial spine is the reason the district has its name recognition, and it is also the reason
the price map turns over a very short distance. The shopping streets around the centre carry the Riviera's
densest retail and restaurant concentration, and the residential streets immediately behind them are
priced and lived in quite differently from those three or four blocks back.

Close in, you get walkability, a year-round commercial environment and the strongest resale story in the
district, and you pay for it with evening noise, delivery traffic and street parking that is contested at
every hour the shops are open. Further out toward the residential blocks, the same money buys more floor
area, a quieter street and usually a parking space, at the cost of needing a car for anything.

That gradient matters more here than in most Athens districts because it operates over a few hundred
metres rather than across a whole neighbourhood. Two apartments with the same specification, the same
price per square metre in the listing and the same postcode can face completely different tenants. Walk
the specific street at the specific hour you expect a tenant to be home. This is not a district you can
assess from a map.

The seasonal split is the second thing the high street does. Glyfada's commercial centre runs harder in
summer, when the coastal traffic and the beach clubs add to the resident base, and settles into a
domestic rhythm in winter. Unlike an island, it does not empty: the offices, the schools and the resident
population are there in February, but a buyer viewing in July is seeing the district at its loudest and
busiest, and one viewing in January is seeing a different place. Both are accurate; neither is complete.

A related point on the ground floor: retail and residential sit in the same buildings here, and a
first-floor apartment above a bar is a different proposition from the identical unit above a pharmacy,
including at resale. Check what the commercial units below actually are and what their licences permit
them to become.

The parking question deserves separating out, because in this district it is not a convenience item.
Off-street parking in the streets near the commercial centre is scarce enough to be a distinct line in
the price, and a unit without it is competing for a narrower tenant pool in a district where almost every
household runs a car. Where a listing includes a space, establish that it is on the same title rather than
an informal arrangement with the building, because the two are not the same asset and only one of them
survives a sale.

For a long-let owner, which is what a Golden Visa asset must be, the winter version is the one that
matters. Your tenant signs a twelve-month lease and lives through both. Ask the agent what the street is
like out of season, and treat a summer-only impression of Glyfada as half the information.

## Who Glyfada suits

A buyer who wants an Attica address with a working local economy, a straightforward path through the
120 m² rule, and the deepest resale market on the Riviera. It is the most conventional €800,000 purchase
this site covers, and conventional is an advantage when the asset has to support a permit for years.

It suits badly a yield-first investor, who will do better in central Athens at a fraction of the entry
price, and badly anyone who wants quiet: the high street is the point of the place, and it is loud.
Buyers who want the coastline without the commerce should look one municipality south.

### Risks and what to check before an offer

The main risk in Glyfada is not the market but the specific building. Older stock carries unpermitted
alterations, enclosed verandas, converted basements, added floors, which do not count toward main usable
area and can complicate title. Ground-floor commercial frontage is not a Golden Visa asset whatever the
listing says, so the property class on the permit needs checking. And noise exposure on Poseidonos and
the main avenues is permanent, not a matter of double glazing.

## What a Glyfada purchase costs to complete

The €800,000 threshold is the qualifying investment, not the capital required. On an Attica deed of that
size the acquisition stack adds roughly 7% to 10%, which puts the realistic all-in figure between
€856,000 and €880,000 before furnishing.

Transfer tax at 3.09% is about €24,720. Notary and registration run 1.5% to 2.0%, legal fees €2,500 to
€5,000 or more depending on complexity, and the engineer's certification €800 to €1,500. Golden Visa
state fees add €2,000 for the main applicant, €150 per adult family member and €16 per residence card, with minors exempt from the application fee. Where the unit is a first
delivery from a developer, 24% VAT may apply in place of transfer tax, and on a deed this size the
difference is large enough that it should be settled in writing before the preliminary agreement.
