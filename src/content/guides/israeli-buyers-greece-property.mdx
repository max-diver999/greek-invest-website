## Why Israeli Buyers Are Choosing Greece in 2026

Greece offers Israeli investors a combination that few jurisdictions match simultaneously: EU-linked residency through a regulated property route, Schengen mobility without minimum stay, direct real estate ownership in a euro-denominated market, and lifestyle infrastructure, beaches, healthcare, international schools, kosher-adjacent dining in Athens, that Israeli families already know from holidays.

The 2025 nationality data crystallises the trend. Greece approved 636 Golden Visa permits for Israeli nationals, up 91.5% from the prior year. Turkish buyers grew even faster at roughly 160% to 3,291 permits, and UK applicants rose 50.8% to 797, but Israeli growth stands out because it reflects a relatively recent acceleration rather than a decade-long base. Chinese investors remain the cumulative majority at approximately 47.9% of all approvals, which frames Israeli demand as a diversifying second wave rather than market dominance.

Structural tailwinds reinforce the nationality data. Spain closed its Golden Visa to real estate in 2025, removing the closest Mediterranean competitor. Portugal no longer accepts residential property for its Golden Visa. Greece remains the primary EU Mediterranean route where a buyer can hold a deed, not a fund unit. Law 5100/2024 raised thresholds and banned short-term rentals on qualifying assets, but the core value proposition, five-year renewable residency, family inclusion, no minimum physical presence, survived intact.

For Israeli buyers specifically, Greece also functions as a geographic hedge. Athens is closer than London or Zurich, Crete offers a second-home culture Israelis already understand from domestic coastal markets, and the euro denomination provides currency diversification relative to shekel-denominated domestic portfolios.

**Athens Riviera (Glyfada, Voula, Vouliagmeni, Alimos).** The southern Attica coast is Israel's prestige address in Greece. International schools, marina infrastructure, restaurant density, and a thirty-minute airport connection attract Israeli families who want EU residency alongside a usable second home. Prices run €3,500 to €6,000+ per square metre on quality stock, making the €800,000 tier the realistic entry for 120 m². **Central and southern Athens.** Buyers prioritising Golden Visa efficiency over beach proximity target Kallithea, Nea Smyrni, Palaio Faliro, and Marousi, districts where €800,000 still buys 120 to 140 m² on well-connected metro lines. This corridor suits Israeli investors who want urban tenancy demand and minimal lifestyle dependency on the asset.

**Crete (Chania, Heraklion, Apokoronas).** The €400,000 regional tier makes Crete the capital-efficiency choice. At regional averages of €1,800 to €2,500 per square metre, Israeli buyers often secure 150 to 220 m² with coastal access, impossible in Attica at the same budget. Long-term yields run 5.0 to 6.0% gross in urban Chania and Heraklion. The trade-off is thinner international resale liquidity versus Athens and a flight time that adds ninety minutes from Tel Aviv.

## Short-Term Rental Ban: What Israelis Must Understand

Israeli buyers accustomed to Tel Aviv and Eilat short-stay economics sometimes receive agent pitches positioning Golden Visa apartments as high-yield STR assets. That framing is misleading for the qualifying property. The permitted income model is long-term residential lease only, typically twelve-month contracts to professionals, students, or families.

The notary is a neutral public officer who validates the deed. They do not act for you, do not search title, and do not confirm Golden Visa eligibility. Beyond that universal point, three things make a Greek lawyer specifically necessary for Israeli buyers.

**Document chain from Israel.** Powers of attorney, marriage and birth certificates and company documents executed in Israel need apostille through the Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs and certified translation into Greek. Hebrew-source documents add a translation step that Greek notaries will not waive, and getting the sequence wrong is the most common cause of a missed signing appointment for Israeli buyers.

**Remote completion is the norm.** Most Israeli purchases complete by power of attorney rather than in person. The POA has to be drafted in Greek to a scope your Greek lawyer specifies, then executed and apostilled in Israel. Allow two to three weeks and keep the authority narrow, limited to executing a deed on terms already agreed in writing.

**Treaty position needs coordinating, not assuming.** The exclusive taxing rights under Articles 6 and 13(1) are favourable, but they interact with Israeli disclosure obligations that your Greek lawyer cannot advise on. The workable structure is a Greek lawyer for title, area and deed, plus an Israeli accountant for reporting, briefed on each other's positions before funds move.

## How Israel taxes your Greek property

Israeli buyers have the most favourable treaty position of any nationality in this series, and it rests on two specific articles.

**Article 6: exclusive taxing rights on rental income.** Under the Israel–Greece double taxation treaty, income from immovable property is taxed only in the state where the property is located. That is exclusive rather than shared: Greek rental income is taxed in Greece at 15% to 45% and is not taxed again in Israel. This differs from the credit mechanism that applies to Americans, Canadians and Indians, where the income is assessed twice and relief is given for tax already paid.

**Article 13(1): the same treatment for capital gains.** Gains on the disposal of immovable property are likewise taxed only in the state where the property sits. Combined with the Greek suspension of capital gains tax on property through the end of 2026, an Israeli resident selling a Greek property inside the suspension window has no Greek CGT to pay and no Israeli charge on the gain either. That is a genuinely unusual outcome and it deserves confirmation from an Israeli adviser on your specific facts rather than reliance on a general statement.

**Reporting still applies even where tax does not.** Exclusive taxing rights remove the Israeli tax charge; they do not remove Israeli disclosure obligations on foreign assets and accounts. A Greek bank account opened for the Circular 1/2026 fund trail, and the property itself, both sit inside Israeli reporting requirements. Confirm the current thresholds with your accountant before the purchase rather than at the next filing.

**Practical banking.** Greek compliance teams are familiar with Israeli buyers, and the source-of-funds documentation that satisfies them is standard: evidence of the funds in Israel, the SWIFT trail, and a clear line from the remitter to the name on the Greek deed. Where funds come from an Israeli property sale, keep that deed and its tax clearance in the pack.

**Where the treaty does not help.** The treaty governs taxation, not the Golden Visa. Israeli buyers face the same 120 m² certified usable area rule, the same single-deed requirement, and the same prohibition on short-term letting the qualifying asset as everyone else.
