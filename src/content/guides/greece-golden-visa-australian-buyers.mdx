## Superannuation and SMSF: Why Direct Purchase Generally Fails

Australian buyers frequently ask whether they can use superannuation funds, particularly self-managed super funds, to buy Greek Golden Visa property. The short answer is generally no. Australian superannuation law requires funds to meet a sole-purpose test: providing retirement benefits to members. The ATO views residential property purchased for personal use, family residency, or Golden Visa permit acquisition as failing that test.

**Specific constraints:**

1. **SMSF cannot buy residential property for member use.** Even if the property is rented to third parties most of the time, the residency component fails sole-purpose rules.
2. **Overseas property carries additional compliance burden** and valuation requirements that few SMSF auditors support for non-Australian jurisdictions.
3. **Golden Visa residency benefit** accrues to the member personally, not to the fund, which the ATO views as a non-retirement benefit.

Some Australian advisers have explored complex structures involving commercial property purchase in Greece, where an SMSF buys an eligible commercial conversion under the €250,000 tier and treats it purely as investment property with no personal use. This remains a niche strategy requiring specialist SMSF legal and tax advice, and Golden Visa applicants should not assume it is viable without independent confirmation.

**Typical Australian funding sources for Greek Golden Visa:**

- Personal savings and offset accounts
- Equity release from Australian residential property
- Sale proceeds from investment properties or shares
- Inheritance or family gifts
- Business sale proceeds

Australian buyers should consult an Australian tax adviser on capital gains tax implications of selling assets to fund the Greek purchase, and whether Greek rental income or capital gains on eventual resale trigger Australian tax obligations under the Greece to Australia tax treaty.

## Short-Term Rental Ban: What Australians Must Understand

Australian buyers accustomed to coastal STR markets in Byron Bay, Noosa, or Margaret River sometimes receive agent pitches positioning Golden Visa apartments as high-yield STR assets. That framing is misleading for the qualifying property. The permitted income model is long-term residential lease only, typically twelve-month contracts to professionals, students, or families.

The Greek notary authenticates the deed and acts for neither side. For Australians there are three reasons a buyer-side Greek lawyer is not optional.

**No treaty means the structuring decisions matter more.** With no comprehensive double tax agreement between Australia and Greece, how the property is held, who appears on the deed, and how presence in Greece is managed all carry consequences that a treaty would otherwise smooth over. Your Greek lawyer sets the deed; your Australian adviser sets the tax position. They need to be talking before the preliminary agreement, not after.

**Distance makes the power of attorney the whole transaction.** Almost every Australian purchase completes by POA. It must be executed before a notary in Australia, apostilled under the Apostille Convention through DFAT, and translated into Greek. Allow three weeks for the chain and a further week for courier time, and keep the scope narrow.

**Diaspora property carries inherited-title risk.** Many Australian buyers are purchasing in a village their family came from, sometimes from relatives. Greek succession law routinely leaves property with multiple heirs holding unequal shares, some untraceable, and a family relationship makes buyers less rather than more likely to insist on a twenty-year title search. Insist anyway.

## Risks Australian Buyers Should Price Before Signing


Two risks are larger for Australians than for other buyers and both stem from distance and the missing treaty. AUD-to-euro exposure runs from the offer to completion on a purchase carrying a 7% to 10% cost stack, so the FX strategy belongs at the offer stage rather than the deposit. And with no double tax agreement, Greek tax above your Australian liability on the same income is lost rather than carried forward, which changes the after-tax yield materially.
**Currency risk** on AUD-to-euro conversion at offer stage. Mitigation: lock FX strategy with adviser before deposit; model total euro cost including 7 to 10% acquisition stack.

**Permit timeline risk**, buyers planning immediate Schengen reliance may face 8 to 14 months between deed and permit. Mitigation: maintain existing travel documents during processing; do not book long European trips based on purchase alone until permit issued.

**Flight-distance risk** for property management and inspections. Mitigation: appoint Greek property manager for tenant liaison and maintenance; budget annual inspection trip.

## Common Mistakes Australian Buyers Make


The distinctively Australian error is assuming a self-managed super fund can hold the property. The sole-purpose test generally prohibits an SMSF from acquiring residential property that provides a present-day benefit to a member, and a residency permit is exactly such a benefit, so the structure fails before the Greek rules are even reached. Take Australian advice on ownership structure before the preliminary agreement, not after the deed.
**Assuming SMSF can buy.** Australian superannuation sole-purpose test generally prohibits residential property for member use or personal residency benefits like Golden Visa.

**Skipping engineer certificate on resale stock.** Older Attica and island buildings frequently carry unauthorised extensions that block clean resale and complicate GV files.

**Ignoring ENFIA at purchase stage.** Annual property tax on a €400,000 Crete property runs €800 to €2,000; on an €800,000 Athens property, €2,000 to €5,000. Model holding costs before yield calculations.

## How Australia taxes your Greek property

Australians are in the most exposed position of any nationality covered on this site, and the reason is structural rather than a matter of rates. Australia and Greece have no comprehensive double tax treaty, only an Airline Profits Agreement, so relief on Greek rental income runs through Australia's domestic Foreign Income Tax Offset rather than a treaty mechanism, and there is no tie-breaker article to resolve dual residence.

**There is no comprehensive double tax treaty between Australia and Greece.** The two countries have an Airline Profits Agreement covering the taxation of airline operations, and nothing broader. As of March 2026 the Australian Ambassador to Greece and the Greek Minister of Finance were advancing talks towards a bilateral Double Taxation Agreement, but no comprehensive treaty is in force. Every other major buyer nationality in this series has one.

**What that means in practice.** Greek rental income is taxed in Greece at 15% to 45% as the source country. The Australian Taxation Office then assesses the same income as part of your worldwide income at your marginal rate. Relief comes from Australia's domestic Foreign Income Tax Offset (FITO) rules rather than from a treaty, and the offset is capped: it cannot exceed the Australian tax payable on that foreign income. Where Greek tax exceeds the Australian liability on the same income, the excess is simply lost rather than carried forward as it might be under a treaty mechanism.

**No treaty tie-breaker on residence.** If you spend more than 183 days a year in Greece you become a Greek tax resident on worldwide income while potentially remaining an Australian resident under the ATO's domicile and 183-day tests. Without a treaty there is no tie-breaker article to resolve dual residence, which is exactly the situation a Golden Visa holder planning eventual relocation can drift into. This is the single strongest argument for Australian buyers to take advice before, not after, changing their pattern of presence.

**CGT on exit.** Greek capital gains tax is suspended through the end of 2026, so a sale in the window attracts no Greek tax. Australian CGT still applies to the gain on a property held by an Australian resident, with the 50% discount available on assets held longer than twelve months. As with Americans, no Greek tax paid means no offset to claim.

**Watch the treaty negotiations.** If a comprehensive DTA is concluded, the relief position for Australian owners of Greek property changes materially. Any Australian buyer should have their adviser track that rather than assume the current position is permanent.
