## Does Greece Tax Inheritance When the Deceased Was a Foreign Resident?

Greek banks, the Ktimatologio, and AADE all treat the asset as Greek-situs. Heirs can owe estate or inheritance tax at home as well, but the double-tax treaties Greece holds with the UK, Germany, France, the US and Australia allocate primary taxing rights on immovable property to Greece, with foreign tax credits available in the other jurisdiction.

The practical sequence for heirs resembles a compressed purchase in reverse. A Greek lawyer identifies the asset, confirms cadastre title, calculates the taxable base, prepares the acceptance declaration, and coordinates payment or deferral of inheritance tax before the heir's name appears on the register. Non-resident heirs almost always act through a [power of attorney for Greek property](/guides/power-of-attorney-property-greece/) because multiple in-person steps at notary and tax office are required.

| Heir residence abroad | Triggers non-resident AFM and fiscal representative |
| Value basis | Objective value, or market value where it is higher, at the date of death |
| Home-country estate tax | Applies separately unless a treaty allocates the charge to Greece |

## How Greek Inheritance Tax Categories Work by Relationship

Greek inheritance tax is not a flat percentage on the estate. Law 2961/2001 sorts heirs into three
categories by their relationship to the deceased, gives each category a tax-free amount applied to that
heir's own share, and taxes the remainder on a progressive scale. Two heirs receiving identical halves of
the same apartment pay different amounts if they sit in different categories.

| Category | Who is in it | Tax-free per heir | Rate range above it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Spouse, children, grandchildren, parents | €150,000 | 1% to 10% |
| B | Great-grandchildren, grandparents, siblings, step-parents, stepchildren, parents-in-law | €30,000 | 5% to 20% |
| C | Everyone else, including unrelated beneficiaries and distant cousins | €6,000 | 20% to 40% |

The spread between the ends of that table is the whole planning question. Category A starts at 1% after a
€150,000 exemption. Category C starts at 20% after €6,000. On the same asset, the difference between
leaving Greek property to a child and leaving it to a friend or a partner you never married is not a
matter of degree.

Category A is the band most readers need, and its steps are: the first €150,000 of the heir's share is
free, the next €150,000 is taxed at 1%, and larger shares move to 5% and then 10%. Categories B and C run
on their own schedules between the endpoints in the table. Take the intermediate band boundaries for all
three from the AADE schedule current at the date of death rather than from any article, this one included:
the categories and the endpoints are statutory and stable, the internal thresholds are the part that
finance ministry decisions move.

One further relief matters more than any of the bands for the families this site deals with. Where the
beneficiaries are the surviving spouse and the deceased's minor children, up to €400,000 per beneficiary
is exempt. A €400,000 regional Golden Visa deed inherited by a spouse can therefore pass with no Greek
inheritance tax at all, which is a different outcome from the one most foreign owners assume.

**A worked comparison on one asset.** Take a €400,000 Athens apartment passing in full to a single heir.
To a child, €150,000 is exempt and the next €150,000 attracts 1%, so the charge is modest and concentrated
in the top slice. To an unrelated friend, €6,000 is exempt and the remaining €394,000 is taxed from 20%
upward. Same apartment, same value, same paperwork, and a bill that differs by an order of magnitude.
This is why the succession question is settled while the owner is alive and not by the heirs afterwards.

## What Assets and Liabilities Pass Through the Greek Estate?

Greek inheritance tax applies to the net value of each heir's share at death. Mortgages and documented debts reduce the taxable base first; the relationship allowance is applied to what remains.

Inheritance tax focuses on the net value of what each heir receives, not merely the headline property price. The taxable base is the property's value at death less the debts and expenses Greek tax law admits. Which of your debts qualify is a documentation question, and undocumented ones are simply not deducted.

Greek-situs items commonly in scope:

- Freehold apartments, houses, and building plots registered at the Ktimatologio
- Usufruct or bare ownership splits if the deceased held a partial right
- Shares in Greek companies whose primary asset is local real estate, in some structures

Outside direct inheritance tax on immovable property, but still on the heirs' critical path:

- Bank accounts in Greece, which follow separate declaration paths
- ENFIA arrears, which must be cleared or assumed before clean transfer
- Mortgage balances, which reduce net value if properly documented

Heirs also inherit compliance obligations. Within sixty days of acceptance, the E9 property declaration must reflect the new owner, the same filing rule that applies after purchase, as explained in the [Greece AFM and property tax guide](/guides/greece-afm-tax-number-property/). Annual ENFIA continues on the inherited square metres. If the property generated rental income before death, undeclared income can surface during estate administration and delay acceptance.

## How Heirs Obtain an AFM and Complete Acceptance

Heirs accepting Greek property need an AFM before notarial acceptance, with non-residents using fiscal representatives and power-of-attorney routes that add 5 to 14 business days before AADE can link ENFIA and E9 filings to the new owner within 60 days of deed transfer.

Every heir who will appear on the Greek title needs an AFM before AADE and the notary can complete acceptance. Non-residents receive the pink slip naming a fiscal representative, in practice their Greek lawyer, through the same DOY process buyers use. Remote heirs rely on notarised power of attorney legalised for use in Greece, a workflow identical to pre-purchase setup described in the AFM guide.

The acceptance timeline practitioners cite runs roughly as follows, though cases vary:

1. **Death certificate and succession opening:** Lawyer collects civil registry documents, foreign certificates apostilled and translated where required.
2. **Title and encumbrance review:** Cadastre extract confirms the deceased held clear title and identifies mortgages or usufruct.
3. **Tax calculation:** Counsel applies current relationship category allowances and rate bands to each heir's share.
4. **Acceptance declaration before notary:** Heirs accept or renounce shares; renunciation by one heir can shift burden to others.
5. **Tax payment or instalment plan:** AADE issues payment instructions; some estates qualify for staged payment depending on value and asset type, confirm with counsel.
6. **Ktimatologio registration:** Updated ownership recorded; heir receives extract for future sale or Golden Visa application.

Statutory deadlines are unforgiving. Greek law sets a window for declaring inheritance, commonly described as four months from death for heirs present in Greece and extended periods for heirs abroad who prove they learned of the death later. Missing the window can trigger automatic acceptance assumptions and penalty interest. Do not infer your deadline from a blog post; your lawyer counts from the actual death date and your discovery date under current code.

## Will vs Intestacy: Which Path Applies to Foreign Owners?

Greek succession for foreign owners forces a choice among a home-country will, a Greek notarial will for local assets, or Greek intestacy rules that default shares to spouse and children under statutory order, with acceptance declarations due within 4 months of death for heirs present in Greece under current practitioner timelines.

Three documents collide in foreign-owner estates: a home-country will, a Greek notarial will limited to local assets, and Greek intestacy rules if no valid will covers the property.

**Greek notarial will:** Drafted before a Greek notary, stored in the notarial archive, directly enforceable for Greek assets. Preferred when the owner spends significant time in Greece or holds multiple local properties.

**Foreign will:** Valid if it meets form requirements, but translation, apostille, and conflict-of-law analysis add months. A Greek notary will stop where forced-heirship produces a different result from the foreign document.

**Intestacy:** If no valid will governs the Greek asset, Greek statutory order determines heirs: spouse and children in defined shares, then parents, then more remote lines. Intestacy does not avoid tax; it merely defaults the split.

| Planning approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Greek notarial will only for local assets | Fast enforcement, clear with notary | Requires Greek drafting appointment |
| Single home-country will | Convenient while living abroad | Slower probate, conflict risk on immovable |
| Living owners holding joint structure | Can simplify transfer on first death | Joint holding suits spouses; for non-spouses it complicates both tax and the permit |
| Lifetime gift instead of testament | Shifts the timing of the tax event | Gift tax applies; GV minimum hold rules if permit active |

Owners with Golden Visa property should read the [family member rules guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-family-members-rules/) before assuming a spouse or adult child automatically inherits the permit along with the apartment. Migration status and title succession are separate files.

## Golden Visa Property Passing to Heirs: Permit vs Title


These are two separate things and they pass differently. The property is an asset and transfers to heirs under Greek succession law like any other Greek real estate, with inheritance tax assessed on the objective value at Category I to III rates depending on relationship. The residence permit is personal and does not pass at all: heirs who want their own permit must qualify in their own right on the inherited property, which requires it to still meet the tier and 120 m² tests.
When a Golden Visa holder dies, two questions arise: who owns the property, and who holds the residence permit?

**Title** passes by inheritance tax and notarial acceptance. Heirs become owners after compliance steps complete.

**Permit** does not simply rename. The Ministry of Migration ties permit validity to the qualifying investment and the primary applicant. Surviving spouses and dependent children who were already included on the permit may have renewal paths, but adult children who were not dependents do not automatically step into the deceased parent's permit without meeting current investment and documentation rules.

| Heir situation | Typical title outcome | Typical permit outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Spouse co-owner on deed and permit | Shared title continues; permit renewal case-by-case | Counsel files migration update |
| Adult child inherits sole property | Child becomes owner after tax | New GV application if child wants permit |
| Heirs sell immediately | Transfer after acceptance | Permit ends with sale of qualifying asset |
| Property below current tier after partial inheritance | Shared ownership possible | Fails the GV threshold test if an heir applies on it |

Heirs who do not want residency sell. The [guide to selling after Golden Visa](/guides/can-you-sell-property-after-golden-visa-greece/) covers hold periods, bank traceability, and what happens to permit status when the qualifying asset leaves the heir's name. If an heir aims for citizenship rather than permit renewal, inheritance timing interacts with the seven-year naturalisation clock described in the [citizenship after Golden Visa guide](/guides/greece-citizenship-after-golden-visa/), but citizenship is personal to the applicant; inheriting property alone does not shorten residence requirements.

## EU Cross-Border Succession and Non-EU Owners

EU Regulation 650/2012 applies to deaths on or after 17 August 2015 and coordinates cross-border estates for EU nationals electing home-country law, while Greek immovable property still registers through Greek notarial acceptance and inheritance tax categories on local value. Non-EU owners from the UK, US, Australia, and China generally follow Greek situs rules regardless of home wills, and heirs face 4-month acceptance windows domestically with extensions abroad when late discovery is documented. Double probate adds 6 to 12 months when only foreign wills exist without a Greek notarial counterpart. Keep apostilled birth certificates on hand for the parent links: name mismatches are what delay AFM issuance for an accepting heir. Tax bases on €400,000 shares differ materially between Category I children and Category III unrelated beneficiaries under ministry tables updated in 2026. Gift tax applies separately if owners transfer during life, disturbing Golden Visa hold rules when permits stay active on the same deed.

EU Regulation No 650/2012 created a European Certificate of Succession and harmonised choice-of-law rules for deaths on or after 17 August 2015. EU citizens domiciled abroad can elect the law of their nationality to govern their estate, but immovable property in Greece still registers through Greek notarial procedure and remains exposed to Greek inheritance tax on local value.

Non-EU nationals, including UK citizens post-Brexit, US, Australian, Canadian, and Chinese owners, cannot rely on the regulation's elective rules in the same way. Practitioners still use the regulation's concepts when multiple EU heirs inherit together, but the Greek asset path runs through Greek tax categories and acceptance declarations.

The friction points that recur in cross-border estates:

- **Double probate:** Home-country grant of probate plus Greek acceptance, cured by early Greek will or European Certificate where eligible

- **Treaty credit claims:** Home-country accountants need Greek tax payment receipts to claim foreign tax credits

## Succession Planning Checklist for Non-Resident Owners

Succession planning for non-resident Greek owners runs on a document checklist covering Ktimatologio IDs, ENFIA accounts, Greek notarial wills, and heir AFM strategy at least 6 months before expected need, because apostille chains on foreign certificates take 8 to 10 weeks.

Use this checklist while the owner is alive, not after death when options narrow.

**Document layer**

- [ ] Inventory every Greek asset with Ktimatologio ID, ENFIA account, and bank loan reference
- [ ] Confirm whether property is sole name, joint, or company-held
- [ ] Store building permit and engineer certificates with the will packet
- [ ] Align home-country will with Greek notarial will if both exist

**Tax and representative layer**

- [ ] Ensure heirs know which lawyer holds fiscal representative status
- [ ] Pre-identify heir AFM strategy: in-person vs PoA
- [ ] Model inheritance tax under each relationship category with counsel, using current allowances
- [ ] Review gift vs inheritance timing if owner wants to transfer during life

**Golden Visa layer**

- [ ] List dependents already on permit file
- [ ] Confirm the property still meets the 120 square metre and single-asset rules, in case an heir applies
- [ ] Note Circular 1/2026 bank trail if heirs must requalify

**Operational layer**

- [ ] Maintain E9 and ENFIA current; arrears block clean acceptance
- [ ] Keep a sealed instruction letter with lawyer contact and insurance details
- [ ] Review life insurance liquidity to pay tax without forced sale

## Red flags to check

Greek estate red flags include foreign-only wills without Greek notarial counterparts, undeclared rental income on inherited files, and Golden Visa short-term rental history on the qualifying deed, each adding 6 to 12 months to acceptance timelines on €400,000 coastal assets.

Experienced Greek estate lawyers flag the same defects repeatedly in foreign-owner files.

6. **Assumed low tax without calculation:** Category III treatment on a misclassified heir relationship produces surprise bills.

Treat any informal promise that "Greece charges almost nothing on inheritance" as a red flag unless a licensed adviser ran your relationship category and year of death through official tables.

Re-run the Category I against the Category III calculation on your actual share before the four-month acceptance window closes. A misclassified relationship is the difference between a modest bill and a five-figure one on a mid-value Athens apartment.

## Buyer scenarios and decision framework
Buyer scenarios for inherited Greek property split between Golden Visa families preserving spouse permits on €400,000 deeds, non-resident lifestyle owners minimising heir friction, and EU dual-estate cases electing applicable law under Regulation 650/2012 while still paying Greek tax on local value.

Different owner profiles should plan on different timelines and documents.

**Scenario A: Golden Visa investor, spouse and children abroad**

Primary goal: preserve permit for spouse, pass title cleanly to children if desired. Action: confirm dependents on permit file, draft Greek will respecting forced shares, model tax on spousal transfer vs child transfer, keep property compliant with GV rental ban. Read [family member rules](/guides/greece-golden-visa-family-members-rules/) before adding adult children to the deed for convenience.

**Scenario B: Non-resident lifestyle buyer, no permit**

Primary goal: minimise heir friction and tax uncertainty. Action: Greek notarial will, maintain AFM and E9, instruct heirs on acceptance deadline, consider life insurance for tax liquidity on a €300,000 to €800,000 coastal asset.

**Scenario C: EU citizen with estate in two countries**

Primary goal: elect applicable law where regulation allows while accepting Greek tax on immovable value. Action: EU succession specialist plus Greek notary, European Certificate of Succession if eligible, treaty credit planning with home accountant.

**Scenario D: Owner considering lifetime gift to children**

Primary goal: move asset during life. Action: compare gift tax bands to projected inheritance tax, verify Golden Visa minimum hold if permit active, update E9 after gift registration.

| If your priority is... | Lean toward... | Watch out for... |
|---|---|---|
| Fast heir transfer | Greek notarial will, clean cadastre | Missing permits |
| Lowest tax | Early counsel modelling, not internet rates | Category misclassification |
| Keeping GV in family | Dependents on permit, compliant asset | Adult child permit reapplication |
| Liquidity for heirs | Insurance or cash reserve | Forced sale in weak market |
| Simplicity | Single heir acceptance | Renouncing heirs shifting burden |

## Pros and Cons of Common Succession Structures

Proactive Greek succession planning costs €2,000 to €6,000 in coordinated will and tax memo fees, against 6-month probate delays and surprise Category III tax on €400,000 shares that can exceed €20,000 for misclassified heirs.

**Pros of proactive Greek planning**

- Heirs avoid panic searches for documents and lawyers during grief
- Relationship category modelling surfaces tax before acceptance deadline
- Golden Visa and title paths stay aligned with migration counsel
- Clean E9 history prevents AADE blocks

**Cons and limits**

- Greek and home-country advice together adds €2,000 to €6,000 upfront for coordinated wills and tax memos
- Forced heirship defeats a pure "leave it all to one child" instruction in a foreign will
- Lifetime gifts trigger separate gift tax and can break Golden Visa hold requirements
- Rate and allowance changes can outdated a plan drafted five years earlier; review after major law reforms such as Law 5100/2024 on investment tiers

No structure eliminates Greek inheritance tax on immovable property; planning only makes the amount, timing, and process predictable within legal bounds.
