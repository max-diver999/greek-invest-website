**Quick answer:** Both zones sit in the same €800,000 Attica tier, and the comparison most articles
draw between them is the wrong one. The centre's yield reputation rests on Kipseli and the transitional
districts at €2,000 to 2,800 per square metre. Spending €800,000 on a single title at those prices means
buying around 300 square metres, and that stock is not what Athenian apartment blocks contain. At the
qualifying threshold the centre you can actually buy is Kolonaki and its neighbours, where gross yields
run 3.5% to 5%, below the Riviera, not above it.

So the real choice at €800,000 is between prime centre and coast, and on the numbers the coast wins on
income as well as on lifestyle. The Kipseli yield case belongs to buyers at the €400,000 regional tier or
to anyone buying outside the Golden Visa entirely.

Athens investors rarely choose "Athens", they choose a submarket. The €800,000 threshold applies equally to a Kipseli apartment and a Voula seafront flat, but the return profile, regulatory overlay, and buyer pool differ sharply. This comparison maps Glyfada and Voula against Kipseli and the wider center for investors who have committed to Attica but not to a district.

## Same Golden Visa Tier: Different Economics

- €800,000 minimum in one residential title
- 120 square metres minimum usable area (engineer certificate)
- No short-term tourist rentals on the qualifying asset

Tier parity is where most comparisons stop, and it is where the interesting constraint starts. The
threshold has to be met on one title. Divide €800,000 by each district's price per square metre and the
answer tells you what you would have to find.

| Budget arithmetic | Kipseli (~€2,400/m²) | Kolonaki (~€5,200/m²) | Glyfada (~€4,000/m²) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| €800,000 on one title buys | ~333 m² | ~154 m² | ~200 m² |
| Typical apartment in that stock | 80 to 120 m² | 100 to 180 m² | 100 to 150 m² |
| Does the arithmetic meet the stock | No: you would need three flats | Yes | Yes |
| GV 120 m² rule | Achievable, but not on one deed at this budget | Achievable | Achievable |

The Kipseli column is the finding. A 333 square metre single residential title in a 1960s Kipseli
apartment block is not a rare listing; it is close to a non-existent one, and buying three flats does not
help because the threshold and the 120 square metre certificate both attach to a single deed. Merging
adjacent units is legally possible and slow, and it is a construction project rather than a purchase.

The practical consequence: a Golden Visa buyer at €800,000 who wants the centre is buying Kolonaki,
Mets or Koukaki at prime prices. They are not buying the Kipseli yield. Any comparison that quotes
Kipseli's 6 to 7.5% next to an €800,000 budget is quoting a return the reader cannot execute.

## Yield, once the tier is applied

Read the yield table without a budget attached and the centre wins comfortably. Read the same table with
€800,000 and a single-title requirement attached and the ranking inverts, because the rows that produce
the high numbers are rows you cannot reach.

| Zone | Gross LTR yield (planning band) | Reachable on one €800,000 title? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kipseli / transitional centre | 6 to 7.5% | No: the budget implies ~300 m² of stock that is not there |
| Athens city-wide average | 5.43% | Not a purchasable thing; a composite across all price points |
| Glyfada / Voula Riviera | 4 to 5.5% | Yes |
| Kolonaki / prime centre | 3.5 to 5% | Yes |

That leaves two reachable rows, and the coastal one is the higher of the pair. It is an uncomfortable
result for the received wisdom about Athens, and it follows from a single structural fact: the yield
districts are cheap per square metre, and a large fixed threshold on one deed converts cheapness into an
impossible floor area.

Two honest caveats. The bands are planning ranges, not quotes on a specific flat, and a particular
Kolonaki apartment with a strong corporate tenant can beat a mediocre Glyfada one. And the Kipseli case
becomes real the moment the constraint relaxes: at the €400,000 regional tier outside Attica, or on a
second non-qualifying purchase, or for a cash buyer with no residency objective at all.

Net yields after ENFIA, management, maintenance, and Greek rental income tax typically fall 1 to 1.5 points below gross. Model via [Greece rental yield guide](/guides/greece-rental-yield-guide/) and [buy-to-let Greece guide](/guides/buy-to-let-greece-guide/).

## STR Moratorium: Center Constraint Riviera Avoids


Two separate restrictions stack in central Athens and only one reaches the Riviera. The national Golden Visa ban applies to any qualifying property anywhere in Greece, permanently, for the life of the permit. The Athens moratorium on new short-term rental registrations runs through the end of 2026 and applies to central districts regardless of Golden Visa status. So a central non-qualifying apartment may be unlicensable, while an equivalent Riviera one is not.
Two STR rules stack in central Athens, only one applies on the Riviera for non-GV assets.

### Layer 1: Golden Visa STR ban (national)

Law 5100/2024 prohibits short-term tourist rentals on the qualifying Golden Visa property everywhere in Greece. Applies to Kipseli and Glyfada equally.

### Layer 2: Athens municipality moratorium (local)

The Athens STR moratorium freezes new ΑΜΑ licences in saturated central districts through end-2026. Zones include Kolonaki, Exarchia, Monastiraki, Koukaki, Petralona, and parts of the historic centre where STR density exceeded the 40% statutory threshold.

| Zone | New STR licence (non-GV) | GV asset STR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Center moratorium districts | Frozen through end-2026 | Prohibited always |
| Kipseli (typically) | Check current saturation map | Prohibited on GV asset |
| Glyfada / Voula | Standard ΑΜΑ rules | Prohibited on GV asset |

Practical impact: A yield investor buying center stock for LTR is unaffected by the moratorium, long-term leases remain fully legal. An investor hoping to STR a non-GV center apartment faces licence freeze; a Riviera buyer in Glyfada can still obtain new licences on non-GV stock, but cannot STR the GV qualifying unit anyway.

Pre-moratorium licensed center properties trade at a premium, grandfathered ΑΜΑ numbers transfer with the property. That premium matters for non-GV investors, not for standard Golden Visa structuring.

## Verdict

For a Golden Visa buyer at €800,000 on a single title, the Riviera is the stronger choice on income and
on lifestyle at once, which is not the trade-off this comparison is usually said to present. Glyfada and
Voula stock reaches the threshold at a realistic floor area, yields 4 to 5.5% gross against Kolonaki's
3.5 to 5%, and carries the Ellinikon appreciation case on top.

The centre earns the decision on different grounds: a buyer who wants to live in Athens rather than beside
it, who values a year-round tenant pool over a seasonal one, or who is buying prestige stock whose resale
market is domestic and deep. Those are good reasons. Higher yield, at this threshold, is not among them.

Both zones share the €800,000 threshold, the 120 m² rule, and the GV STR ban. The moratorium adds a center-only constraint on new STR licences through end-2026, relevant for non-GV plays, not for standard GV LTR underwriting. ## Financial Comparison of Athens Riviera vs Athens Center


Run at net rather than gross, both options land well under the yields quoted for cheaper Athens stock, and for the same reason: Greek rental income is taxed progressively at 15%, 25%, 35% and 45% on gross rent, with only a flat 5% repairs deduction to set against it, so an €800,000 asset pays the upper rates on most of its income. Modern Riviera stock with parking and a good energy rating nets around 2.4%. A renovated 1970s Kolonaki flat nets closer to 1.7%, because prime capital value does not translate into proportionally higher rent and the maintenance reserve on older stock is real.

| Financial Metric | Option A: Athens Center (Kolonaki) | Option B: Athens Riviera (Glyfada) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase price | €800,000 | €800,000 |
| Floor area | 110 m² | 135 m² |
| Price per m² | ~€5,409 Kolonaki | ~€4,250 to €5,000 Glyfada |
| Stock | 1970s block, renovated | 2021 build, parking and balconies |
| Monthly long-term rent | €2,900 | €3,500 |
| Gross annual rent | €34,800 | €42,000 |
| Gross yield | 4.35% | 5.25% |
| Greek rental income tax (15/25/35/45%, after the 5% repairs deduction) | €7,971 | €10,855 |
| Management (8%) | €2,780 | €3,360 |
| Vacancy allowance | €1,740 (5%) | €2,520 (6%) |
| Maintenance reserve | €4,000 (0.5%, older stock) | €1,600 (0.2%, new build) |
| ENFIA | €2,300 | €2,100 |
| Buildings insurance | €1,400 | €1,500 |
| Accountancy and non-resident filing | €900 | €900 |
| Net annual income | €13,709 | €19,165 |
| **Net yield on purchase price** | **1.71%** | **2.40%** |
| 120 m² Golden Visa rule on a single title | Fails at 110 m² | Passes at 135 m² |

Option B (Athens Riviera) delivers roughly 40% more net income than Option A on identical capital, driven by higher rent per euro invested, a much lower maintenance reserve on new-build stock, and demand from tenants who will pay for parking and energy performance. It also clears the 120 m² Golden Visa minimum on a single title with room to spare. 

Option A (Athens Center) commands a premium location in Kolonaki but carries higher maintenance costs due to the building's age, and the 110m² size fails the 120m² Golden Visa requirement unless the buyer is exempt or utilizes a non-residential conversion route. Center properties, however, benefit from year-round domestic and corporate demand, making them highly defensive assets during economic downturns.

## Sub-Market Selection Checklist for Buyers

1. **Tenant Profile and Lease Terms:** Center properties attract diplomats, corporate executives, and academics who prefer 1-2 year leases. Riviera properties attract affluent families and expats who demand multi-year leases and premium amenities (parking, security, pools).
2. **Golden Visa Compliance:** Both locations sit in the €800,000 Attica zone. However, finding modern apartments over 120m² is significantly easier on the Riviera, whereas Center apartments are often smaller and require legal mergers of adjacent units to qualify.
3. **Capital Appreciation Potential:** The Athens Riviera is undergoing massive transformation driven by the Ellinikon project, leading to higher projected capital growth. The Center offers stable, mature values with lower volatility but compressed yields.
