**Quick answer:** there is no best Greek island, and the useful question is which of
four variables you are optimising for. Price runs from Kefalonia at a little under
€2,400 per square metre to Mykonos at about €7,623. Airlift, meaning direct
international flights, is what most of that gap pays for. Season length and year-round
viability vary independently of both. And the Golden Visa tier splits between €400,000
and €800,000 in a way that does not follow price. This page ranks the islands on each
axis separately.

All figures are asking prices from Indomio listings rather than recorded transactions;
the [market overview](/property-for-sale/) explains why that distinction matters.
[Skiathos](/property-for-sale/skiathos/) is absent because no published index covers
it, which that page addresses directly.

The spread from top to bottom is roughly six times, and it is worth noticing that the
range inside single islands is often larger than the range between them. Kefalonia
alone runs from €1,334 to a €2,400 average; Lefkada from €1,970 to €4,910. Choosing an
island is a coarser decision than choosing where on it.

## The airlift ladder, which explains most of the price ladder

| Island | International access | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Mykonos, Santorini | Direct long-haul and European through the season | Global buyer pool, top prices |
| Rhodes | International year-round plus heavy summer charter | Wide pool, year-round viability |
| Skiathos | Direct summer charter despite its size | Punches above its scale |
| Corfu, Zakynthos, Kefalonia | Seasonal European charter | European pool, seasonal |
| Lefkada | No airport; road bridge, Preveza nearby | Drive-in access, no flight dependency |
| Skopelos, Alonissos, small Cyclades | Ferry only | Thin pool, deepest discount |

This is the single best explanation of the price ladder above. What separates Mykonos
from Naxos is not landscape, it is that one has direct international flights and a
global brand and the other has a domestic airport and a ferry.

For a buyer the practical reading is that the discount for inconvenient access is
real, and so is the inconvenience. It shows up when you visit, when you let, and most
of all when you sell, because a thin buyer pool is comfortable while you are buying
and is the whole problem when you are not.

## Season length and winter viability

These are different questions and they get conflated constantly.

| Island | Season length | Functions in winter |
|---|---|---|
| Rhodes | Long, April to October | Yes: city, hospital, year-round airport |
| Lefkada | Long, road access extends it | Partly: mainland services a drive away |
| Corfu, Zakynthos, Kefalonia | Moderate, May to October | Towns yes, resort areas no |
| Skiathos | Short and intense | Minimally |
| Mykonos, Santorini | Short, May to early October | Towns partly, resorts no |
| Small Cyclades, Skopelos | Short | Little |

Season length decides letting income. Winter viability decides whether the place is
usable, sellable and habitable outside the season, and whether works can happen. A
buyer who wants income cares about the first column; a buyer who wants a life cares
about the second; and the two rank the islands differently.

Rhodes and Lefkada score well on both for opposite reasons: one is large enough to
sustain itself, the other is attached to the mainland.

## The Golden Visa tier map

| Tier | Islands |
|---|---|
| €800,000 | Mykonos, Santorini, Rhodes, and other islands above the population threshold |
| €400,000 | Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, Skiathos, Skopelos, Alonissos, Paxos, Ithaca, smaller Cyclades and Dodecanese |

The tier does not follow price, which produces two situations worth knowing about.

Rhodes at about €2,421 per square metre sits in the €800,000 tier, so a qualifying
purchase there is roughly 330 square metres, at the large end of the island's market.
An hour away by ferry, Symi and Karpathos need half that.

Lefkada is in the €400,000 tier, but at Apollonio prices near €4,910 that sum buys
about 81 square metres against a 120 square metre area requirement, so on the
expensive coast the area rule binds before the price does.

Both are cases where reading only the tier, or only the price, gives the wrong answer.
The [tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones,
and the tier follows the cadastral parcel rather than the island's name in every case.

One restriction applies across all of them: a qualifying property may only be let on
leases of twelve months or longer, and short-term letting is prohibited for the life
of the permit. The
[letting restriction guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/) covers
what renewal checks. It costs most on the islands with the shortest seasons, where the
peak rate is the whole income case.

## The mistake that costs the most

Across every island page on this site the same error recurs, and it is worth naming
here because a hub is where people are still deciding.

Buyers optimise for the wrong variable, and specifically they optimise for the one
that matters during the two weeks they are viewing rather than the one that matters
for the ten years they own.

In August every island scores well on the variables that dominate a viewing: weather,
beaches, atmosphere, how the village looks at seven in the evening. Almost none of
those varies enough between islands to justify a threefold price difference. The
variables that do vary enormously, and that decide whether the purchase works, are
invisible in August: whether trades are available in January, how long a resale takes,
whether there is a doctor, what the ferry does in November, and how many other people
would want this house.

The practical version is that the decision has two halves and people only make one.
Which island is the half that gets all the attention. Which house on which island,
and specifically how it scores on access, condition and buyer pool, is the half that
determines the outcome, and the price ladder above shows why: the range inside single
islands is often wider than the range between them.

A buyer who spends their research time on the second half will do better on any island
than a buyer who spends it on the first half and picks the famous one.

## Cost, tax and what applies everywhere

Three things apply identically wherever you buy in Greece, and they are worth having in
one place rather than rediscovering per island.

Acquisition costs run 7 to 10% above the purchase price. On a resale the largest item
is the transfer tax at an effective 3.09% of the taxable value, which is the higher of
the state's objective value and the declared price. A first sale by a developer falls
under VAT at 24% instead. Greek VAT at 24% also applies on top of each professional
fee, so a quoted lawyer's fee is not what leaves your account.

Rental income for an individual runs on a scale reaching 45% from 2026, with only a
flat 5% deduction for repairs allowed against gross rent and nothing for management,
insurance or finance. That is the number that turns attractive gross yields into
ordinary net ones.

And ENFIA, the annual property tax, is assessed on objective value and arrives whether
or not the property is used or let.

## How to actually choose

Four questions, answered honestly, narrow this faster than any amount of browsing.

**How many weeks a year will you be there?** Under four, and access dominates
everything: buy where you can arrive without a connection. Over twelve, and winter
viability dominates instead, which points somewhere with a hospital and open shops.

**Is income part of the case, or a bonus?** If it is part of the case, you need a
season and an airport, and you cannot combine it with a Golden Visa qualifying
property. If it is a bonus, the cheap interiors become available and the calculation
changes completely.

**What will you do about February?** Every island page on this site ends with the same
instruction and it is the most consistently ignored advice in the category. Visit
outside August before committing. The version of an island people fall for and the
version they own are different places.

**Who will buy it from you?** The thinner the buyer pool, the longer the exit. That is
the cost of the discount at the bottom of the price ladder, and it is a real cost
rather than a theoretical one.

## Where each island lands

**[Rhodes](/property-for-sale/rhodes/)** for year-round living with services and
flights, at mid-range prices in the €800,000 tier.

**[Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/)** for the most space per euro among
islands with a functioning market, in the €400,000 tier and with the weakest
competition in search.

**[Zakynthos](/property-for-sale/zakynthos/)** for established letting demand and a
deeper resale pool than Kefalonia, at a higher entry price.

**[Lefkada](/property-for-sale/lefkada/)** for road access, which removes the ferry
premium on materials, trades and visitors and widens the buyer pool.

**[The Cyclades](/property-for-sale/cyclades/)** for recognition and the highest
prices, with the shortest seasons and the strongest peak rates.

**[Skiathos](/property-for-sale/skiathos/)** for direct summer flights to a small
island, with the honest caveat that nobody publishes a price index for it.

And if the answer to all four questions turns out to be practical rather than
romantic, the mainland deserves a look: the
[Peloponnese](/property-for-sale/peloponnese/) has more coastline than any island
here, at about €1,778 per square metre and reachable by motorway.

## Risks that apply to every island

**Building legality.** Enclosed terraces and additions built without permits are the
most common reason an island sale cannot complete. The
[engineer's certificate](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) is what
catches them.

**Undivided inheritances.** Island properties frequently sit in family ownership
across several heirs, all of whom sign, and the heir hardest to reach sets the
completion date rather than the seller or the buyer.

**Access and services.** Adopted road, mains water, drainage. Cheap plots are often
cheap for these reasons.

**Forestry and coastal designation.** Wooded and shoreline plots carry restrictions
that are invisible on the ground and checkable in advance.

**Winter.** Stated four times on this page because it is the thing buyers most
reliably discover afterwards. The pattern is consistent enough to be worth trusting:
people who visited in February before buying rarely regret the island they chose, and
people who did not are the ones who list it three years later having used it twice.
