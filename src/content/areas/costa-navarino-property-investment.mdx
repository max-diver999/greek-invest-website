**Quick answer:** Costa Navarino sits in Messinia and therefore in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier, but the
tier is not the interesting number. Branded resort stock generally starts well above it, so a buyer here
is not solving for the threshold. What they are solving for is a contract structure: the estate was
developed and is controlled by a single company, and that company is the counterparty on the deed, the
service charge, the amenity access, the rental arrangement and usually the resale consent.

Every other page in this collection describes a market. This one describes an owner.

---

## One counterparty, five relationships

In an ordinary Greek purchase the seller disappears at the deed. Your neighbours become an owners'
assembly, your service charge is set by that assembly, and your resale is a matter between you and a
buyer. On a master-planned resort estate none of that holds.

| Relationship | In an ordinary purchase | On a resort estate |
|---|---|---|
| Seller | Gone after completion | Still present, as operator and developer |
| Service charge | Set by the owners' assembly | Set under the estate's own regime |
| Amenity access | Not applicable | Contractual, and priced |
| Letting | Your choice of agent | Frequently a resort rental programme |
| Resale | Between you and a buyer | Often subject to consent or a right of first refusal |

None of those is improper and all of them are normal in branded residential globally. What matters is
that they are all with the same entity, so a buyer's negotiating position after completion is weaker than
it looks at completion. Read the estate regime, the amenity agreement and the rental programme documents
before the property particulars, not after.

## What the service charge is buying, and why it does not stop

Resort estates carry running costs that ordinary Greek property does not: landscaping across a large
estate, private roads, security, water systems and the shared infrastructure that makes the place work.
Those charges are the product rather than an overhead — the maintained environment is what a buyer is
paying for — but they are also fixed, indexed and payable whether or not the owner is in Greece.

Two questions settle most of it. What is the current annual charge for the specific unit, in writing, with
the last three years' history. And what is the mechanism by which it can rise, and who decides.

An owner who uses the property heavily gets value from a high charge. An owner who visits twice a year and
lets the rest is paying resort economics for a holiday flat, and the arithmetic on that rarely works.

## The rental programme and the Golden Visa do not fit together

Short-term tourist letting of a qualifying property is prohibited nationwide for the life of the permit.
A resort rental programme lets by the night. Those two statements cannot both apply to the same deed.

A buyer intending to use a Costa Navarino property as a Golden Visa asset therefore needs the qualifying
unit outside any rental pool and let on a twelve-month residential lease — in a location whose tenant
base is a resort rather than a town. That is a genuinely difficult letting proposition, and it is the
reason most Golden Visa files here place the qualifying asset somewhere else in Messinia and treat the
resort property as the lifestyle purchase.

[Kalamata](/areas/kalamata-property-investment/), forty minutes away, is where that qualifying asset
usually goes, because it has the year-round tenant a resort does not. The
[short-term rental ban](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/) explains why the two cannot be
combined in one property.

## Price here is a list, not a market

An estate under single ownership publishes prices; it does not discover them. There are no independent
comparables in the ordinary sense, because every transaction on the estate has had the same seller, and
external Messinian comparables describe a different product.

That has a specific consequence at resale. The buyer pool for a branded resort unit is people who want
that estate, which is smaller than the pool for a Messinian house and far smaller than the pool for a
Kalamata apartment. Liquidity follows the brand, and the brand is controlled by the counterparty.

## What to establish before committing

1. **The estate regime and any right of first refusal or consent requirement on resale.**
2. **The current service charge for the unit, its three-year history, and the escalation mechanism.**
3. **Whether amenity access is included, contractual or purchasable, and whether it transfers to a buyer.**
4. **Whether the unit is inside a rental programme and what exiting it costs.**
5. **The property class on the permit** — residential or tourist use — because a tourist-use encumbrance
   is incompatible with a Golden Visa file.
6. **Title and boundaries in the ordinary way**, since estate branding does not remove Greek conveyancing;
   the [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) covers that part.

## Who this suits

Somebody who wants a house on that estate, will use it enough to justify the charge, and is buying the
maintained environment rather than a yield or a permit. On those terms Costa Navarino is coherent and the
contract structure is the price of what makes it pleasant.

It suits badly a Golden Visa buyer looking for the cheapest compliant route, who is better served by the
[Peloponnese generally](/guides/peloponnese-property-investment-guide/), and badly an investor, because a
single-counterparty asset with consent-gated resale is the opposite of a liquid one.

## A decision framework before you engage with the estate

| Question | If the answer is | Then |
|---|---|---|
| How many weeks a year will you use it? | Fewer than 6 | The service charge economics do not work; buy in Kalamata instead |
| Is this your Golden Visa asset? | Yes | It must sit outside the rental programme and let on a 12-month lease |
| Do you need the amenities? | No | You are paying resort economics for a holiday house |
| What is your exit horizon? | Under 5 years | Consent-gated resale into a narrow buyer pool is the wrong structure |

### Risks and red flags on a single-counterparty estate

- **Consent or first-refusal on resale**, which narrows your buyer pool to people the estate accepts.
- **Service-charge escalation** with no owners' assembly to vote it down; ask for 3 years of history.
- **Rental-programme lock-in**, and the cost of exiting it, which is often the yield the purchase was
  underwritten on.
- **Tourist-use classification** on the permit, which is incompatible with a Golden Visa file.
- **Thin comparables**: every prior transaction had the same seller, so there is no independent price
  discovery, and external Messinian comparables at €1,500 to €2,500 per square metre describe a different
  product entirely.

## What the purchase costs beyond the price

Resort branding does not change Greek conveyancing, and the acquisition stack on a Messinian deed is the
same one that applies anywhere else in the country. On a €400,000 purchase it runs to roughly €430,000
all in; on the €800,000 that branded stock more often reaches, closer to €860,000.

| Line | On a €400,000 deed | On an €800,000 deed |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer tax at 3.09% | ~€12,360 | ~€24,720 |
| Notary | €4,000 to €6,000 | €8,000 to €12,000 |
| Lawyer | €4,000 to €6,000 | €6,000 to €10,000 |
| Land registry | €1,900 to €2,600 | €3,800 to €5,200 |
| Engineer and certification | €800 to €1,500 | €800 to €1,500 |
| Golden Visa state fees | €2,000 per adult, €1,000 per dependent under 21 | Same |

Where a unit is a first delivery from a developer, 24% VAT can apply instead of the 3.09% transfer tax,
which on an €800,000 deed is a difference well above €150,000. That determination is made per unit and
belongs to your own lawyer rather than to the seller's brochure.

Messinia sits in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier and the 120 m² certified-area rule applies here exactly as it does in Attica, so a resort unit under that area does not qualify at any price. Long-let gross yields across Messinia run 3.5% to 4.5%, which is the number a rental-programme projection should be read against rather than a nightly rate.

On top of the acquisition stack sits the estate's own annual charge, which is the line that distinguishes
this purchase from a Messinian house and the one most often left out of a buyer's model. Ask for it in
euros per year for the specific unit, with three years of history, before anything else.
