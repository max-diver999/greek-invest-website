**Quick answer:** retiring to Greece from outside the EU means a residence permit,
because visa-free presence is capped at 90 days in any rolling 180-day period across
the Schengen area. The usual route is the Financially Independent Person permit,
which asks for roughly €3,500 a month in passive income or about €126,000 on deposit,
plus health insurance that meets the permit's specification. Employment income does
not qualify. The tax decision, meaning whether to elect the flat 7% pensioner regime,
belongs before the move rather than after it, and it usually moves more money than
the cost of living does.

Most guides to retiring in Greece are written by someone selling a visa service and
open with the climate. The two things that decide whether this works are less
romantic: whether your income is of the right kind, and what happens to it once
Greece can tax it.

## The permit: what the FIP route actually asks

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| Passive income | About €3,500 a month for the main applicant |
| Spouse | Roughly 20% on top |
| Each dependent child | Roughly 15% on top |
| Alternative to income | A deposit around €126,000, covering the three-year validity |
| Health insurance | A policy meeting the permit's cover specification, not simply any policy |
| Permit term | Three years, renewable |

Those figures are published consistently across independent immigration advisories.
They are not read here from a Ministry or consular primary document, and consular
requirements vary by post and change, so treat them as the planning number and
confirm with the consulate handling your file before relying on them.

## The condition that disqualifies people

Employment income does not count.

The Financially Independent Person route is built on passive income: pensions,
investment returns, dividends and rental income, each of which has to be stable,
traceable and documented with bank statements. A salary from an employer does not
qualify however large it is.

This catches two groups. People who are semi-retired and still drawing part-time
employment income, and people who assume any sufficient income will do. Someone still
working remotely for a foreign employer is not a retiree for these purposes and
belongs on the digital nomad route, which is a separate permit with its own
conditions.

The practical consequence is that the composition of your income matters more than
its size. A person on €3,000 a month of pension and dividends is closer to qualifying
than a person on €6,000 of salary.

## The tax decision that comes first

Once presence passes 183 days in a calendar year you are generally Greek tax resident
and worldwide income comes into scope. A foreign pensioner can elect a flat 7% rate
under Article 5B of Law 4172/2013 instead of the ordinary progressive scale, and the
election is what makes the arithmetic work for most retirees.

The point for this page is narrower than the regime itself: **the election is not
automatic and not retrospective.** It is made by a person transferring tax residence,
under conditions attached to prior non-residence and to the country paying the
pension. Arriving, becoming tax resident, and asking about the regime afterwards
removes the choice. The
[7% regime guide](/guides/greece-7-percent-pension-tax-retirees/) sets out the
eligibility test, the
[non-dom guide](/guides/greece-non-dom-tax-regime-5a-guide/) covers the alternative
for very high foreign incomes, and
[sequencing the move](/living-in-greece/moving/) covers how arrival month interacts
with the residence clocks.

## What the file actually contains

The permit is granted on documents rather than on judgement, which means the work is
assembling them in the right order.

| Document | What it proves | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Passport and photographs | Identity | You |
| Proof of passive income | The €3,500 monthly threshold, or the deposit alternative | Bank statements, pension statements, dividend records, covering a run of months rather than one |
| Health insurance policy | Cover meeting the permit specification | An insurer, after you have read the specification |
| Criminal record certificate | No disqualifying record | Your home jurisdiction, apostilled |
| Proof of accommodation in Greece | Somewhere to live | A tenancy agreement or a deed |
| Greek tax number (AFM) | Identity in the Greek system | The tax office, obtainable by a representative |

Two of those rows do most of the delaying.

**Proof of income is a run, not a snapshot.** A single statement showing a healthy
balance proves nothing about stability. What satisfies the requirement is a
documented pattern across months, from sources that are visibly passive.

**Apostilles take longer than people plan for.** A criminal record certificate has to
be issued, apostilled under the Hague Convention and often translated by a certified
translator. Three to four weeks is normal when nothing goes wrong, and a single wrong
detail restarts it.

As of 2026 the application is made at a Greek consulate before travelling rather than
after arrival, which changes the sequence materially: the file has to be complete
while you are still in your home country, not assembled once you are in Greece.

## Renewal, and what it checks

The permit runs three years and renews. Renewal re-examines the same two things the
original grant turned on: that the qualifying income or deposit still exists, and that
health cover is still in force.

What renewal does not require is a minimum stay, in the sense of a counted number of
days. What it does require is that the permit still describes your situation. Someone
whose passive income has stopped, or whose insurance lapsed, is not in the position
the permit was granted for.

Time held as a lawful resident counts toward long-term status and eventually toward
naturalisation, but the counting rules there measure physical residence rather than
permits held. The
[citizenship guide](/guides/greece-citizenship-after-golden-visa/) sets out how that
distinction works for the investment route, and the same principle applies here.

## FIP against the Golden Visa

These are frequently confused and they solve different problems.

| | Financially Independent Person | Golden Visa |
|---|---|---|
| Property purchase | Not required | Required, from €400,000 outside the prime zone |
| Basis | Demonstrable passive income | The qualifying investment |
| Minimum stay | Intended for people living there | None, at all |
| Term | Three years, renewable | Five years, renewable while the asset is held |
| Best for | Actually retiring to Greece | Holding the option without moving |

Buying a €400,000 property to obtain a permit you could have had on income alone is
the most expensive way to retire to Greece. The Golden Visa earns its cost when the
point is optionality rather than relocation, or when you were buying property anyway.

If a purchase is part of the plan for its own sake, the
[property investment guide](/guides/greece-property-investment-guide/) covers the
market side and the [cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/)
covers what sits above any purchase price.

## Where retirees actually settle, and why

Scenery is not the variable. Healthcare access and winter service levels are, and
they diverge far more than prices do.

**Crete** absorbs more long-term foreign residents than any other Greek island,
because it carries two cities with hospitals and regional government and it runs
through the winter rather than closing.

**The Athens Riviera** suits people who want coastal living with a functioning city
and full medical care behind it, at the highest prices of the three.

**The Ionian islands**, Corfu and Kefalonia in particular, are cheaper and greener,
with the sharpest seasonal contrast. Asking prices on Kefalonia run under €2,400 per
square metre against roughly €2,932 to €4,698 on Corfu, and
[the Kefalonia market](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/) sets out the internal spread.

The one recommendation that holds regardless: visit in February. The version of a
Greek island that people retire to is the August one, and the version they live in is
not.

## What a refusal looks like

Applications on this route are rarely refused on judgement. They are refused, or more
often stalled indefinitely, on the file. Four patterns account for most of it.

**Income that is not visibly passive.** A transfer arriving monthly from a company you
own is income, but a consulate reading the statements has to be able to see that it is
passive rather than remuneration. Dividends declared and documented as dividends read
differently from a standing order of the same amount. The distinction is evidential
rather than semantic, and it is worth structuring before the statements are printed.

**Income that is sufficient but not stable.** A portfolio drawdown that varies between
€2,800 and €4,400 across the months shown averages above the threshold and still
reads as unreliable. Consistency across the run does more work than the average.

**Insurance that covers the wrong things.** Policies are commonly rejected not for
their limits but for exclusions, territorial scope or the absence of a specific class
of cover. Read the permit specification first and buy against it, rather than
presenting an existing international policy and hoping.

**Documents that expired inside the process.** Criminal record certificates and some
apostilles carry validity periods measured in months. A file assembled slowly can
reach the consulate with its earliest document already stale, which restarts that
element rather than the whole application, but adds weeks each time it happens.

**A fifth, quieter one: accommodation that does not match the application.** Proof of
somewhere to live is a document like any other, and a short holiday booking or an
address used only for correspondence reads differently from a tenancy. A twelve-month
lease signed before the file goes in is the cleanest version, and it also forces the
useful discipline of choosing a place before committing to the country.

The common thread is that none of these is a question of merit. Someone comfortably
able to support themselves in Greece can be refused on presentation, and the fix is
sequencing rather than argument. Assemble the slowest items first, the shortest-lived
items last, and read every specification before buying against it.

## If circumstances change after the grant

Two situations come up often enough to plan for.

**Income falls below the threshold mid-term.** The permit runs three years and is not
re-tested continuously, but renewal will look at the position then. Someone whose
qualifying income has fallen is renewing into a different set of facts than the one
they were granted on, and the deposit alternative exists partly for this.

**You want to start working.** The permit is built on not needing to. Taking Greek
employment is a change of basis rather than an addition to it, and it points to a
different permit. Remote work for a foreign employer sits in the same awkward space
and is the reason the digital nomad route exists separately.

## Risks and red flags

**Health insurance that does not meet the specification.** The most common cause of a
delayed file. The policy you already hold is probably not the policy the permit
requires.

**Assuming the 7% rate applies automatically.** It is an election with conditions.
Becoming tax resident first and asking later removes the choice.

**Counting salary as qualifying income.** It is not qualifying income for this route,
and discovering that after resigning is an expensive sequence.

**Buying property before the permit is settled.** The purchase does not help the FIP
application and it commits capital before you know the location suits you in winter.

**Treating consular figures as fixed.** Requirements vary by post and move. The
numbers on this page are planning figures, not a specification.

## Buyer and retiree scenarios

**A UK couple with a private pension of €4,200 a month, moving in spring.** Income
composition qualifies, and the pair requirement of roughly 20% above the single
figure is met. The 7% election is checked before departure, and a spring arrival puts
them past 183 days in the same year so the regime starts immediately. They rent for
the first year rather than buying.

**A US retiree on €3,000 pension plus €1,500 of dividends, moving in October.**
Combined passive income clears the threshold and the composition qualifies, since
both sources are passive. An October arrival keeps presence under 183 days in the
arrival year, so Greek tax residence begins the following January and the first US
filing year stays clean. US filing obligations continue regardless.

**A semi-retired consultant on €5,000, half of it employment income.** Does not
qualify on the FIP route despite the headline figure, because employment income is
excluded. The realistic options are restructuring the income, the digital nomad
route, or the Golden Visa if a €400,000 purchase was going to happen anyway.
