**Quick answer:** Halkidiki asks up to about €2,667 per square metre on
published market summaries, which places it above the Peloponnese at about €1,778 and
below the busier Ionian islands. It is northern Greece's coast, an hour from
Thessaloniki by road, with three peninsulas of which two are open to buyers. The whole
region sits in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier, unlike Thessaloniki itself which is
prime zone at €800,000. Its buyer pool is mostly Greek and Balkan, which makes the
market steadier and cheaper than an equivalent island.

## A note on the data before the numbers

Every other regional page on this site quotes a single portal index with a reference
month. Halkidiki is not covered by that dataset in the same way, so the figures here
come from published market summaries and are given as a band rather than a point.

That is a smaller claim than the ones made elsewhere and it is worth flagging rather
than smoothing over. Treat a band topping out near €2,667 as an order of magnitude that positions
the region against its alternatives, not as a valuation. For an actual purchase, local
listings and a local agent are the source, and the
[market overview](/property-for-sale/) explains why asking prices and transaction
prices answer different questions in any case.

## Three peninsulas, two of which you can buy on

| Peninsula | Character | Open to buyers |
|---|---|---|
| Kassandra | Most developed, resort building, densest summer population | Yes |
| Sithonia | Greener, mountainous, far less built, pine forest to the coves | Yes |
| Athos | Largely the autonomous monastic state | No |


Halkidiki reaches into the Aegean as three fingers, and they are genuinely different
places.

**Kassandra**, the westernmost, is the closest to Thessaloniki and the most developed.
Resort building, holiday apartments, beach clubs and the densest summer population.
This is where the letting market is and where the summer traffic goes.

**Sithonia**, the middle, is greener, more mountainous, far less built and much
quieter. Pine forest running down to coves, fewer large developments, and the
peninsula that most people who know the region prefer. Prices reflect the scarcity of
built stock rather than the presence of infrastructure.

**Athos**, the easternmost, is largely the autonomous monastic state of Mount Athos.
It is not open to property purchase, and it is closed to women entirely. For a buyer
it is scenery on the horizon rather than a market.

So the practical choice is Kassandra or Sithonia, and it is the same choice that
recurs across Greece in different clothes: the developed side with the letting demand,
or the quiet side with the landscape.

## The buyer pool is the story here

This is what makes Halkidiki different from every island page on this site, and it
explains both the prices and the way the market behaves.

The dominant buyers are Greeks from Thessaloniki, for whom Halkidiki is not a
destination but the local coast, the place you go on a Friday afternoon. Underneath
that sits substantial Balkan demand: buyers from Bulgaria, Serbia, Romania and North
Macedonia, all of whom can drive here in a day and many of whom have been coming for
a generation.

Northern European interest exists and is a fraction of what the islands attract.

Two consequences follow.

**The market is steadier.** Domestic and regional demand driven by proximity does not
move in step with international holiday sentiment. A soft year for foreign buyers in
Greece is not automatically a soft year here.

**Prices are capped by the marginal buyer.** A Thessaloniki family comparing a
Halkidiki house against other Greek options prices differently from a northern
European comparing a Greek island against Spain and Croatia. That is a large part of
why the region asks less than an equivalent island coastline.

For a foreign buyer both of those are advantages, with one caveat: at resale you are
selling into a mostly domestic market, so the price is set by what a Greek or Balkan
buyer will pay rather than by what you paid.

## Golden Visa: the threshold halves outside the city

Thessaloniki is named in the prime zone under Law 5100/2024 and requires €800,000.
Halkidiki, an hour away by road, sits in the €400,000 tier.

That is one of the sharper discontinuities in the Greek tier map, and it runs along a
boundary that a buyer would not otherwise notice. Someone looking for a qualifying
purchase near Thessaloniki can halve the threshold by looking at the coast instead of
the city, at the cost of everything a city provides.

At Halkidiki prices, €400,000 corresponds to roughly 150 to 200 square metres, which
clears the 120 square metre main usable area requirement with room. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones and
the [Thessaloniki €800,000 guide](/guides/thessaloniki-golden-visa-800000-property/)
covers the city side of the same boundary.

As everywhere, the tier follows the cadastral parcel, so a property near the boundary
should be confirmed against the land registry rather than assumed from its postal
address.

## Season, and why it is not an island season

Halkidiki is seasonal and the shape is different from anywhere offshore.

Because it is a road destination for a city of a million people and for a large Balkan
catchment, weekend demand runs well beyond the peak weeks. Shoulder seasons are
genuinely active rather than notionally so. Kassandra's resort areas empty in winter as
resort areas do everywhere, but nothing becomes inaccessible, because everything
remains a drive.

For an owner that means a longer usable season, more realistic winter access, and a
letting profile with lower peaks and a broader base than a Cycladic property would
show. It also means summer weekend traffic on the Kassandra road that people who have
not experienced it consistently underestimate.

## Thessaloniki, and why the region depends on it

Halkidiki cannot be understood without the city an hour away, in a way that no island
depends on any city.

Thessaloniki is Greece's second city, with a population around a million in the wider
area, a major university, a port, a hospital network and an international airport with
good European connections. Halkidiki is its coastline in the same sense that the
Athens Riviera is Athens's, except further away and considerably less built.

Three things follow from that relationship.

**Services are an hour away rather than absent.** Hospitals, specialists, large
retail, professional services and an airport are all reachable in a normal drive. That
is a different proposition from an island where the nearest hospital is a ferry or a
short flight, and it matters most to exactly the buyers who most often overlook it:
older ones, and anyone planning to spend real time here.

**The letting market has a domestic base.** City weekends, Greek summer holidays and
Balkan road tourism produce demand that is not dependent on international flight
schedules or on a foreign holiday season.

**Resale depends on Thessaloniki's economy.** That is the other side of the same coin.
A market underpinned by a single regional city is exposed to how that city is doing,
in a way that a market of international buyers is not. It is steadier and it is less
diversified, and both statements are true at once.

For anyone weighing this region against a Greek island, the honest framing is that you
are choosing a different kind of risk rather than less of it.

## What to check in Halkidiki specifically

The standard [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies,
with three local emphases.

**Forestry designation.** Much of Sithonia and parts of Kassandra are wooded, and land
classified as forest carries restrictions on building that are strict and not always
obvious from the ground. This is the single most common complication in the region.

**Coastal setback.** Building near the shoreline is regulated, and a plot with sea
frontage is not automatically a plot with a buildable sea frontage.

**Cadastre against reality.** The [cadastre check](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/)
matters here as elsewhere, particularly on larger rural plots where boundaries were
historically informal.

## What a budget buys

| Budget | At the upper band, about €2,667/m² | For comparison, Peloponnese at €1,778/m² |
|---|---:|---:|
| €250,000 | about 94 m² | about 141 m² |
| €400,000 | about 150 m² | about 225 m² |
| €600,000 | about 225 m² | about 337 m² |

At the top of the reported band a €400,000 budget corresponds to roughly 150 square
metres of built area before costs, which clears the 120 square metre Golden Visa area
requirement with room. Toward the lower end of the band it buys appreciably more.

Those numbers sit between the Peloponnese and the Ionian islands, which is exactly
where the region sits in every other respect. Acquisition costs come off buying power
first, and the [cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) sets them
out.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer connected to northern Greece or the Balkans.** This is the region's natural
buyer, and the case is straightforward: coast, pine forest, a drive rather than a
flight, and prices below the islands. Resale into the same pool that bought it.

**A buyer with €400,000 wanting a Golden Visa file near a city.** Halkidiki qualifies
at half the Thessaloniki threshold, with the area rule comfortably met. The property
is likely to be a normal house for its area rather than something stretched to reach a
number.

**A long-haul buyer choosing between here and an island.** Harder. Thessaloniki
airport has good European connections and fewer long-haul ones, the international
buyer pool at resale is thin, and the recognition that supports island prices is
absent. The advantages here are real and they are mostly advantages to someone who can
drive.

## Risks and red flags

**Forestry classification on wooded plots.** The most common regional complication and
entirely checkable in advance, though the check takes time and is worth starting on the
day an offer is contemplated rather than the week before completion.

**Assuming the Thessaloniki tier applies.** It does not, and the difference runs in
your favour, but the boundary is a parcel question rather than a postal one.

**Treating the price band as a valuation.** The data here is thinner than for the
islands, which is why it is a band.

**Underestimating the summer road.** Kassandra in August is a Greek city's weekend, and
that is a specific and busy thing, closer in feel to a crowded domestic resort than to
a quiet foreign holiday island.

**Buying international recognition that is not there.** Halkidiki is a superb
coastline with a domestic market. It is not an island brand and the resale market
reflects that.

**Assuming Sithonia is simply cheaper Kassandra.** It is less built because more of it
is wooded, protected or difficult, which is why it is quieter and also why a plot there
needs more checking before it turns out to be buildable.
