**Quick answer:** there is no reliable published price per square metre for Skiathos,
and anyone quoting one is quoting a luxury listing aggregator rather than a market
index. What can be established is the shape of the place: an unusually small island
with an international airport, a short and intense season, a quiet winter, the
€400,000 Golden Visa tier, and a neighbouring island in Skopelos that offers the same
landscape without the flights or the crowds.

## Why this page has no price table

Every other regional page on this site opens with a table: a figure, a source, a
reference month. Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Rhodes, Lefkada, the Peloponnese and Athens all
have one, because a portal index publishes them.

Skiathos does not appear in that dataset. What does appear in search results are
figures from luxury listing aggregators, which sample a handful of high-end villas and
produce averages in the seven figures. Those numbers are real in the sense that
somebody is asking them, and useless as a description of a market, because the sample
is not the market.

Publishing one anyway would look more authoritative than this section does and would
be considerably less true. So the honest position is: nobody publishes a defensible
Skiathos average, this page will not invent one, and what follows is what can actually
be established.

If you need a number for a specific purchase, the source is local listings for
comparable properties in the specific part of the island, read by somebody who knows
what has actually sold. That is a smaller and more useful claim than an island
average.

What can be offered instead is the neighbourhood the island sits in. These are
measured markets with published indices, and Skiathos sits somewhere among them
rather than outside them.

| Measured comparison | Asking price per m² | Source and reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Lefkada, Apollonio | about €4,910 | Indomio, June 2026 |
| Zakynthos, province | about €2,889 | Indomio, October 2025 |
| Rhodes, island average | about €2,421 | Indomio, January 2026 |
| Kefalonia, island average | under €2,400 | Indomio, June 2026 |
| Peloponnese, region | about €1,778 | Indomio, March 2026 |

Skiathos is smaller than every island in that table, more seasonal than most of them,
and better connected by air than all but Rhodes. Where those three pull against each
other is exactly why a single average would be misleading even if one existed.

## What can be established: the airport

Skiathos is small and has an international airport, which for an island of this size
is genuinely unusual and is the single most important fact about its property market.

Direct summer charter and scheduled flights from the UK and northern Europe have run
for decades. The runway is short and the approach is well known to anyone who follows
aviation. The practical effect for a property owner is the one that matters: guests
and owners arrive on one flight rather than a flight plus a ferry, and the buyer pool
at resale includes anyone who can book a direct seat.

Compare that with [Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/), which is larger and has
an airport with thinner international service, or with Skopelos next door, which has
none. On the islands, airlift is the variable that most reliably separates a market
that functions from one that does not, and Skiathos punches far above its size on it.

The corollary is dependence. A market built on direct summer flights is a market
exposed to the airlines that operate them, in a way that a large island with a
year-round economy is not.

## The Sporades around it

**Skopelos** is half an hour away by ferry, greener and more heavily forested, with no
airport, far less development and a market that is thinner in both directions. It is
where people who want the Sporades without the Skiathos summer end up, and the trade
is a smaller resale pool.

**Alonissos** is quieter again, with a marine park offshore and correspondingly little
development. A place bought by people who wanted Alonissos.

**Skyros**, further out and administratively separate from the others, is barely a
market at all in the sense a foreign buyer would recognise.

| Island | Airport | Development | Golden Visa tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skiathos | International, direct summer flights | Developed tourism economy | €400,000 |
| Skopelos | None, ferry only | Light, heavily forested | €400,000 |
| Alonissos | None, ferry only | Minimal, marine park offshore | €400,000 |
| Skyros | Small, limited service | Barely a foreign market | €400,000 |

All four sit in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier alongside Skiathos, so the tier is not
what differentiates them. Access is, and the table above is really a table about
flights.

## The season, and the winter

Skiathos runs on a summer that is roughly May to early October, with the strong weeks
narrower still. Flights, ferries and most businesses follow that curve.

Winter is quiet even by Greek island standards. The permanent population is small,
services reduce, and a substantial share of what makes the island attractive in August
is closed. That is not a criticism of the place, it is a description of a small island
with a tourism economy, and it applies to most of the Aegean.

What it means for a buyer is the same instruction that appears on every island page
here, and it is repeated because people keep not doing it: see the island in February
before you commit. The August version sells itself. The February version is the one
you would own.

## Golden Visa position

Skiathos, Skopelos and Alonissos all fall in the €400,000 tier rather than the
€800,000 prime zone, since none is named in Law 5100/2024 and none passes the
population threshold.

A qualifying purchase is a single residential property at or above €400,000 on one
title deed with at least 120 square metres of main usable area, certified by a Greek
engineer rather than taken from a listing. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones.

The restriction that follows matters here for the same reason it matters across the
Aegean: a qualifying property may only be let on leases of twelve months or longer,
and short-term letting is prohibited for the life of the permit. On an island whose
entire income case is a ten-week summer, that removes the income case rather than
reducing it. The
[letting restriction guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/) covers
what renewal checks.

## What to check on Skiathos specifically

The standard [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies,
with three emphases that follow from the island being small, wooded and steep.

**Forestry designation.** Skiathos and Skopelos are heavily forested, and land
classified as forest carries building restrictions that are strict and not visible
from the ground. This is the most common complication in the Sporades.

**Access and terrain.** Steep sites are common, and a plot with a view may not have
vehicle access to the door. That affects building costs, servicing and eventual
resale.

**Building legality.** The
[engineer's certificate](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) does the usual
work, and on older island stock enclosed terraces and additions are common. The
[cadastre check](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/) covers boundaries, which on
small wooded islands are frequently old and informally understood.

## What the missing data actually tells you

The absence of a published index for Skiathos is itself informative, and worth reading
rather than lamenting.

Portal indices exist where there is enough listing volume to average. Kefalonia,
Zakynthos, Rhodes and the Peloponnese all clear that bar. Skiathos does not, which
means the number of properties on the market at any moment is small.

Three consequences follow for a buyer, and none of them appears in a listing.

**Individual transactions move the market.** On a thin market a single ambitious sale
resets what nearby sellers ask, and a single motivated one resets what buyers expect.
Neither is a trend, and both will be presented as one.

**Comparables are scarce.** Valuing a property here means finding genuinely similar
recent sales, and on a small island there may be two rather than twenty. That makes
local knowledge worth more than anywhere with a functioning index, and it makes a
confident valuation from a distance worth less.

**Exit takes longer.** The same shortage of listings that prevents an index reflects a
shortage of transactions, which is the condition you meet when selling. A property
that suits a narrow buyer waits for that buyer.

None of this argues against buying on a small island. It argues for buying something
you would be content to hold, and for treating any confident market projection about
Skiathos with the scepticism the underlying data deserves.

## The competition here is the weakest in Greece

Worth stating plainly, because it is unusual. The search results for Skiathos property
are held by one single-office website, four portals running their generic island
template, a Facebook group and a listings aggregator.

Not one of them tells a prospective buyer what the island costs, how it compares to
anywhere else, what the tier is, what forestry designation means or what happens in
February. The information gap here is wider than on any island of comparable demand,
which is a statement about the incumbents rather than about the island.

That gap is why this page exists in the form it does. It cannot close the price
question, because the data is not there. It can close every other question a buyer
has, which is more than any current alternative does.

## What is knowable and what is not

Being explicit about the boundary is more useful than pretending it is not there.

| Question | Answerable here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What does the island cost per m² | No | No published index covers it |
| How does it compare to measured islands | Yes | Their indices are published |
| Which Golden Visa tier applies | Yes | Set by statute and population |
| Can I let it short-term | Yes | Set by statute, and by permit status |
| What is the season | Yes | Observable and consistent |
| What restricts building | Yes | Forestry, terrain and legality, all checkable |
| What will a specific house sell for | No | That is a valuation, not a page |

The pattern is that everything set by law, geography or observation can be answered,
and everything that requires a market measurement cannot. That is not a limitation of
this page so much as a description of what data exists about small Greek islands, and
it applies equally to every competitor quoting a confident figure.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer wanting a summer house with direct flights.** This is Skiathos's case and it
is a strong one. Few Greek islands of this size let guests arrive without a connection.
Budget for a short, intense season and a quiet winter, and price the property on
whether you want it rather than on projected occupancy.

**A buyer wanting the Sporades without the crowds.** Skopelos, at the cost of a ferry
transfer and a thinner resale market. Greener, quieter, less built, and bought by
people who chose it deliberately.

**A buyer taking a €400,000 Golden Visa file.** The tier applies across the Sporades
and the area rule is the thing to verify, since 120 square metres of certified main
usable area is a real constraint on small island stock. The letting restriction
removes the summer income model, so the purchase has to stand as a house.

## Risks and red flags

**Any quoted island average.** There isn't one. Treat a confident figure as a signal
about the source rather than about the island.

**Forestry classification.** The most common Sporades complication, checkable in
advance and expensive afterwards.

**Airline dependence.** A market built on direct summer flights is exposed to the
carriers that fly them.

**Buying in August.** The single most repeated warning on this site, and the one most
consistently ignored on small islands.

**Assuming Skopelos is cheaper Skiathos.** No airport is a different proposition, not
a discount.
