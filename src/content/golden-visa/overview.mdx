**Quick answer:** the Greece Golden Visa grants residence, with no minimum stay
requirement, in exchange for a qualifying property held on a single title deed:
€800,000 in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and the larger islands,
€400,000 in the rest of Greece, or €250,000 for a conversion or a listed
restoration. The two higher tiers require 120 m² of main usable area as certified by
a Greek engineer. Short-term letting of the qualifying property is prohibited for
the life of the permit. Allow twelve to eighteen months and 7 to 10 percent in
acquisition costs above the threshold.

## What the permit actually gives you

The Greek Golden Visa is a residence permit obtained by investment. It grants the
right to live in Greece, not the obligation, and that distinction is the whole
product. There is no minimum stay requirement to hold it or to renew it, provided
the qualifying investment is maintained. Holders may enter and remain in Greece
freely and travel within the Schengen area on the usual short-stay terms.

What it does not give you is equally worth stating plainly. It is not citizenship
and it does not put you on an accelerated path to one: naturalisation runs on a
separate clock that counts physical residence, which is precisely what this permit
does not require. It carries no right to work as an employee in Greece. And it
attaches to the investment, so it lasts exactly as long as the qualifying asset is
held.

That combination suits one profile very well and another poorly. If you want
optionality, a European base you can use when you choose, and a residence status
that does not require you to move, this is an efficient instrument. If you intend
to actually live in Greece, the financially independent person and digital nomad
routes reach the same place without requiring a property purchase at all.

## One property means one deed

Under Law 5100/2024 the qualifying investment must be a single residential property
held on a single title deed. This is the condition that most often surprises buyers,
because it removes the obvious workaround.

Two apartments in the same building do not qualify, however adjacent they are, and
however neatly the sum reaches the threshold. Two deeds are two properties. The only
way a pair of units qualifies is if they have been legally merged into one property,
with the merger completed and registered before the application, which is a
construction and registration exercise rather than a paperwork one.

The practical consequence is that budget and geography interact. A buyer with
€400,000 in the prime zone cannot assemble the €800,000 threshold from two smaller
purchases there; they either move to a €400,000 region or they change the budget.
The [tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out which
zones fall where, and the zone is decided by the cadastral parcel rather than by
the postal address.

## The 120 m² test is an engineer's number

The €400,000 and €800,000 tiers require at least 120 m² of main usable area. Three
things about that measurement decide files.

It is **main usable area**, not gross area and not the number in the listing.
Balconies, terraces, parking spaces, storage rooms and shares of communal space are
excluded from the count.

It is **certified by a licensed Greek engineer** on the Electronic Building Identity.
The Ministry reads the certificate. It does not read the brochure, the agent's
particulars or the seller's assurance, and the gap between those numbers and the
certified one is routinely ten to twenty square metres on older stock.

It is **a threshold, not a target**. A property certified at 118 m² does not
qualify, and there is no discretion at the margin.

The [120 m² rule guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) works
through how the measurement is taken and where listings and certificates most often
diverge. The operational advice is simple: get the engineer's number before the
deposit, not before the application.

## What the €250,000 route actually requires

The reduced threshold exists, and it is narrower than the headline suggests. It
applies where a building is converted from another use to residential, or where a
listed property is restored. It is also the one tier exempt from the 120 m² rule.

| Tier | Where it applies | Area rule | Typical file |
|---|---|---|---|
| €800,000 | Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, islands above the population threshold | 120 m² main usable, one deed | Prime-zone apartment or villa |
| €400,000 | The rest of Greece, including most of Crete and the Peloponnese | 120 m² main usable, one deed | Regional apartment, town house |
| €250,000 | Conversion from another use, or restoration of a listed building | Exempt | Project with works, not a finished home |

What that table understates is the nature of the €250,000 route. It is a
construction project with a permit trail, a completion obligation and a timeline
that runs alongside the permit application rather than before it. The saving on
entry is real and the execution risk is real too, which is why it suits buyers who
have done this kind of work before and rarely suits first-time overseas buyers.

## The letting ban, and what it removes

A Golden Visa property may only be let on a lease of twelve months or longer.
Short-term tourist letting of the qualifying asset is prohibited for the life of
the permit, and breaching it puts the permit at risk on renewal rather than merely
attracting a fine.

This is the single most consequential rule for anyone modelling returns, because it
removes the peak-season nightly rate from the arithmetic entirely. A Cycladic
apartment that would earn well in July and August is, as a qualifying asset, a
long-let property earning a long-let yield. Any projection built on holiday-let
income does not describe a property that can also carry a Golden Visa. The
[short-term letting guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/)
sets out what counts as short-term and what the renewal check looks at.

The rule binds the property, not the owner, so it survives a change of plans and it
survives a change of manager.

## How the zone is actually decided

The tier follows the property, and the property is located by its cadastral parcel
rather than by its postal address or by the name of the area in the listing. That
distinction produces real surprises, because municipal boundaries and marketing
geography do not agree.

A property described as being in a €400,000 region may sit on a parcel inside an
Attica boundary. An island that a buyer thinks of as small may be above the
population threshold that puts it in the prime zone. Neither of these is discoverable
from the particulars, and neither is a judgement call once the parcel is identified:
the zone is a fact about the land registry entry.

The sequence that avoids the problem is short. Identify the KAEK, the cadastral
parcel number, before the offer. Check the zone against the parcel rather than the
address. Only then set the budget for that property. Doing it in the other order
produces the situation where a buyer has agreed a €450,000 price on something that
needs to be €800,000 to qualify, and the only remedies at that point are to spend
more or to walk away from a deposit.

location against the tiers, and the
[eligible projects directory](/guides/golden-visa-eligible-projects-directory/)
lists developments where the tier and the area rule have already been confirmed.

## What it costs beyond the threshold

The investment threshold is the visible number. Two other stacks sit on top.

| Cost | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Application fee, main applicant | €2,000 | Payable to the Greek authorities |
| Application fee, each adult family member | €150 | Separate from the main fee |
| Property transfer tax on a resale | 3.09% of taxable value | €12,360 at €400,000; €24,720 at €800,000 |
| VAT on a first sale by a developer | 24% | Applies instead of transfer tax, subject to the suspension regime |
| Acquisition costs overall | 7% to 10% of price | Notary, lawyer, registry, agent, each plus 24% VAT |

The item that most often lands outside the budget is the 24 percent VAT riding on
every professional fee, which makes a quoted lawyer's fee different from the amount
that leaves the account. The
[full cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) itemises the rest.

## The timeline, and what stretches it

Budget twelve to eighteen months end to end: roughly sixty to ninety days for the
purchase phase, then eight to fourteen months for the permit itself.

Three things push files past eighteen months, and none of them is the decision.

**Title defects.** An extension built without a permit, or an inheritance where one
heir has not signed. The engineer cannot certify, the notary cannot complete, and
the clock stops until it is resolved.

**Cadastre mismatches.** The registered footprint differs from the building. This
surfaces at registration, after money has moved, which is the expensive moment to
discover it.

**Incomplete bank traceability.** Funds must arrive in Greece through the banking
system and be documented from source. A transfer that cannot be traced cleanly to
its origin will be queried, and the query is answered with paperwork from a bank in
another country, which takes as long as it takes.

The [timeline guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-timeline-application-2026/) breaks
the phases down with the documents each one needs.

## Renewal, and where the citizenship clock starts

The permit renews on a five-year cycle for as long as the qualifying investment is
held. Renewal checks that the asset is still owned, still qualifying, and still not
being let short-term.

Citizenship is a different question with a different clock. Naturalisation counts
physical residence, and the Golden Visa deliberately does not require any. A holder
who spends three weeks a year in Greece accrues residence permits, not naturalisation
time. That is not a defect in the route, it is the trade the route makes, and it only
becomes a problem for people who bought it expecting the other thing. The
[citizenship guide](/guides/greece-citizenship-after-golden-visa/) sets out what the
naturalisation path actually counts.

Family members are included on the main applicant's file rather than holding
independent status, and the rules on which relatives qualify and until what age are
specific. The
[family members guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-family-members-rules/) covers who
can be included and what happens as children age out.

## Who this route suits

It suits a buyer who wants a European residence option without relocating, who can
hold the asset for the medium term, and who is buying a property they would be
content to own on its own merits. The permit is a benefit attached to a purchase,
and a purchase made only for the permit tends to be a purchase made badly.

It suits less well a buyer whose plan is to move to Greece, for whom cheaper routes
exist, and a buyer whose return model depends on short-term letting, which the rules
prohibit on the qualifying asset.

The test worth applying before anything else is simple. Strip the residence permit
out of the decision entirely and ask whether you would still buy this property at
this price. If the answer is yes, the permit is a benefit on top of a sound purchase.
If the answer is no, the permit is being asked to carry a purchase that does not
stand on its own, and that is the position from which most disappointing Golden Visa
files start.
