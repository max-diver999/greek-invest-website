**Quick answer:** Rhodes asks about €2,421 per square metre on January 2026 listing
data, up 22.40% in a year, ranging from roughly €1,453 in Archaggelos to €3,339 in
Lindos. That is around a third of Mykonos and broadly comparable with the Ionian
islands. What separates Rhodes from both is scale: it is large enough to carry a city,
a hospital and a year-round economy, which changes what owning here means. It falls in
the €800,000 Golden Visa tier because of its population, unlike the smaller Dodecanese
islands around it.

## What the island asks

| Where | Asking price per m² | Source and reference |

| Lindos | about €3,339 | Indomio, January 2026 |
| Dodecanese region average | about €2,438 | Indomio, November 2025 |
| Rhodes island average | about €2,421 | Indomio, January 2026 |
| Archaggelos | about €1,453 | Indomio, January 2026 |

Asking prices on Rhodes rose 22.40% year on year to January 2026, and the wider
Dodecanese rose 20.87% to November 2025. Both are asking prices from live listings
rather than recorded transactions, which the
[market overview](/property-for-sale/) explains at length.

Two readings of a rise that size are both defensible. Rhodes has drawn increased
foreign attention and has more year-round viability than comparable islands, which
supports real demand. And on a market of this scale, a change in what gets listed
moves the average without anything happening to prices actually paid. The figure is a
signal that the market is active rather than a return anyone should project forward.

Against the neighbours, Rhodes sits close to [Zakynthos](/property-for-sale/zakynthos/)
at €2,889 and above [Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/) at a little under
€2,400, and at roughly a third of [Mykonos](/property-for-sale/cyclades/) at €7,623.

## The island that keeps working after October

This is the argument for Rhodes and it is not a small one.

Rhodes Town carries a permanent population in the tens of thousands, a general
hospital, courts, a university presence, year-round shops and an airport that runs
through the winter with domestic and some international service. The resort strips
along the coast empty out, as they do everywhere, but the island itself does not shut.

For an owner that changes four things that matter more than they sound.

**Works can happen in winter.** Trades are on the island year-round, so a renovation
timetable is set by the scope rather than by who is available in February.

**A property can be viewed and sold in any month.** Resale is not confined to a
summer window, which is a real constraint on smaller islands.

**Healthcare is on the island.** For older buyers and for anyone considering more than
a few weeks a year, this is frequently the deciding factor rather than a footnote.

**Long letting is possible.** There is a resident rental market, which means a
property can earn outside the tourist season rather than sitting empty.

Very few Greek islands offer that combination. Crete does, at greater scale. Corfu
does, partially. Most of the Aegean does not.

## Where on the island, and what each part is for

**Rhodes Town and the northern tip.** The year-round island. Services, the port, the
airport within reach, and the medieval city. The most expensive part outside Lindos
and the part that behaves least like a resort.

**Lindos and the east coast.** The postcard Rhodes, and at about €3,339 per square
metre the most expensive area on the island. Strong letting demand, heavily seasonal,
and a village with strict controls on what may be built or altered.

**The west coast.** Windier, cooler, less developed, with the beaches that suit
watersports rather than swimming. Prices below the island average and a different
kind of buyer.

**The interior and the south, including Archaggelos.** Where the €1,453 end of the
range lives. Villages, agriculture, distance from the airport, and the part of the
island most overlooked from abroad. The money goes furthest here and the resale pool
is smallest.

The pattern is the one that repeats across Greek islands: price tracks proximity to
the airport and to the letting market, not the quality of the place.

## The old town is a different legal environment

The walled medieval city of Rhodes is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the most
tightly controlled property environments in the country.

Buying there brings constraints that go well beyond ordinary planning rules:
restrictions on alteration, on materials, on external appearance and on anything that
touches the fabric of a protected building. Works may require approvals from heritage
authorities, and excavation can uncover archaeology that stops a project entirely
while it is assessed.

None of that makes it a bad purchase. Buildings inside the walls are genuinely rare
assets and behave accordingly. It does mean that anyone considering one should
establish the specific constraints on the specific building before an offer rather
than after, because "we will open this up and put in a bathroom" is a sentence that
can turn out to be impossible.

## Golden Visa: Rhodes is prime zone, its neighbours are not

Rhodes sits above the population threshold that places an island in the €800,000 tier
under Law 5100/2024. The smaller Dodecanese islands nearby, Symi, Karpathos, Patmos
and the rest, fall in the €400,000 tier.

That produces a sharp discontinuity across a short ferry ride. A qualifying purchase
on Rhodes needs €800,000 in a single property with at least 120 square metres of main
usable area; the same buyer on a neighbouring island needs half that. Given Rhodes
asking prices near €2,421 per square metre, €800,000 corresponds to roughly 330 square
metres, which is a large house rather than a typical one.

The practical effect is that Golden Visa buyers on Rhodes concentrate in the higher
end of the market by necessity. The
[tier structure](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones,
and the [120 square metre rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/)
covers the area test that comes with both higher tiers.
As always the tier follows the cadastral parcel rather than the island's name.

## The Dodecanese around it

Rhodes is the entry point to an archipelago that most buyers never look past, and the
neighbours are a genuinely different proposition rather than smaller versions of the
same thing.

**Symi** is a single spectacular harbour of neoclassical houses stacked up two hillsides,
an hour from Rhodes by ferry, with a tiny year-round population and a season that is
short even by Aegean standards. Property there is bought by people who wanted Symi
specifically, and it resells to the same narrow group.

**Karpathos** is long, mountainous and windy, with a strong emigrant diaspora that owns
much of the housing stock and a culture that has stayed unusually intact. It is
harder to reach than Rhodes and priced accordingly.

**Patmos** carries a monastery that is a UNESCO site and a quiet, expensive market
disproportionate to its size, sustained by a small number of buyers who return.

**Kos** is the other Dodecanese island with an international airport and mass tourism,
which makes it the closest structural comparison to Rhodes, at a smaller scale and
with less year-round depth.

All of these sit in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier rather than Rhodes's €800,000 one,
which is the single most consequential fact about the archipelago for anyone
combining a purchase with a residence application. A buyer priced out of a qualifying
purchase on Rhodes may find the same budget qualifies comfortably an hour away, at the
cost of everything the larger island offers in services and connections.

## What a budget buys here

At the island average of about €2,421 per square metre, a €300,000 budget corresponds
to roughly 124 square metres of built area before costs. At Archaggelos prices near
€1,453 the same budget corresponds to about 206 square metres, and in Lindos at about
€3,339 to about 90.

Two adjustments apply as everywhere. Acquisition costs come off buying power first,
and the [cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) sets out what they
consist of. And condition, land and outbuildings move a real property well away from
any average in both directions.

The third adjustment is specific to Rhodes and worth stating: the island is large
enough that location within it changes the practical value of a property more than
the price difference suggests. A house forty minutes from the airport and a house
ninety minutes from it are different assets even at the same price per square metre,
because one gets used more and sells faster.

## What to check on Rhodes specifically

The standard [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies,
with three local emphases.

**Heritage and archaeological constraints**, not only inside the old town. Rhodes has
archaeology across the island, and a plot can carry restrictions that a listing does
not mention.

**Building legality on older stock.** The
[engineer's certificate](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) does the usual
work, and enclosed terraces and additions are as common here as elsewhere.

**The cadastre entry against what is on the ground.** The
[cadastre check](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/) matters on an island where
plot boundaries in villages are often old and informally understood.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer with €250,000 wanting a year-round house.** Achievable inland or in the
south, where the budget corresponds to roughly 172 square metres at Archaggelos
prices. The trade is distance from the airport and a smaller resale pool. Not a Golden
Visa file, so letting stays unrestricted.

**A buyer with €800,000 taking a Golden Visa file.** The €800,000 tier applies, and at
island average prices that corresponds to about 330 square metres, so the qualifying
property is at the large end of the market. Lindos or the northern coast are where
that money buys something that makes sense as a house rather than as a threshold.

**A retiree considering an island with a hospital.** Rhodes is one of the few Greek
islands where that combination exists at this price level, and the healthcare
argument is usually what decides between it and a cheaper Aegean alternative. The
[wider Greek market](/property-for-sale/) compares the regional options on service
levels rather than on price alone.

## Risks and red flags

**Projecting the 22.40% forward.** It describes asking prices in one twelve-month
window on a market where listing mix moves averages.

**Assuming the €400,000 tier because it is not Mykonos.** Rhodes is prime zone. The
neighbouring islands are not, and the difference is €400,000.

**Buying inside the walls without establishing what may be altered.** Heritage
constraints are real, specific to the building, and not negotiable.

**Treating the resort coast as the island.** The strips empty in winter even though
the island does not, and an August viewing on the east coast does not describe
February anywhere.

**Underestimating distance.** Rhodes is large. The drive from the southern villages to
the airport is a serious journey, and it affects both use and resale.

**Buying a resort-strip apartment as a year-round asset.** The two things this page
separates, the island working in winter and the coast emptying in winter, are not the
same claim. A block built for package holidays in a resort that closes is a seasonal
asset on a year-round island, and it behaves like the former.
