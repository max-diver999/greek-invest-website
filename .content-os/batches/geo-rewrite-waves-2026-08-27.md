Baseline: `.content-os/reports/GEO-DIAGNOSTIC-2026-08-27.md`. Corpus 139 files, mean **25.0/75**,
21 pages at zero, 29 pages capped by a gate.

Nothing in R1 and beyond starts without approval. No page is deleted and no 301 is added without
explicit permission — every merge below is a **proposal**, listed separately for that reason.

---

## How the per-page angles were produced, and how far to trust them

Four agents read the real pages in each cannibal cluster and proposed, per page, the one topic it
could truthfully own. Four more agents then attacked those proposals for invented facts, overlapping
claims and vagueness. They returned **61 problems across 37 proposed angles** — verdict `needs-work`
on all four clusters.

That result is the point, not a setback. Two examples of what the attack caught:

- Santorini's proposed angle rested on "a cave house has no external walls and no conventional
  measurable floor area". A yposkafo has a built façade with door and windows, and Greek engineers
  measure it conventionally. The angle was an invention.
- Heraklion's rested on the Kastelli airport move "removing the noise discount from the eastern
  suburbs". Neither the page nor any source says the discount exists.

So the angles below are **candidates, not findings**. Each one is verified against primary sources
before a word is written, and any that does not survive is replaced or the page is proposed for
merge. This is why the waves are expensive: the differentiator has to be found and checked, not
looked up.

Two angles came through unflagged: `areas/kallithea-property-investment` and the finding that
`highest-rental-yield-areas-greece` has no differentiator at all and should merge.

including closing paragraphs and source lines; score plus content validation after each page;
re-check the wave against **itself** for duplication before committing; one commit per wave with
before → after per file; push to the working branch.
