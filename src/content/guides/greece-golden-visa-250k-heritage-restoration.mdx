**Quick answer:** Greece's €250,000 Golden Visa survives on two routes: converting a commercial building to housing, or fully restoring a listed building, works finished before the application is filed. Realistic all-in is €290,000 to €330,000 over 12 to 24 months of project, and the 120 m² rule does not apply.

At €250,000 this is the cheapest Golden Visa in Greece and the most mispriced number in the programme's marketing: half the listings quoting it omit the works, the certificates and the year of project management standing between the price and the permit. The [conversion-route overview](/guides/greece-golden-visa-250000-conversion-route/) states the rules; this guide is the practice layer, what the projects actually cost, how long each stage runs, and where files die.

Everything is current as of 22 August 2026, and one framing governs throughout: this route is a development project with a visa attached, not a purchase with paperwork, and it rewards exactly the buyer who would enjoy running one.

## What Do the Two €250,000 Routes Require?

Both routes require one property, a €250,000 qualifying investment, and a finished building before filing: the conversion route turns lawful commercial space into approved housing, while the heritage route fully restores a listed building. Article 100 of Law 5100/2024 preserved both nationwide, Athens included, and neither carries the 120 m² minimum.

- Conversion: commercial in, certified housing out.
- Heritage: listed shell in, restored monument out.
- Both: complete before the Ministry file lodges.

| Requirement | Conversion route | Heritage route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset bought | Lawful commercial building | Listed / preserved building |
| Core obligation | Approved change of use to residential | Full restoration per heritage terms |
| Works status at filing | Complete, certified | Complete, certified |
| Geographic limit | None, Athens included | None |
| 120 m² rule | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Single property | Yes | Yes |

The absence of the 120 m² rule is the route's quiet structural advantage, and it is worth spelling out. On the standard tiers, €400,000 regionally or €800,000 in prime zones, a qualifying property must certify at least 120 m² of main usable area on one deed, which prices small central stock out of the programme entirely. The €250,000 routes carry no size floor at all: a 70 m² converted Plaka workshop or a 90 m² restored neoclassical in Nafplio qualifies fully, which makes these the only Golden Visa doors into the historic centres where 120 m² single-title apartments barely exist. The hard edge is the completion condition, which reorders the whole transaction: the building must exist as lawful housing, certificates in hand, before the Ministry sees the file, so the visa arrives at the end of a 12-to-24-month project rather than at the start of one.

## Which Buildings Actually Fit Each Route?

Conversion stock is central and unglamorous: former offices, workshops, ground-floor retail and small warehouses across Athens and Piraeus where zoning permits housing. Heritage stock is neoclassical and protected: pre-war listed buildings in Plaka, Nafplio, Hydra, Symi and the historic centres, typically bought for €120,000 to €200,000 and restored expensively.

- **Conversion candidates:** 1960s to 1980s office floors around the Athens centre, Piraeus port-adjacent workshops, closed retail in gentrifying districts; entry often €120,000 to €200,000 with works making up the qualifying balance.
- **Heritage candidates:** listed neoclassicals with protected facades and, often, decades of deferred maintenance; acquisition can be low, but heritage-approved works run €1,500 to €2,500+ per square metre.
- **What disqualifies quietly:** zoning that will not grant residential use, unauthorised additions that must be [regularised first](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/), co-ownership shares where one heir never signs, and buildings whose listing terms forbid the layout you need.
- **What the engineer decides:** feasibility before the deposit, the [certificate chain](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) after the works; this route lives and dies on engineering paper.

The market pattern as of 2026: curated conversion projects in central Athens and Piraeus are marketed specifically to this route, often pre-permitted and sometimes pre-certified, which compresses timeline and risk in exchange for a developer's margin priced inside the €250,000. Where the raw-material hunting is best, by district: the blocks between Omonia and the market halls for 1960s office floors at €1,100 to €1,600 per square metre; Piraeus behind the port for workshops at €900 to €1,400; Kypseli and Patisia for ground-floor retail beneath solid interwar blocks; and, on the heritage side, Nafplio's old town, Hydra, Symi and the Plaka fringes, where listed shells surface rarely and sell on relationships more than portals. Every one of those districts also carries this route's characteristic hazards, inheritance-tangled titles and undocumented past works, which is why the feasibility opinion precedes the deposit here even more strictly than elsewhere in Greece.

## What Does the €250K Route Cost All-In?

The realistic budget is €290,000 to €330,000 all-in: the qualifying €250,000 across purchase and works, plus 3.09% transfer tax on the acquisition, professional and permit fees, and a 10% to 15% works contingency. Complex heritage projects can pass €400,000, the point where the route stops making sense.

| Cost line | Typical range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | €120,000 to €200,000 | Transfer tax at 3.09% on this |
| Works to completion | €60,000 to €130,000 | Conversion; heritage runs higher |
| Permits, engineer, architect | €15,000 to €30,000 | Change of use or heritage consents |
| Legal, both files | €8,000 to €15,000 | Title, project contracts, immigration |
| Contingency | 10% to 15% of works | Old buildings keep secrets |
| Total | €290,000 to €330,000 | vs ~€430,000 at the regional tier |

The €100,000 to €140,000 saving against the [€400,000 tier](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) is real and it is earned rather than given: it pays for a year of project risk, contractor management and certificate-chasing that the standard tiers never ask of a buyer. One arithmetic rule protects every file on this route: the €250,000 floor is a threshold, not a price. Money spent above it on qualifying works still counts toward eligibility, so a €280,000 project is simply a compliant project, while a project that finishes at €245,000 of evidenced qualifying spend counts for nothing at all, because the test is binary and inspected once, at filing. Experienced counsel therefore budget the works to land at €260,000 to €270,000 of documented spend, buying €10,000 to €20,000 of margin against the one number the Ministry will actually check.

## How Long Does the Route Take, Stage by Stage?

The honest calendar is 12 to 24 months from offer to a lodgeable file: 3 to 4 months of purchase, 3 to 8 of permits, 6 to 12 of works for a conversion and more for heritage, then certification, followed by the same 8-to-14-month Ministry queue as everyone. Total: two to three years to a card.

1. Months 0 to 4: diligence and purchase, feasibility opinion before any deposit, the ordinary [buying process](/guides/how-to-buy-property-greece-step-by-step/) underneath.
2. Months 3 to 10: change-of-use or heritage permits; the stage with the widest variance and, on listed buildings, the archaeology and culture-ministry consents.
3. Months 9 to 20: works; conversions are predictable, restorations are archaeology with invoices.
4. Completion: habitation-grade certification, the Electronic Building Identity updated, the qualifying-spend file reconciled.
5. Filing onward: the [standard timeline](/guides/greece-golden-visa-timeline-application-2026/) applies unchanged.

Applicants who need residence sooner have two hybrids. The first is holding a valid national visa or existing Schengen access while the project runs, treating the works year as ordinary life rather than a waiting room. The second is buying a pre-permitted, near-complete project where the developer has already carried stages one to three: the calendar then compresses toward a normal purchase, the price carries the developer's margin for exactly that service, and the diligence question shifts from feasibility to whether the certificates being promised at handover are the certificates the Ministry file needs, listed by name in the contract.

## What Does a Real €250K Conversion Look Like in Numbers?

The arithmetic is clearest on a real shape: a 78 m² former workshop two streets behind the Piraeus market halls, bought for €148,000, converted for €118,000 of documented works, and filed at €266,000 of evidenced qualifying spend, a deliberate €16,000 of margin clear of the €250,000 floor.

| Stage | Item | Cost | Month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase | Workshop, lawful commercial title | €148,000 | 0 to 4 |
| Purchase | Transfer tax at 3.09% + fees | ~€11,600 | 4 |
| Permits | Change of use, engineer, architect | €19,000 | 4 to 10 |
| Works | Conversion to 1-bed + studio layout | €99,000 | 10 to 19 |
| Certification | Completion papers, ΗΤΚ update | included | 19 to 21 |
| Filing | Golden Visa lodged | fees apply | 21 |

Total cash out is roughly €297,000 including the non-qualifying taxes and fees; qualifying spend evidenced is €266,000, every invoice banked.

- Month 21: a finished central apartment near €3,000/m² district comparables.
- Standing value €230,000 to €245,000 against €266,000 of project cost, before the permit is priced.
- The same €297,000 buys no visa anywhere on the standard tiers.

What the example also shows is where the route's margin lives. Buy the shell €20,000 worse, or let works drift 15% over budget, and the project converges on regional-tier money with none of the regional tier's simplicity, which is the honest boundary of the whole strategy.

## What Are the Advantages and Disadvantages of the €250K Route?

The advantages are price and freedom from the 120 m² rule: entry €100,000 below the fund route's ticket and €140,000 below a regional purchase, in central locations the standard tiers cannot reach. The disadvantages are time, execution risk and the completion condition that pays out only at the end.

**Advantages**

- Cheapest qualifying investment in the programme: €250,000 against €350,000 and €400,000.
- No 120 m² test: central, characterful, small stock qualifies.
- Nationwide, Athens and the islands included; no regional restriction.
- Restored central housing lets well on the long leases the rules require, 4% to 6% gross in the districts this stock sits in.
- Real value creation: a finished conversion is usually worth more than its inputs.

**Disadvantages**

- 12 to 24 months of project before the visa clock starts.
- Execution risk sits with you: permits, contractors, heritage authorities.
- Capital is committed long before any residence right exists.
- Heritage overruns can erase the entire saving; €400,000 finishes are not rare.
- Exit market for restored listed buildings is a connoisseur's market, thinner than mainstream stock.

## What Are the Risks and Red Flags on €250K Files?

Files on this route die at four specific points: buying before feasibility is proven, permits that never land, spend that cannot be evidenced as qualifying, and marketing that sells the €250,000 headline without the works behind it. Each failure has a contractual or documentary defence available on day one for almost nothing.

- **Feasibility risk.** No deposit before a written engineer-and-lawyer opinion that residential use or the restoration consent is obtainable on this building.
- **Permit risk.** Make the preliminary agreement conditional on the change of use or heritage consent; a €150,000 warehouse that cannot become housing is just a warehouse.
- **Evidence risk.** The qualifying €250,000 must be traceable: banked payments, itemised contractor invoices, the [Circular 1/2026 trail](/guides/golden-visa-greece-bank-transfer-proof-funds/) on the purchase money. Cash-paid works do not count and cannot be repaired retroactively.
- **Marketing risk.** "Golden Visa from €250,000" listings that are ordinary apartments, or projects where the developer holds the permit risk but prices it like they do not: read which stage of the five you are actually buying into.
- **Completion-condition risk.** Filing early, before certificates, wastes the fee and reveals the file; the condition is binary and tested once.

## Which Buyer Scenario Fits the €250K Route?

The route fits the hands-on buyer with time, the developer-shopper buying a packaged project, and the small-capital applicant for whom €100,000 of saving justifies a year of patience. It does not fit deadline cases, remote buyers without local project support, or anyone who wants a finished key on day one.

- Time-rich and hands-on: this route pays you twice.
- Packaged project at stage four: pay the margin, skip the casino.
- Any deadline at all: use the fund or a standard tier.

**The hands-on restorer.** Comfortable with contractors, resident enough to visit site, genuinely wants the neoclassical: the route pays this buyer twice, in the €100,000+ saving and in the finished asset's uplift.

**The package buyer.** Buys a pre-permitted Athens conversion at stage four of five: pays a margin, skips the permit casino, and lands closer to 15 months total than 30.

**The deadline case.** Wrong route entirely: the [fund's four weeks](/guides/greece-golden-visa-350k-fund-route/) or a [regional purchase's fourteen](/compare/athens-riviera-vs-crete-golden-visa/) exist precisely for this file.
