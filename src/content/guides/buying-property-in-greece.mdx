**Quick answer:** a foreigner can buy almost anywhere in Greece on the same freehold
terms as a Greek national, and the only real restriction is the border zone consent
a non-EU buyer needs in designated areas. The sequence is: obtain an AFM tax number
and a Greek bank account, engage a lawyer and a civil engineer, sign a notarial
preliminary agreement with the searches written in as conditions, then complete
before a notary and register the deed. Costs run 7 to 10 percent over the price,
of which the transfer tax on a resale is an effective 3.09 percent of the taxable
value, while a first sale by a developer carries 24 percent VAT instead.

Greece does not make it hard for a foreigner to own a house. It makes it hard to
own one without noticing what you agreed to. The purchase itself is a Napoleonic
deed process: a public notary reads the deed aloud, the parties sign, and the deed
is registered. Everything that protects you happens before that room, in searches
and certificates that nobody will run unless you instruct someone to run them.

This guide is the sequence. Where a number is statutory it is cited to the law that
sets it. The [full cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) and the
[search-by-search due diligence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) each have
their own guide, and this page links out rather than repeating them.

## Can a foreigner buy at all, and where the exception sits

Greece places no general restriction on foreign ownership of residential property.
A buyer from the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia or the Gulf acquires
the same freehold interest as a Greek national, registers it in the same cadastre,
and passes it on under the same inheritance rules.

The exception is geographic rather than personal. Areas designated as border zones,
which include stretches near land frontiers and a number of islands, require a
non-EU buyer to obtain consent from a regional committee before the deed can be
executed. The application is administrative rather than adversarial, and it is
granted routinely for residential purchases, but it takes weeks and it cannot be
retrofitted after signature. If the property sits in a designated zone, the consent
application belongs in the timetable on the day you make the offer.
The [border zone rules for foreign buyers](/guides/border-zones-greece-foreign-buyers/)
set out which areas are covered and what the committee asks for.

## The two numbers that come before everything

Nothing moves until you have both of these, and both can be obtained without
setting foot in Greece.

**The AFM.** A nine-digit Greek tax number issued by the tax office to residents
and non-residents alike. It appears on the deed, on the utility accounts, and on
every tax filing you will ever make in Greece. Getting one requires a passport, a
proof of address, and either your presence or a representative acting under power
of attorney.

**A Greek bank account.** The purchase funds must arrive in Greece through the
banking system and be traceable, because the notary records the payment mechanism
in the deed. Account opening for a non-resident is a compliance exercise rather
than a formality: expect to document the source of funds, and expect the bank to
ask again if the sum is large relative to your declared income. Start it early. Of
all the steps in a Greek purchase, this is the one that most often sets the pace.

## Three professionals, three different jobs

Buyers from common-law countries routinely assume the notary is the equivalent of a
conveyancing solicitor. The notary is not.

| Role | Acts for | What they actually do |
|---|---|---|
| Notary (συμβολαιογράφος) | Neither party; a public officer | Drafts and authenticates the deed, reads it aloud, confirms identity and capacity, lodges it for registration |
| Lawyer (δικηγόρος) | You, and only you | Title chain, cadastre check, planning and legality position, tax clearances, contract terms, and the power of attorney if you are remote |
| Civil engineer (μηχανικός) | You, engaged by you | Inspects the structure, confirms what is built matches what is permitted, issues the legality certificate the deed requires |

The estate agent is a fourth party and acts for the seller unless you have a written
buyer's mandate. That is not a criticism of Greek agents, it is simply what the
contract says.

## The contract chain, and where money leaves your account

A Greek purchase moves through three documents, and the money moves at two of them.

**Reservation.** An informal agreement to take the property off the market, usually
against a small holding deposit. Terms vary and the deposit is often not protected.
Treat this as a courtesy rather than as security, and keep the sum small.

**Preliminary agreement (προσύμφωνο).** A notarial pre-contract that fixes price,
parties, timetable and the conditions on which completion depends. This is where a
deposit of real size changes hands, commonly around ten percent, and this is the
document that decides what happens if the searches turn something up. Make the
conditions explicit: clean title, engineer's legality certificate issued, tax and
ENFIA clearances obtained, and border zone consent granted where relevant. A
preliminary agreement without conditions converts your deposit into the seller's
compensation for your caution.

**Final deed (συμβόλαιο αγοραπωλησίας).** Signed before the notary, with the
balance paid, and then registered at the land registry or cadastre office. Until
that registration is complete you have a contract, not a property right. Ask your
lawyer for the registration confirmation, not for the signed deed.

## What the searches must actually prove

"Do your due diligence" is advice nobody can act on. This is the list, and each
line names the document that closes it.

| What you need to know | The document that proves it |
|---|---|
| The seller owns it and can sell it | Title chain from the land registry or cadastre, going back far enough to clear inheritance |
| The footprint on the ground matches the record | Cadastre extract with the KAEK number, read against the engineer's survey |
| Nothing was built without permission | Civil engineer's legality certificate, covering extensions, terrace enclosures and basement conversions |
| No tax debt travels with the property | Tax clearance certificate, plus an ENFIA certificate confirming the annual property tax is paid |
| No mortgage or charge survives completion | Encumbrance search at the registry, dated close to completion |
| A non-EU buyer may complete here | Border zone consent, where the location requires it |

The two that most often produce a real problem are the legality certificate and the
inheritance chain. Greek building stock carries a long history of informal
extensions, and a terrace that was glazed in without a permit is not a cosmetic
issue: the engineer cannot certify it, and without the certificate the notary
cannot complete. Undivided inheritances are the other one. A house owned by six
cousins needs six signatures, and the cousin in Melbourne is the one who delays
completion by four months.

## The cost stack, and the rates that set it

Budget 7 to 10 percent of the purchase price. The composition is statutory at the
top and negotiable at the bottom.

| Item | Rate | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Property transfer tax (ΦΜΑ) on a resale | 3.09% of taxable value | 3% statutory plus the municipal surcharge |
| VAT on a first sale by a developer | 24% | Applies instead of transfer tax, subject to the suspension regime |
| Notary | Scale fee on the deed value | Plus 24% VAT |
| Lawyer | Typically a percentage or a fixed engagement | Plus 24% VAT |
| Land registry or cadastre | Scale fee | Registration, not optional |
| Agent | Commission by agreement | Plus 24% VAT |

Two things surprise buyers. The first is that Greek VAT at 24 percent rides on top
of every professional fee, so a quoted lawyer's fee is not the amount that leaves
your account. The second is the tax base: transfer tax is charged on the taxable
value, which is the higher of the objective value calculated by the state formula
and the declared price. On a €400,000 taxable value the transfer tax is €12,360, and
on €800,000 it is €24,720. The
[transfer tax guide](/guides/greece-property-transfer-tax-fma/) works through how the
objective value is calculated and when it exceeds the price you agreed.

## New build against resale

The choice changes the tax treatment, not just the finish.

A first sale by a developer falls within VAT at 24 percent rather than transfer tax,
subject to the suspension regime that has applied to new-build sales. A resale
carries transfer tax at 3.09 percent. On the same headline price those are very
different sums, and the difference is large enough that it belongs in the comparison
before you fall for the kitchen.

Resale stock also carries history, which cuts both ways. It is where the legality
issues live, and it is also where the surveys are cheaper to interpret because the
building has stood through a few winters. New build removes the legality question
and replaces it with delivery risk and a snagging process.

## Buying remotely by power of attorney

Most readers of this site buy without being in Greece for the search, and the
mechanism is a power of attorney granted to the Greek lawyer.

The chain is: draft the power of attorney with your Greek lawyer so it grants
exactly the acts required and no more, sign it before a notary in your own country,
have it apostilled under the Hague Convention, and have it translated by a certified
translator where the language requires. Then it is lodged in Greece and the lawyer
can act.

Allow three to four weeks for that chain rather than assuming it takes days, and
scope the document narrowly. A power of attorney that authorises the purchase of a
specified property on stated terms is safe. One that authorises the holder to deal
with your Greek assets generally is a different instrument, and there is no reason
to sign it. The
[power of attorney guide](/guides/power-of-attorney-property-greece/) sets out the
wording that limits it properly.

## If the purchase is also a Golden Visa file

The residence permit adds conditions to the purchase, and they bind the property
rather than the buyer.

Under Law 5100/2024 the qualifying investment is €800,000 in a single residential
property in the prime zone, which covers Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini
and islands above the population threshold, and €400,000 in a single property
elsewhere. A reduced threshold of €250,000 applies where a building is converted
from another use to residential, or restored as a listed property. The
[Golden Visa hub](/golden-visa/) sets out the tiers and the qualifying conditions.

Two practical points follow. The permit attaches to a single property, so assembling
the threshold from two smaller purchases does not work. And a Golden Visa property
may only be let on a lease of twelve months or longer: short-term tourist letting of
the qualifying asset is prohibited, which removes the summer-rental arithmetic from
the investment case entirely.

## What changes after you own it

Two recurring costs, and one of them moved this year.

**ENFIA**, the annual property tax, is assessed on the objective value and arrives
every year regardless of whether the property is used or let. The
[ENFIA guide](/guides/enfia-property-tax-greece/) covers how it is calculated and
where the supplementary charge on larger portfolios begins.

**Rental income**, if you let the property, is taxed on a scale that changed on
1 January 2026 under Law 5246/2025. The bands now run 15 percent on the first
€12,000, 25 percent from €12,001 to €24,000, 35 percent from €24,001 to €35,000, and
45 percent above that. The 25 percent band is new, and income that previously sat in
a lower band moved into it. An individual landlord may set only a flat 5 percent
deduction for repairs and maintenance against gross rent, so the tax is charged on
something close to the gross figure rather than on profit after costs. Any yield
arithmetic built on the pre-2026 scale needs redoing.

## The failure modes, and the certificate that catches each

| What goes wrong | Where it surfaces | What prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Extension or terrace built without a permit | Engineer cannot issue the legality certificate, deed stalls | Engineer's inspection before the preliminary agreement, not after |
| Undivided inheritance, one heir missing | Title chain shows co-owners who have not signed | Full title search back through the inheritance, early |
| Seller's unpaid ENFIA | Tax clearance refused | ENFIA certificate requested as a condition in the preliminary agreement |
| Cadastre footprint differs from reality | Registration query after completion | KAEK extract read against the engineer's survey, before signature |
| Border zone consent not obtained | Notary cannot execute the deed | Consent application filed at offer stage |
| Objective value above the agreed price | Transfer tax higher than budgeted | Objective value calculated before the deposit, not at the notary |

Every one of these is documentary, every one is knowable in advance, and every one
becomes expensive only when the deposit has already moved. That is the argument for
spending on a lawyer and an engineer before the preliminary agreement rather than
after it: the sums involved are small against a purchase price, and they buy the
right to walk away.

## Buyer scenarios

**A UK buyer taking a resale house on Corfu at €280,000.** Transfer tax at 3.09
percent on the taxable value, an engineer's survey that matters because the house is
older stock, and an inheritance chain worth checking properly. Not a Golden Visa
file at this price, so the twelve-month lease restriction does not apply and the
letting options stay open.

**A US buyer taking a €400,000 apartment in a regional city as a Golden Visa file.**
The threshold is met with a single property, the transfer tax is €12,360, and the
letting restriction binds: twelve-month leases only. The rental income arithmetic
runs on the 2026 scale, not the old one.

**A buyer purchasing off-plan from a developer at €500,000.** VAT at 24 percent
rather than transfer tax, subject to the suspension regime, so the tax question
needs answering before the reservation rather than at the deed. Delivery risk and
snagging replace legality risk.
