**Quick answer:** Lefkada asking prices run from about €1,970 per square metre in the
inland Karias area to about €4,910 in Apollonio on June 2026 listing data. The island
sits in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier. What distinguishes it from every other Greek
island is the causeway: Lefkada is connected to the mainland by road, so there is no
ferry and no flight dependency, which changes the cost of building, maintaining and
eventually selling a property here.

## The one Greek island you can drive to

Lefkada is joined to the mainland at Aktio by a causeway and a floating bridge, a few
minutes from Preveza airport and a straightforward drive from Athens or from the
Italian ferry ports.

That sounds like a detail and it is the most consequential fact about owning property
here. Every island page on this site ends up describing the same set of frictions:
materials that arrive by ferry, tradespeople who have to be brought across, winter
timetables that thin out, visitors who need a flight and a boat. Lefkada does not have
them.

| What island property usually costs extra | On Lefkada |
|---|---|
| Ferry premium on building materials | Road delivery at mainland rates |
| Bringing specialist trades across | They drive |
| Out-of-season access for owners | Drive, any month |
| Buyer pool at resale | Anyone willing to drive, not only to sail |
| Winter service reduction | Reduced, not severed |

The saving is real and mostly invisible in a price per square metre, because it shows
up in renovation budgets, maintenance and use rather than in the purchase. A
renovation on Lefkada is priced closer to a mainland renovation than to an island one,
which on a project of any size is a large number.

The other side is that the same road works both ways. Lefkada gets Greek weekend and
summer traffic that ferry-only islands do not, and the east coast in August reflects
that.

## What the island asks

A spread of roughly two and a half times inside a small island is wide, and it maps
onto the coast-versus-interior division rather than onto distance. Apollonio at the
top sits above [Zakynthos](/property-for-sale/zakynthos/) at €2,889 and approaches
Corfu levels; Karias at the bottom is comparable with inland
[Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/).

Both figures are asking prices from Indomio, a Greek property portal, and describe
what sellers request rather than what transactions recorded. The
[market overview](/property-for-sale/) sets out why that distinction matters.

## The three parts of the island

**The east coast, Nidri and the sheltered channel.** Calm water, a marina, the
sailing economy, and most of the letting stock. This is where the island is busiest
and where a property is easiest to let and to resell. It is also where the summer
traffic concentrates.

**The west coast.** Cliffs dropping to beaches that appear on every list of the best
in Europe, more wind, fewer services and a more dramatic landscape. Building sites are
harder and views are better. Fewer year-round conveniences.

**The interior, up toward Karias and the mountain villages.** Agricultural, cooler in
summer, and where the €1,970 end of the range sits. Space rather than yield, and the
part of the island where the road connection matters least because you are driving
anyway.

The practical rule is the one that repeats across Greece: the east coast is for
letting, the west is for the view, the interior is for space. What is unusual on
Lefkada is that all three are within half an hour of each other and of a mainland
road.

## Paxos and Ithaca: smaller, thinner, different

The same search cluster reaches two neighbouring islands worth separating clearly.

**Paxos** is tiny, with a small permanent population, no airport and a market
sustained by a limited number of buyers who know it specifically. Prices reflect
scarcity rather than infrastructure. It is bought by people who wanted Paxos and it
resells to the same narrow group, which is a genuine constraint rather than a
criticism: a thin buyer pool is comfortable when you are buying and is the whole
problem when you are selling.

**Ithaca** is quieter still, mountainous, reached by ferry from Kefalonia or the
mainland, and carries a mythological name that does more for recognition than for
prices. It has almost no development pressure, which is exactly what its buyers want.

Both sit in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier. Both are places where the honest advice is
that the purchase should make sense to you as a house, because the market will not do
the work of making it make sense later.

## Golden Visa position

Lefkada, Paxos and Ithaca all fall in the €400,000 tier rather than the €800,000
prime zone, since none is named in Law 5100/2024 and none passes the population
threshold.

A qualifying purchase is therefore a single residential property at or above €400,000
on one title deed with at least 120 square metres of main usable area, certified by a
Greek engineer. At Lefkada's upper-end prices near €4,910 per square metre, €400,000
corresponds to roughly 81 square metres, which is below the area threshold, so a
qualifying property on the expensive coast will cost meaningfully more than the tier
minimum. Inland at about €1,970 the same €400,000 corresponds to roughly 203 square
metres, comfortably over.

That interaction between the price and the area rule is worth working through before
shortlisting rather than after. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) covers the zones and the
[120 square metre rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) covers how
the area is measured, which is by an engineer's certificate rather than by a listing.

## What to check on Lefkada specifically

The standard [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies,
with three local emphases.

**Seismic history.** Lefkada sits in one of the more seismically active parts of
Greece and has been affected repeatedly. Building standards and retrofitting are a
real question rather than a theoretical one, and the
[engineer's inspection](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) does more work
here than on a quieter island.

**Coastal and setback rules.** The west coast in particular has terrain and coastal
protection constraints that limit what may be built and where. A plot with a view is
not automatically a plot you may build on.

**Cadastre against reality.** Village and agricultural plot boundaries in the interior
are frequently old and informally understood. The
[cadastre check](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/) is where that surfaces.

Note also that some Ionian locations fall within designated border zones, where a
non-EU buyer needs regional consent before a deed can be executed. The
[border zone guide](/guides/border-zones-greece-foreign-buyers/) sets out where that
applies and how long it takes, and the answer belongs in the timetable at offer stage.

## What a budget buys

At the inland Karias figure of about €1,970 per square metre, €250,000 corresponds to
roughly 127 square metres of built area before costs. At Apollonio prices near €4,910
the same budget corresponds to about 51 square metres.

That gap is the decision on this island in a single line. The same money is a
substantial village house with land or a small coastal apartment, and the drive
between them is twenty minutes.

Acquisition costs come off buying power first, and the
[cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) sets out what they consist
of. On Lefkada specifically, renovation budgets should be priced closer to mainland
rates than to island ones, which is the road connection paying for itself.

## Who ends up owning here

The buyer profile on Lefkada is unusual enough to be worth naming, because it explains
both the price structure and how a resale behaves.

A large share of foreign owners are sailors, and that is not incidental. The sheltered
channel on the east coast, the marina at Nidri and the cluster of islands within an
afternoon's sail make this one of the better cruising grounds in the Mediterranean.
People arrive by boat, keep coming back, and eventually buy. That produces a buyer
pool defined by an interest rather than by a nationality, which is a more durable kind
of demand than a fashion.

The second group is Greek. The road connection makes Lefkada reachable for a weekend
from Athens and from the whole of western Greece, which no ferry-only island manages.
Domestic weekend demand supports the market in a way that purely international islands
do not experience, and it also fills the east coast in August.

The third group is northern European families who found it through the beaches and
stayed for the practicality. This is the group most likely to buy inland, where the
money goes furthest.

What none of these groups is: an investor buying for yield. Lefkada is not priced as an
income asset and the letting season, while longer than the Aegean, is still a season.
Anyone approaching it as a return calculation is approaching a market whose buyers are
not doing that, and will be competing on price with people who value the thing itself.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer with €200,000 wanting a village house with land.** The interior, where the
budget corresponds to roughly 100 square metres at Karias prices with room for the
plot. The road connection means renovation is realistically priced and can happen in
winter. Not a Golden Visa file, so letting is unrestricted.

**A buyer with €400,000 taking a Golden Visa file.** The €400,000 tier applies but the
120 square metre area rule bites on the expensive coast, where that sum buys about 81
square metres. Either buy inland where the area comes easily, or budget above the tier
minimum on the coast. This is the specific trap on this island.

**A buyer wanting a boat.** The east coast and the Nidri channel, which is the reason
a substantial share of foreign owners are here at all. Sheltered water, a marina and
mainland road access for a trailer is a combination that does not exist elsewhere in
the Ionian.

## Risks and red flags

**Assuming the €400,000 tier is enough on the coast.** The area rule, not the price,
is the binding constraint at the top of this market.

**Underrating seismic questions.** They are real on Lefkada and they are what the
engineer is for.

**Buying a west coast view without checking what may be built.** Terrain and coastal
protection constraints are specific to the plot.

**Treating Paxos or Ithaca as small Lefkadas.** They are thinner markets with narrower
buyer pools and no road connection, and the resale dynamics differ substantially.

**Forgetting the road works both ways.** The same causeway that removes the ferry
premium brings mainland weekend traffic to the east coast in season.

**Buying the boat life without the boat costs.** Mooring, maintenance, winter storage
and the fact that a marina berth is a separate market from a house are all real, and
several owners here discover the annual figure only after the purchase that was
justified by it.
