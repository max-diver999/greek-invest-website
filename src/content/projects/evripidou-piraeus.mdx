There is no commercial arrangement between Greek Invest and Solena covering this scheme. It is
reviewed here because Piraeus is where the distance between a statutory threshold and an ordinary local
transaction is widest anywhere on this site, and because that distance is usually described as
regeneration rather than measured.

## In the port grid, €800,000 is not a price, it is a different market

Ordinary Piraeus stock transacts at around €200,000 to €350,000 for 70 to 95 square metres of 1980s to
2000s building. That is the street. A Golden Visa deed in Piraeus has to register at €800,000, which is
two to four times what the surrounding market clears at.

The consequence is not that Piraeus is expensive. It is that a Golden Visa buyer here is not buying into
the Piraeus market at all. They are buying into a thin layer of boutique new-build that sits on top of
it, priced by what a foreign residency buyer will pay rather than by what a Piraeus household will pay,
and that layer is a few dozen units across the whole municipality.

Everything else on this page follows from that one gap.

## The tenant who can pay proportionally does not live on this street

The only income a qualifying deed may earn is a twelve-month residential lease, because [short-term letting of the qualifying asset is banned nationwide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/). So the question that
decides whether an €800,000 Piraeus unit works as an investment is simple: who signs that lease, and at
what rent.

Evripidou Street is in the working port district — container traffic, ferry and cruise berths, truck
routes, marine services, the businesses that exist because the harbour does. It is a district with a
deep and stable tenant base, and that tenant base rents at Piraeus rents. A unit bought at €800,000 does
not command a rent four times the street's because it cost four times the street's; it commands
something closer to the top of the local range, and the yield compresses accordingly.

This is a structural gap, not a management failure, and no letting agent closes it. It is the reason a
Piraeus Golden Visa purchase is best understood as a residency transaction with a property attached
rather than as a yield play — and why a buyer whose primary goal is income should be reading the
[Piraeus area guide](/areas/piraeus-property-investment/) for sub-€400,000 stock that is not carrying a
permit.

## The harbour decides the street, and the street decides the letting

Piraeus is not uniform and the variation is not gradual. The working harbour sets a hard edge: some
addresses look at container cranes and truck routes, others sit a few hundred metres inland in ordinary
residential fabric, and the rent difference between them is larger than the distance suggests.

Before committing to any Piraeus address, three checks are worth making on foot rather than on a map:

- **What the windows face, at 7am on a weekday.** Port operations are not a background hum; they are
  scheduled and loud.
- **Which road the trucks use.** Freight routing to and from the terminals concentrates on specific
  streets and does not move.
- **Distance to the Metro and the tram**, because that is what the professional tenant is actually
  buying and it is what protects the rent when the port cycle turns.

A brochure describing a scheme as "Piraeus regeneration" is describing the municipality's direction of
travel, which is real, and not the address's exposure to the harbour, which is specific.

## Solena publishes no price list, so eligibility is unanswerable from public material

Solena does not publish unified pricing per Piraeus street address for this scheme. That is not unusual
for boutique off-plan, but it means neither Golden Visa test can be applied to Evripidou from anything
public. Both have to come from the developer in writing:

| What has to be in writing | Why the brochure will not do |
|---|---|
| Deed price per unit | The permit test is on the notarial consideration, not on a from-price |
| Certified main usable area | Balconies, storage and parking are excluded from the 120 m² |
| Whether it is one title | Two smaller units cannot be added together to reach the threshold |
| VAT or transfer tax | 24% on a first delivery against 3.09%, a large difference on a deed of this size |

Treat every quote as unit-specific until the preliminary agreement names a unit. On a scheme priced
around a statutory threshold, the gap between "from €800,000" and "this unit, at €800,000" is the whole
question.

## Where the sibling scheme differs

Solena markets a second Piraeus scheme, [ΕΛΑ Suites Kastella](/projects/ela-suites-kastella/), and the
two are worth reading against each other because they are the same developer, the same municipality and
the same statutory tier producing opposite products.

Kastella is the hill: sea aspect, ten fully furnished units, a lifestyle proposition. Evripidou is the
flat grid: working district, deeper year-round tenant demand, no view premium to pay for. A buyer who
wants the permit and a tenant should look at the grid. A buyer who wants the permit and the apartment
should look at the hill, and should read that page's furnishing question carefully, because a furnished
sale changes what the €800,000 is actually buying.

## What to establish before any payment

1. The specific unit, its deed price, and its certified enclosed area — all three in one document.
2. The building permit number, and confirmation that the issued permit matches what is being sold.
3. The land registry position on the site and the entity that owns it.
4. Security over stage payments: a bank guarantee or escrow, negotiated before signing, since Greek
   [off-plan practice](/guides/off-plan-property-greece-guide/) provides none automatically.
5. A written delay clause with a date and a financial consequence.
6. The lawyer's written confirmation of the tax treatment, VAT or transfer tax, on this specific deed.
