**Quick answer:** Athens and Thessaloniki both require the same €800,000 Golden Visa investment under Law 5100/2024, but they deliver different economics. Athens offers maximum liquidity, Riviera lifestyle, and global flight access at a premium per square metre. Thessaloniki typically delivers more space per euro, growing foreign demand from Turkey and the Balkans, and lower holding friction for buyers who do not need Attica prestige.

Buyers often assume Athens and Thessaloniki differ in Golden Visa price. They do not. Both sit in the €800,000 prime tier. The decision is which market delivers the best asset for the same legal threshold.

This guide compares what €800,000 buys, yield context, buyer demographics, liquidity, and practical due diligence in each city.

Both cities sit in the €800,000 tier, so the comparison is entirely about what the threshold buys. In Athens, €800,000 buys roughly 110 square metres in Kolonaki at about €7,270 per square metre, or 135 square metres of new-build Riviera stock in Glyfada at about €5,930, and the first of those fails the 120 square metre Golden Visa rule outright. In Thessaloniki the same money buys substantially more floor area, which is why a multigenerational household needing 140 square metres or more on one title finds it there without compromise. The yield gap runs the same way: Thessaloniki delivers 5.0% to 6.5% gross on long lets against 4.5% to 5.5% on Riviera stock. What Athens buys with the difference is flight connectivity, the international school map, and a resale market with a foreign buyer pool in it.

Both cities carry the €800,000 threshold, so the yield comparison is a comparison of what that fixed sum earns. Thessaloniki delivers 5.0% to 6.5% gross on long-term residential lets, which on an €800,000 deed is €40,000 to €52,000 of rent. Athens Riviera stock delivers 4.5% to 5.5%, or €36,000 to €44,000, and central Kolonaki at roughly €7,270 per square metre delivers less again because prestige capital value does not convert into proportional rent. Greek rental income tax then applies to gross rent at 15%, 25%, 35% and 45% by band with only a flat 5% repairs deduction, so at these rent levels most of the income is taxed in the top two bands in both cities and the net gap narrows to roughly half a percentage point. Short-term letting is prohibited on the qualifying deed in both.

## Buyer Demand and Exit Liquidity


Exit liquidity is where the two cities separate most sharply, and it is the variable buyers underweight. Athens is the default destination for international capital, with agency networks, English-language service and transaction volume that exceed any other Greek city, which matters when you sell to a foreign buyer rather than a local one. Thessaloniki's pool is improving on the back of Turkish, North Macedonian and Bulgarian proximity, but remains thinner than Attica.
Athens remains the default for international capital exiting to Western European and Gulf buyers. Agency networks, English-language services, and transaction volume exceed any other Greek city.

## Pros and Cons: Athens vs Thessaloniki at €800,000

### Athens: advantages

- Deepest international resale demand and English-speaking agency coverage in Greece
- Riviera access from Glyfada to Vouliagmeni within the same €800,000 legal tier
- Primary flight hub at Eleftherios Venizelos with year-round EU and Gulf connections
- Strong long-term tenant pool near metro lines and international schools in southern suburbs

### Athens: disadvantages

- Riviera premiums can consume budget before meeting 120 square metre comfort targets
- Older building stock may need renovation after engineer review
- Traffic and summer congestion in coastal districts affect owner-use experience

### Thessaloniki: advantages

- More usable square metres per euro at the identical €800,000 threshold
- Growing buyer interest from Turkey and Balkan markets supports niche exit liquidity
- Lower holding friction for owners who visit occasionally rather than living full time
- White Tower city centre and coastal suburbs offer walkable urban lifestyle below Attica premiums

### Thessaloniki: disadvantages

- Thinner premium exit market versus Athens for fast international resale

- Cooler winter climate may matter for buyers expecting year-round Mediterranean warmth
- Agency coverage in English is improving but still behind Attica for complex foreign files

## Banking and Purchase Sequence in Both Cities

The purchase sequence is identical under Law 5100/2024: Greek tax ID, bank account, lawyer review, deposit agreement, notary deed, land registry, then Ministry file. Athens transactions often involve more competitive bidding on Riviera listings, build escalation headroom into your €800,000 cap. Thessaloniki may offer more negotiation room on new-build stock but still requires engineer sign-off on usable area before Golden Visa submission.

Transfer tax at 3.09 percent applies in both cities on taxable value. Budget seven to ten percent all-in acquisition costs. Payment traceability under Circular 1/2026 is non-negotiable in 2026 audits.

After deed, both cities enter the same national Ministry queue, choosing Thessaloniki does not shorten permit processing versus Athens. See [realistic timeline scenarios](/guides/golden-visa-greece-timeline-2026-realistic/) for family planning.

Energy certificate ratings matter more in Thessaloniki new builds marketed to foreign buyers, Class A stock commands premium but reduces renovation risk. Athens older Riviera blocks may need elevator and facade upgrades after purchase. Budget five to fifteen percent contingency on top of acquisition stack in either city when engineers flag pre-1983 building codes. Keep six months of ENFIA and community charges in escrow after deed day in both markets.

Thessaloniki owners who visit quarterly often prefer larger terraces and gulf views without Riviera price pressure. When you compare net yields, use the same vacancy assumption in both cities. ## Buyer scenarios


Thessaloniki wins on floor area and gross yield; Athens wins on flights, schools and the depth of the pool you will eventually sell into. That is the whole trade, and which side of it you belong on depends on whether the property is a residency vehicle you visit or an income asset you never see.
Both cities sit in the €800,000 prime tier, so the choice is not about the threshold. It is about what €800,000 buys and who rents it.

**The residency-first buyer with a European base.** Athens wins on flight connectivity, international schools and resale liquidity to a foreign buyer pool. Accept a gross yield of 4.5% to 5.5% on Riviera stock and treat the asset as a residency vehicle with capital-appreciation optionality, particularly on the Ellinikon corridor. Budget €800,000 plus 7% to 10% acquisition costs and expect a 120 m² to 150 m² apartment in the southern suburbs.

**The yield-conscious buyer willing to hold longer.** Thessaloniki's 5.0% to 6.5% gross band is genuinely higher, and €800,000 buys substantially more floor area. The catch is that the local rental market does not pay proportionately more for a 220 m² apartment than a 130 m² one, so yield falls as unit size rises to meet the threshold. The workable structure is a large central apartment let to a family or an academic household on a twelve-month lease, underwritten at the lower end of the band.

**The buyer weighing a second property later.** Neither city allows two deeds to combine for the threshold. If the plan is to build a Greek portfolio, buy the qualifying asset first in Athens for liquidity, then add Thessaloniki or regional stock as a pure yield play outside the Golden Visa perimeter, where short-term letting remains available on a licensed unit.
