**Quick answer:** Ela Tinos is a twenty-nine-suite hotel-residence by Solena on Tinos, delivered in July
2024. It is the only completed scheme reviewed on this site and the only one that is not a block of
flats, and both facts govern everything else. Tinos exceeds the 3,100-resident island threshold, so the
Golden Visa floor is €800,000 on one deed with 120 square metres of certified enclosed area, and
short-term letting of that deed is prohibited for the life of the permit, which is precisely what a
hotel-residence exists to do. What follows is what a buyer should check, not a view on whether to buy.

Greek Invest earns nothing from Solena and sells no suite in this building. It is
reviewed here because hospitality product is sold to Golden Visa buyers more often than it qualifies for
them, and because a delivered building is the rare case where a buyer can check the claim instead of
weighing it.

## This is not an apartment, and that changes every answer

Ela Tinos is a hotel-residence: twenty-nine suites operating as hospitality, with individual titles sold
to owners. Every other project on this site is a block of flats. That single difference decides the
Golden Visa question, the income question and the exit question, and it decides all three against the
buyer who assumed this was a Cycladic apartment with a service charge.

A hotel-residence is a building whose business model is short stays. A Golden Visa asset is a property on
which short-term tourist letting is prohibited for the life of the permit. Those two statements describe
the same building, and reconciling them is not a matter of negotiation with the operator.

## The permit and the product point in opposite directions

Law 5100/2024 bars short-term letting of the qualifying property nationwide. It does not bar owning a
suite in a hotel-residence, and it does not bar letting one long-term. What it bars is the thing the
building exists to do.

Three consequences follow, and none of them is a matter of interpretation:

- **The operator agreement is an encumbrance on the use, not a service.** If the title or the management
  contract commits the suite to a rental pool that lets by the night, the property cannot simultaneously
  support a residence permit. Read the contract before the deed, not after.
- **The property class on the permit matters more than the marketing word.** A building permitted for
  tourist use (τουριστική χρήση) is not residential, and a Golden Visa file rests on residential
  property. "Suites" on a brochure settles nothing either way.
- **Withdrawing from the pool has a price.** Where the operator agreement allows an owner to opt out, it
  usually costs the yield the purchase was underwritten on. The buyer then holds a hospitality unit on
  Tinos with a twelve-month lease requirement and a tenant pool that does not exist in February.

## Tinos is an €800,000 island, which removes the obvious workaround

Tinos exceeds the 3,100-resident threshold in Law 5100/2024, so it sits in the €800,000 tier rather than
the €400,000 one. A buyer cannot solve the conflict above by buying two smaller suites, because the rule
is one property on one deed, and cannot solve it by buying cheaply, because the floor is the same as
Athens.

That is worth setting against the neighbouring option. On [Antiparos](/projects/ela-antiparos/), a
1,200-resident island, the same developer's product qualifies at €400,000. Tinos costs twice as much to
qualify and delivers a hospitality asset with an operator attached. The comparison is not close for a
buyer whose objective is the permit.

## Delivered in 2024, which makes this a resale, not an off-plan purchase

Ela Tinos handed over in July 2024. Nothing on this page about stage payments, bank guarantees or
completion risk applies, because the building exists and the units have owners.

That is an advantage and it should be used. A buyer here can do what no off-plan buyer can:

| What can be inspected | Why it settles a question a render cannot |
|---|---|
| The finished suite | Enclosed area is measurable on site, not projected |
| The operating hotel | Occupancy, service standard and wear are observable |
| The common charges to date | Two years of actual bills, not a developer's estimate |
| The management accounts | What the pool has actually distributed, against what was marketed |
| The resale market | Whether any original owner has sold, and at what |

That last line is the one to press. A hotel-residence delivered two years ago has a resale history or it
does not, and the absence of one is itself information.

## Twenty-nine suites means the operator, not the owners, runs the building

In a fourteen-unit apartment block the owners' assembly decides how the building is maintained. In a
twenty-nine-suite hotel-residence the operator does, under a contract the owners signed individually,
and an owner who dislikes a decision has a commercial dispute rather than a vote.

Ask three questions about that contract before committing:

1. **Its term and its renewal mechanism.** An operator agreement that outlives your intended hold is a
   constraint on the sale.
2. **Whether it binds a successor in title.** If it does, your buyer inherits it, and the pool of people
   who want a hospitality unit with a fixed operator is smaller than the pool who want a Tinos house.
3. **What the owner's own access is.** Personal-use weeks are allocated in the contract, and on
   hospitality product they are allocated in the low season. The
   [off-plan property guide](/guides/off-plan-property-greece-guide/) covers how Greek purchase contracts
   normally handle owner obligations that outlive the sale.

## Tinos runs on a calendar no other Cycladic island runs on

The revenue case for a hotel-residence rests on the island's season, and Tinos does not have the season
its neighbours have. Mykonos and Santorini fill on international leisure demand from May to October.
Tinos fills on pilgrimage. The Panagia Evangelistria church draws Greek visitors across the year and
concentrates them violently around the 15 August feast, when the island is at capacity and rates are set
by an event rather than by a market.

That produces a different revenue shape: a domestic-weighted guest base, a shorter and spikier peak, and
demand that is far less exposed to the international leisure cycle than a Mykonos operator's is. Whether
that is an advantage depends on what you are underwriting. It is more defensive in a weak European travel
year and it caps the upside in a strong one.

The practical consequence for a buyer is that operator projections built on Cycladic averages do not
describe this island. Ask for the property's own occupancy and rate history month by month rather than a
regional benchmark, and check how much of the annual take lands in the two weeks around the feast. A
building that earns a disproportionate share of its year in August is a building whose accounts are
sensitive to a single fortnight.

## Who should buy Ela Tinos

Somebody who wants a suite in a functioning hotel on Tinos, understands they are buying into an operating
business, and is not relying on it for a residence permit. On those terms the delivered building, the
inspectable accounts and the island are a coherent proposition.

A Golden Visa buyer should treat this scheme as unsuitable unless a lawyer confirms in writing that a
specific suite is residential on the permit, is free of any tourist-use or rental-pool encumbrance,
registers at or above €800,000 on one deed, and certifies 120 square metres of enclosed main usable area.
Those four conditions can be met. They are also, on hospitality product, the four least likely to be met
at once, and the [short-term rental ban](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/) explains why
the third and fourth are not negotiable.
