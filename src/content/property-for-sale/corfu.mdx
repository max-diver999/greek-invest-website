**Quick answer:** Corfu asks about €2,932 per square metre in the main municipality on
June 2026 listing data, rising to about €4,698 in the Kassiopi area and about €5,628
in the municipality of Paxoi within the wider prefecture. It is the most expensive
Ionian island, and the reason is the age and depth of its international market rather
than anything about the coastline. It falls in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier, with the
area rule rather than the price binding on the expensive north-east coast.

## What the island asks

| Where | Asking price per m² | Source and reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Paxoi, within Corfu Prefecture | about €5,628 | Indomio, June 2026 |
| Kassiopi, north-east coast | about €4,698 | Indomio, July 2026 |
| Municipality of Corfu | about €2,932 | Indomio, June 2026 |

All are asking prices from Indomio listings rather than recorded transactions; the
[market overview](/property-for-sale/) sets out why that distinction matters on thin
markets.

Against its neighbours, Corfu tops the Ionian. It sits above
[Zakynthos](/property-for-sale/zakynthos/) at about €2,889 and comfortably above
[Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/) at a little under €2,400, while remaining
far below [Mykonos](/property-for-sale/cyclades/) at about €7,623.

## The premium is a hundred and fifty years old

Corfu is not more beautiful than Kefalonia, and its beaches are not better than
Lefkada's. What it has that neither has is a foreign property market with genuine
history.

The British connection runs to the nineteenth century and the Ionian State, and the
modern version has been building since package flights began. The consequence is a
large stock of foreign-owned property, agents who have sold to international buyers
for generations, and northern European flights across a long season.

That produces three things a newer market does not have.

**Depth of demand.** More buyers looking at any moment, which supports price and
shortens time on market.

**A real resale market.** You are not the only person who might ever want the house.
On the thinner Ionian islands that is a genuine question; here it is not.

**Established professional infrastructure.** Lawyers, engineers, agents and builders
who have dealt with foreign buyers repeatedly and know what the process requires.

The price is what that maturity costs. A buyer choosing Corfu over Kefalonia is paying
roughly a fifth more per square metre for a deeper, more liquid market, and whether
that is worth it depends entirely on how long they intend to hold.

## Where on the island, and why the north-east costs what it does

**The north-east coast**, roughly Kassiopi through Kalami to Agni and Nissaki. Steep
green hillsides dropping to small bays, looking across a narrow strait to Albania. It
has carried British ownership for so long that it acquired a nickname, and it is both
the most expensive part of Corfu at about €4,698 per square metre and the most liquid
at resale.

**Corfu Town and the centre.** The Venetian old town, a UNESCO site, plus the
surrounding municipality at about €2,932 per square metre. Year-round services, the
airport, the port, and the part of the island that functions in February.

**The west coast.** Bigger beaches, more package tourism in places, more wind, and
prices below the north-east.

**The south and the interior.** Olive groves, villages, less international
recognition, and where the same budget buys substantially more. The part of Corfu
that most resembles Kefalonia in price and in feel.

The pattern that repeats across Greece applies here too, with one difference: on
Corfu the premium area is premium because of who has been buying there for fifty
years, not because of the airport. Everywhere else on the Ionian, airlift explains the
price map. Here, history does.

## Golden Visa: the area rule binds first on the north-east

Corfu sits in the €400,000 tier, since it is not named in the prime zone under Law
5100/2024 and falls below the population threshold.

The complication is arithmetic rather than legal. A qualifying property needs at least
120 square metres of main usable area, certified by a Greek engineer. At Kassiopi
prices near €4,698 per square metre, €400,000 corresponds to roughly 85 square metres,
which is below the threshold. In the municipality of Corfu at about €2,932 the same
sum corresponds to roughly 136 square metres, which clears it.

So a Golden Visa buyer on Corfu has a real choice to make: buy in the centre or the
south where €400,000 produces a qualifying property, or buy on the north-east coast
and budget meaningfully above the tier minimum. This is the same trap that appears on
[Lefkada](/property-for-sale/lefkada/), and it catches people who read the tier and
stopped there. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones, and
the [Corfu Golden Visa guide](/guides/corfu-property-investment-golden-visa/) covers
the island-specific detail.

The letting restriction applies as everywhere: twelve-month leases only for a
qualifying property, for the life of the permit.

## Old stock, and what it hides

Corfu has more historic building than most Greek islands, and the buildings are the
reason people fall for it. They are also where the money goes.

**Venetian and British-era town houses.** Thick stone walls that hold damp, structural
timber of unknown age, and roofs that have been patched rather than replaced across
generations. The
[engineer's inspection](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) is doing real
structural work here, not just checking permits.

**Generational additions.** A house extended repeatedly over a century frequently has
work that was never permitted. The engineer cannot certify it, and without the
certificate the notary cannot complete. On old island stock this is the single most
common reason a sale stalls.

**The UNESCO old town.** Corfu Town's old quarter carries alteration controls on
materials, appearance and structure that go beyond ordinary planning rules.
Establish what may be changed on a specific building before an offer.

**Boundaries in olive country.** Rural plots often carry boundaries that were
historically informal. The
[cadastre check](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/) is where that surfaces.

The full [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) covers each
search and the document that closes it.

## Liquidity is the thing you are actually buying

Every other island page on this site talks about the buyer pool as a risk. On Corfu it
is worth reframing as the product, because it is the only Greek island where a foreign
owner can reasonably assume there will be a market when they want out.

The mechanism is simple and it compounds. A long-established foreign market means
agents who list internationally as a matter of course, buyers who arrive already
knowing the island, and comparable sales to price against. Each of those makes the
next transaction easier, which is why depth persists once a market has it and why a
newer island struggles to acquire it however good the coastline.

For a buyer the practical readings are three.

**Time on market is shorter.** A correctly priced Corfu house sells in a normal
period rather than waiting for the one person who wanted that specific island.

**Pricing is more knowable.** With more transactions there are more comparables, so a
valuation is a judgement rather than a guess. On the thinnest islands there may be two
relevant sales in a year.

**The downside is shallower.** A market with several independent sources of demand
does not fall as far when one of them pauses.

None of that argues that Corfu is the better buy. It argues that the premium over
Kefalonia is purchasing something specific, and that a buyer who does not care about
resale is paying for a feature they will not use.

|---|---:|---:|
| €250,000 | about 85 m² | about 53 m² |
| €400,000 | about 136 m² | about 85 m² |
| €600,000 | about 205 m² | about 128 m² |

Acquisition costs come off buying power first, and the
[cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) sets out what they consist
of. On old stock, budget renovation separately and generously: the discount for
condition on a Corfiot town house is frequently smaller than the cost of curing it,
which is the reverse of the situation on cheaper islands.

## Buyer scenarios

**A buyer with €400,000 taking a Golden Visa file.** Works comfortably in the
municipality of Corfu or the south, where that sum clears the 120 square metre rule.
Does not work in Kassiopi without going well above the threshold. Decide which side of
that line you are on before shortlisting.

**A buyer wanting the north-east coast.** Budget above the tier minimum if a permit is
involved, expect a mature market with real competition, and treat the premium as
buying liquidity rather than buying scenery. Resale here is the easiest on the Ionian.

**A buyer comparing Corfu with Kefalonia.** Roughly a fifth more per square metre for
a materially deeper buyer pool, better professional infrastructure and easier exit.
Worth it for a shorter hold or if resale matters; less obviously worth it for someone
buying a house to keep.

## Risks and red flags

**Reading the tier without the area rule.** €400,000 does not produce a qualifying
property on the expensive coast.

**Underestimating old stock.** Structural timber, damp and unpermitted generational
additions are the three that cost money, and only the third stops a sale outright. The
first two simply arrive later, as a bill.

**Assuming the north-east premium is transferable.** It attaches to that coast, not to
the island. A house in the south is priced by the southern market, and a seller who
paid a north-east price for a southern house is carrying that difference themselves.

**UNESCO constraints in the old town.** Real, specific to the building, and not
negotiable. They are also the reason the old town looks the way it does, so a buyer
attracted by that appearance is buying into the rules that produced it rather than
around them.

**Buying on a portal listing alone.** Inventory is what portals do well. Condition,
legality and area certification are not in the listing, and on an island where a large
share of stock predates any modern permit regime that gap is where the risk lives.

**Assuming the long British presence means British standards.** It means a deeper
market and better-informed agents. It does not mean a survey has been done, a permit
exists, or that the previous foreign owner checked any of it before you.
