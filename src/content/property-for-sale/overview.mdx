**Quick answer:** there is no single price for Greek property worth quoting, and the
regional spread is the useful answer. Asking prices run from roughly €1,334 per
square metre in inland island areas to roughly €4,698 in prime Corfu, with the Aegean
Islands the most expensive region at about €3,626. The Bank of Greece index has
transactions up 5.7 percent year on year in the first quarter of 2026, fastest at
6.9 percent outside the main cities. Acquisition costs run 7 to 10 percent above the
price, and a foreigner may buy almost anywhere, subject to border zone consent in
designated areas.

## Why this page is not a listing grid

The search results for Greek property are portals: Rightmove, A Place in the Sun,
Zoopla, Spitogatos, each showing an inventory of houses. They are good at that, they
have the stock, and this page is not trying to compete with a database.

What none of them does is tell you what anything costs before you start scrolling.
A listing grid answers "what is available". It does not answer "is this island
expensive", "what does the market look like this year" or "which region fits what I
want". Those are the questions people actually have first, and they are the ones this
page answers.

## Two kinds of number, and why the difference matters

| Number type | What it measures | What it is good for |
|---|---|---|
| Asking price per m² | What sellers request in live listings | Comparing places against each other right now |
| Price index change | What recorded transactions did | Knowing whether a market is moving, and how fast |

Portals publish the first. Central banks publish the second. Most articles about
Greek property quote one and imply the other, which on thin island markets can be
seriously misleading. Every figure on this site says which one it is.

Asking prices below come from Indomio, a Greek property portal, for the June and
July 2026 reference months. They are a commercial source rather than official
statistics, and they describe opening positions rather than outcomes. Index figures
come from the Bank of Greece.

## The British and the Americans are searching for different things

This is worth knowing before you read any Greek property site, including this one.

Search demand in the two largest markets splits along vocabulary, and the split is
sharp enough that a page optimised for one is nearly invisible to the other. British
buyers search for **property for sale** and **houses for sale**. American buyers
search for **real estate**.

| Phrasing | United Kingdom | United States |
|---|---:|---:|
| property for sale in crete greece | 1,600 | 170 |
| real estate crete greece | not in the top set | 390 |
| houses for sale in greece | 1,300 | 2,400 |
| greece real estate | 110 | 1,000 |

The pattern runs through the whole market, not just Crete. It reflects a real
difference in what the two audiences are doing, as well as in how they say it. The
British market is older, larger for Greece specifically, and oriented toward a house
on a named island. The American market is smaller on property and much larger on
residence and relocation, which is why the American reading of "Greek real estate"
tends to arrive through a Golden Visa or a retirement question rather than through a
holiday.

Neither is more valid, and a site covering only one is covering roughly half the
market. Pages on this site use both vocabularies deliberately rather than picking a
house style.

## What Greece costs, by where you look

| Where | Asking price per m² | Reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Corfu, Kassiopi | about €4,698 | July 2026 |
| Aegean Islands, highest region in Greece | about €3,626 | June 2026 |
| Corfu Prefecture, lowest municipality | about €2,932 | June 2026 |
| Kefalonia, Leivatho | about €2,987 | June 2026 |
| Kefalonia, island average | under €2,400 | June 2026 |
| Kefalonia, Omala | about €1,334 | June 2026 |

The range across those rows is roughly three and a half times. That is the single
most important fact about buying in Greece and it is why "average Greek property
price" is not a useful number. A €400,000 budget is a modest apartment in one of
these places and a substantial house with land in another.

## What the market actually did

On provisional Bank of Greece data for the first quarter of 2026, Greek apartment
prices rose 5.7 percent year on year.

| Segment | Year-on-year change, Q1 2026 |
|---|---:|
| Athens | 5.2% |
| Thessaloniki | 6.4% |
| Other cities | 5.4% |
| Areas outside all of the above | 6.9% |
| **National** | **5.7%** |

The pattern is the interesting part. The fastest-rising segment is not the capital,
it is everywhere else, which is the reverse of the story most coverage of the Greek
market still tells. Regional and island markets are moving faster than Athens.

Two readings of that are both reasonable, and honesty requires giving both. It can
mean the regions are catching up after a cycle in which Athens absorbed the
attention and the capital. It can also mean that thin markets move further on fewer
transactions, so a smaller number of sales shifts the index more. The data does not
choose between them.

For volumes rather than prices, the
[transactions guide](/guides/greece-property-market-transactions-2025/) covers where
the buying has actually been concentrated.

## Which region fits which buyer

Price is the second question. What the property is for is the first.

| Region | What it offers | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| Athens and the Riviera | Year-round services, schools, hospitals, every government office | Highest urban prices, city living |
| Crete | Island life with two proper cities behind it, runs through winter | Fewer international flights outside season |
| Ionian, Corfu and Kefalonia | Green, longer shoulder season, houses with land | Sharp seasonal contrast, thinner resale market |
| Cyclades | The image everyone has of Greece, strongest rental rates | Highest prices per m², shortest season |
| Peloponnese and the mainland | Space, lowest prices, drivable from Athens | Least international buyer depth |

The mistake this table exists to prevent is choosing on scenery in August. Every one
of these regions photographs well in summer, and they differ enormously in February,
which is when a second home either works or does not.

## What the market data does not tell you

Three things matter to a buyer and appear in no index.

**Liquidity varies enormously by region.** Athens and prime Crete have buyer pools
deep enough that a correctly priced property sells within a normal timeframe. Small
islands and inland mainland areas have far fewer buyers, which lengthens both the
purchase and, more importantly, the eventual resale. The price index measures what
sold. It does not measure how long the seller waited.

**Stock quality is uneven and the averages hide it.** A large share of Greek
residential stock is older, and a meaningful part of it carries some form of
unpermitted alteration. Two properties at the same price per square metre can be
separated by a full renovation and by whether the engineer can certify the building
at all. Any comparison of price alone is comparing something the buyer does not
actually purchase.

**Season shapes everything outside the cities.** In the islands and the coastal
mainland, letting demand, contractor availability, service levels and even the
ability to show a property to a buyer all move with the calendar. That does not
appear in a price series and it decides how well a second home works.

None of that argues against buying. It argues for reading a price figure as one
input among several rather than as the answer, and for seeing a place outside August
before committing to it.

## Where the yields are, and where they are not

Price is half of an investment case. The other half is what the asset earns, and the
spread there is narrower than the price spread but points in a different direction.

| Market | Average gross rental yield |
|---|---:|
| Athens, city-wide | 5.43% |
| Greece, national average | 4.40% |
| Kavala, weakest of the major cities in the reference set | 3.47% |

The pattern is the reverse of the price table. Athens is among the more expensive
places to buy and among the better places to earn, because the demand behind the
rent is year-round employment rather than eight weeks of holiday. Island yields look
attractive on a peak-season nightly rate and thin considerably once the empty months
are averaged in.

Two adjustments belong on any Greek yield figure before it is used. Gross is not net:
the [rental income scale](/guides/greece-rental-income-tax-non-resident/) runs to
45 percent from 2026 with only a flat 5 percent deduction against gross rent, so the
tax falls close to the gross number. And a Golden Visa qualifying property may only
be let on leases of twelve months or longer, which removes the seasonal model
entirely from that part of the market.

## What foreign buyers are actually buying

Two figures describe the shape of foreign demand better than any survey.

**Resale dominates.** Around 78 percent of international buyers in Greece target
resale stock rather than new build. That is not a preference for character; it is
where the supply is, particularly outside Athens, and it is why the legality and
title questions matter as much as they do here.

**Investment flows fell while prices rose.** Foreign direct investment into Greek
real estate fell 25.3 percent in 2025 against the prior period, in the same window
that the price index kept climbing. Those two facts sit together uncomfortably and
the honest reading is that fewer, larger or more selective transactions can coexist
with rising recorded prices. Anyone presenting the price rise as evidence of
broadening foreign demand is showing you one number and not the other.

## Where the Golden Visa tiers sit on the map

For buyers whose purchase is also a residence application, the price geography above
is overlaid by a second map that does not follow it.

| Tier | Where |
|---|---|
| €800,000 | Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, islands above the population threshold |
| €400,000 | The rest of Greece, including most of Crete, the Ionian and the Peloponnese |
| €250,000 | Conversion from another use, or restoration of a listed building |

The overlay produces the awkward cases. A modest property in a prime-zone suburb may
need €800,000 to qualify while a far larger house on Kefalonia qualifies at €400,000.
The tier follows the cadastral parcel rather than the postal address or the marketing
name of the area, so it is checked against the land registry entry before an offer.
The [tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones
and the 120 square metre area rule that comes with the two higher tiers.
