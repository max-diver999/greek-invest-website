**Quick answer:** The investment rules did not change in 2026. Law 5275/2026, published on
6 February 2026, rewrote the immigration code for employment, studies and family
reunification, and two independent Greek law firm summaries of it do not list investment
permits among the categories it covers. What did change is the flow: new investor
applications fell about 44% year on year in the first half of 2026, to 2,551 from 4,553,
while permits granted rose about 21%, to 4,919 from 4,057. The pending queue stood at
33,051 files in May.

## The law that did not change the Golden Visa

This is the correction worth making first, because acting on the wrong version of it costs
real money.

Law 5275/2026 was published in Government Gazette FEK A' 17 on 6 February 2026. It is a
substantial piece of legislation that rewrites the Greek immigration code, transposes an EU
directive, and reorganises residence permits into clearer categories: employment,
highly qualified work, studies, family reunification, humanitarian permits, tech and talent
visas. It lengthens the EU Blue Card from two years to three. It lets graduates stay a
further year to look for work or start a business.

It does not govern the investor residence permit.

Two independent Greek law firm summaries set out the permit categories the law covers, and
investment and real-estate permits are not among them. One of them states directly that the
alert does not address investor residence permits, and that the Elevate Greece startup
provision it mentions is an employment route for foreign nationals joining Greek startups
rather than an investment route.

That distinction matters because a good deal of 2026 coverage collapsed it. Articles
titled "Greece Golden Visa 2026 changes" attributed to the new law a €250,000 startup
investment pathway into the Golden Visa. As far as the law firm summaries go, no such
pathway was created: the startup provision is about hiring, not investing.

**What this means practically.** If you are planning a purchase, the rules you must meet are
still those of Law 5100/2024, activated by Circular 1/2026. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the thresholds and
the [circular explainer](/guides/greece-golden-visa-circular-2026-explained/) covers the
documentation. Nothing in the February law displaces either.

## Applications fell 44%, approvals rose 21%

| Measure | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New investor applications | 4,553 | 2,551 | about -44% |
| Investor permits granted | 4,057 | 4,919 | about +21% |

Source: Greek Ministry of Migration and Asylum, Legal Migration monthly reports.

Those two lines move in opposite directions, and the combination is the actual story of the
year. Fewer people are joining the programme while more people are getting through it.

The fall is not a 2026 event on its own. Initial permanent-investor applications ran to
9,382 in 2024 and 7,025 in 2025, itself a decline of 25.1%. Through the first five months
of 2026 the count was 2,137, an average near 427 a month against roughly 585 a month across
2025 and 782 across 2024.

The straightforward reading is that Law 5100/2024 pulled demand forward. Buyers who wanted
the old thresholds moved before they closed, 2024 absorbed that rush, and what has followed
is the level of genuine demand at the new prices rather than a collapse in interest. A
programme that raised its entry price should file fewer applications afterwards. This one
does.

## The queue, and the part of it nobody counts

| Pending files | March 2026 | May 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Permanent-investor | 10,032 | 9,210 |
| Family members | 25,637 | 23,841 |
| **Total** | **35,669** | **33,051** |

Two things stand out.

**The family queue is more than twice the investor queue.** 23,841 family files against
9,210 investor files. Every headline about "33,000 pending Golden Visa applications"
describes a queue in which most of the files belong to spouses, children and dependent
parents rather than to investors. If you are planning a family application, that is the
line you are actually standing in, and it is the longer one.

**The decisions do not fully explain the fall.** The queue dropped 2,618 files between the
March and May reports. Decisions issued across April and May totalled 1,541. New
applications over those two months, at the 2026 run rate, would have added roughly 850. Put
those together and you account for under 700 files of a 2,618 fall.

The published reports do not explain the remaining gap, and this page will not guess at it.
Withdrawals, rejections, consolidation of duplicate files and reclassification are all
ordinary possibilities. The honest position is that the queue is falling faster than the
decision counts alone would produce, and the reason is not in the public data.

## Monthly decisions are falling, not rising

| Month 2026 | Initial permits | Renewals | Total decisions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| March | 1,239 | 207 | 1,446 |
| April | 755 | 127 | 882 |
| May | 599 | 60 | 659 |

This sits awkwardly beside the half-year total, and both numbers are correct. Permits
granted across the first half of 2026 were up about 21% on the year before, and the monthly
run rate inside that half fell by more than half between March and May.

A great deal of 2026 commentary describes Greek processing as speeding up. On the half-year
comparison it did. On the trend through spring it did not. If you are estimating how long
your own file will take, the [timeline guide](/guides/golden-visa-greece-timeline-2026-realistic/)
works from what applicants actually report, and neither of these aggregates substitutes for
that.

## The 2024 rush is still working through the system

The application counts describe a wave, and the wave has a tail worth thinking about.

Roughly 9,382 initial investor applications were filed in 2024, against 7,025 in 2025 and a
2026 run rate near 427 a month. A large share of the 2024 figure was people moving before
the higher thresholds closed. Those buyers bought under time pressure, in a market that knew
they were under time pressure, and they bought whatever cleared the old threshold rather
than whatever suited them.

Two consequences follow, and neither is in the monthly reports.

**A concentrated cohort reaches its five-year mark together.** A Golden Visa property must be
held while the permit is held. Buyers who completed through 2024 arrive at 2029 as a group,
and whatever share of them wants to sell will want to sell into the same window. Whether
that matters depends entirely on the asset: a well-chosen apartment in a deep urban market
absorbs it, and a rushed purchase on a thin island market may not.

**The rushed cohort bought at the top of a hurried market.** Sellers price to the deadline
when a deadline exists. Anyone who bought in the last months before a threshold change
should assume they paid a premium for the timing and underwrite their exit accordingly,
rather than assuming the purchase price is a floor.

If you are buying now, the position is the opposite one and it is the better one. There is
no deadline in front of you, the queue ahead of you is shrinking, and you can take the time
to run the [due diligence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) properly.

## How to read the Ministry's own figures

Every number on this page comes from the Greek Ministry of Migration and Asylum's monthly
Legal Migration reports. They are public, and reading them directly beats reading coverage
of them. Four things to know before you do.

**Applications and decisions are different tables.** A month's decision count has no fixed
relationship to that month's applications, because the files being decided were filed months
or years earlier. Comparing the two within one month produces a meaningless ratio, and a
surprising amount of commentary does exactly that.

**Investor and family files are counted separately.** Always check which one a figure
describes. The combined number is more than twice the investor number, so a claim built on
the combined figure will overstate the investor queue by a wide margin.

**Initial permits and renewals are separate lines.** A renewal is an existing holder, not a
new entrant. Totals that merge them overstate how many people are joining the programme.

**Rolling totals get revised.** Files move between categories and late registrations appear,
so a figure for a given month can differ between one report and a later one. Cite the report
date alongside the number, which is what the tables above do.

The [statistics guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-statistics-2026/) works through the longer
series.

## What has not changed

Worth stating plainly, because the volume of "2026 changes" coverage implies otherwise.

The two property thresholds stand: the higher tier in Attica, the regional units of
Thessaloniki, Mykonos and Santorini, and islands above 3,100 inhabitants; the lower tier
everywhere else. The reduced tier for conversion and restoration projects still exists on
its own conditions.

The 120 square metre main usable area requirement stands, and it is still measured on the
engineer's certified figure rather than on a listing's floor area.

The ban on short-term letting the qualifying property stands. A Golden Visa property may be
let on a lease of twelve months or more, or used personally, and not placed on a holiday
rental platform. The [letting rules guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-no-short-term-rental/)
covers the compliance detail.

Nothing about the fall in applications relaxes any of these. A quieter queue is not a
softer rulebook.

## What to watch through the rest of 2026

**Whether the monthly decision rate recovers.** March to May fell steeply. Three months is
not a trend, and the next reports will say whether it was seasonal or structural.

**Whether the family backlog moves.** It is the larger queue and the one that determines
when a family is actually settled rather than when the investor is approved.

**Whether the application fall levels off.** Two full years of decline, 25.1% then a steeper
first half, at some point reach the level the new thresholds support. Where that floor sits
tells you what the programme looks like from here.

**Not another law.** Law 5275/2026 was the immigration reform of the year and it left
investor permits alone. Treat any claim that the Golden Visa rules changed as something to
check against the gazette reference before acting on it.

## Risks and red flags in the current coverage

**Articles attributing a startup investment route to the Golden Visa.** The Elevate Greece
provision in Law 5275/2026 is an employment route. Investing €250,000 in a registered
startup is not, on these sources, a Golden Visa pathway.

**"Processing has sped up" without a period attached.** True on the half-year comparison,
false on the March to May trend. Ask which one is meant.

**Backlog headlines that merge investors and families.** The 33,051 figure is mostly
dependants. Investor files alone are 9,210, and if you are applying without dependants that
smaller number is the only one describing your queue.

**Percentage falls quoted without their base.** A 44% drop reads as a collapse and a 25.1%
drop reads as a slide, but both describe a programme still receiving thousands of
applications a year. The direction is real; the language around it is usually heavier than
the numbers support.

**Any yield or return figure attached to a Golden Visa purchase.** Unrelated to this news,
but it travels with it: the qualifying property must be 120 square metres, and the headline
Greek rental yields describe one-bedroom flats. The
[rental yield guide](/guides/greece-rental-yield-guide/) sets out the size effect.
