**Quick answer:** Kefalonia asks a little under €2,400 per square metre island-wide
on June 2026 listing data, with a wide internal spread from roughly €1,334 in Omala
to roughly €2,987 in Leivatho. That places it well below Corfu and below the Aegean
Islands region. The island falls in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier, and a qualifying
property may only be let on leases of twelve months or longer, so the permit and the
holiday-letting model cannot both apply to the same house.

Every page ranking for this search is a listing grid. Four of the ten are
single-office local agencies and four are portals showing the same island template
they use everywhere. Not one of them tells you what the island costs, how that
compares to anywhere else, or what the difference is between the number a seller
asks and the number a market records.

That is what this page does.

## What Kefalonia costs, and where each number comes from

Two kinds of number circulate about Greek property and they are routinely mixed. It
is worth separating them before quoting either.

| Number type | What it measures | Source used here |
|---|---|---|
| Asking price per m² | What sellers are currently asking in live listings | Indomio, Greek property portal, June 2026. Commercial source, not official statistics |
| Price index change | What recorded transactions actually did | Bank of Greece, Q1 2026 release, provisional |

An asking price is an opening position. A price index is an outcome. On a thin
island market the gap between them can be wide, and anyone quoting a single "average
price" without saying which one it is has flattened that distinction away.

With that stated, here is the island.

| Area | Asking price per m² |
|---|---:|
| Leivatho | about €2,987 |
| Island average | a little under €2,400 |
| Omala | about €1,334 |

The internal spread is the headline. Between the top and bottom areas the asking
price roughly doubles, which means the same budget buys either a compact place near
the better-connected south or roughly twice the floor area inland. On an island of
this size that is not a long drive apart, and the decision is about access,
services and letting demand rather than about distance.

## Kefalonia against the alternatives

The useful comparison is not Kefalonia against Greece. It is Kefalonia against the
islands a buyer is actually choosing between.

| Where | Asking price per m² | Reference |
|---|---:|---|
| Corfu, Kassiopi | about €4,698 | July 2026 |
| Aegean Islands region, highest regional average | about €3,626 | June 2026 |
| Corfu Prefecture, lowest municipality | about €2,932 | June 2026 |
| **Kefalonia, island average** | **under €2,400** | June 2026 |
| Kefalonia, Omala | about €1,334 | June 2026 |

Kefalonia comes in below every one of those reference points. The reason is not
quality, it is market maturity: Corfu has carried an international buying market for
decades, with the airlift and the foreign-owned stock that follow, and the price
reflects the depth of that demand rather than the quality of the coastline.

What that means practically is that Kefalonia offers Ionian geography at a price
closer to the mainland, with a shallower resale market as the trade. Both halves of
that sentence matter.

## What the transaction index says the market is doing

Asking prices tell you what sellers want. The Bank of Greece index tells you what
happened.

On provisional data for the first quarter of 2026, Greek apartment prices rose
5.7 percent year on year. The breakdown matters more than the headline: Athens rose
5.2 percent, Thessaloniki 6.4 percent, other cities 5.4 percent, and areas outside
all of those 6.9 percent. The fastest-rising segment in the country was the one
Kefalonia belongs to.

Two readings of that are both defensible. It can mean regional and island markets
are catching up after a cycle in which Athens absorbed most of the attention. It can
also mean thin markets move further on fewer transactions. The index does not
distinguish between them, and anyone telling you which it is with confidence is
telling you an opinion.

For the longer view on Greek transaction volumes rather than prices, the
[market transactions guide](/guides/greece-property-market-transactions-2025/)
covers where the buying has actually been happening.

## Where on the island, and what each part is for

The price table above is the arithmetic. The geography behind it is what actually
decides where people buy, and Kefalonia divides into four fairly distinct markets.

**The south and the Argostoli side.** The capital, the airport, the hospital and most
of the year-round population. This is where the island functions in February as well
as August, and it is where the Leivatho end of the price range sits. Anyone intending
to spend real time here, rather than four weeks a year, tends to end up in this half
for reasons that have nothing to do with the view.

**Lassi and the resort belt.** The established letting market, closest to Argostoli
and to the airport, with the shortest transfer time and the deepest rental demand.
Prices reflect that. It is also the part of the island where a property is easiest to
let and easiest to resell, because the buyer pool is largest.

**The north, around Fiskardo and Assos.** The postcard Kefalonia, stone villages and
deep water, and the part most people picture. It is also the furthest from the
airport and the hospital, the most seasonal, and the part where winter is quietest.
Properties here command a premium in summer photography and demand the most honesty
about February.

**Lixouri and the Paliki peninsula, and the inland valleys.** Where the €1,334 end of
the range lives. Cheaper because it is further from the services and the letting
demand, not because the land is worse. For a buyer who wants space and does not need
a rental yield, this is where a budget stretches furthest, and it is the part of the
island most often overlooked by people searching from abroad.

The practical consequence is that "how much does Kefalonia cost" has four answers,
and choosing between them is a decision about access and season rather than about
price. A buyer who needs the hospital and the airport nearby is choosing the south
whatever the price table says. A buyer who wants a summer house and does not mind a
drive is choosing the north or the west, and their money goes considerably further.

## Why the Ionian buyer is a different buyer

Kefalonia is green, wetter than the Cyclades, and mountainous, with a season that
extends further into the shoulders than the Aegean islands manage. That produces a
different buyer and a different property.

The Cycladic purchase is typically compact, white, expensive per square metre and
bought partly for the look of it. The Ionian purchase is more often a house with
land, bought by a northern European family who will use it for long stretches. Stone
rather than render, olive trees rather than a caldera view, and a price per square
metre roughly half of what the Aegean asks at the top.

That difference also shows up in the letting calendar. A longer shoulder season is
worth more to an owner than a higher peak rate, because it spreads occupancy rather
than concentrating it into eight weeks.

## What a budget actually buys here

Translating a price per square metre into a house is where the abstraction becomes
useful. At the island average, a €400,000 budget corresponds to something in the
region of 165 square metres of built area before costs, which on Kefalonia is a
substantial house rather than an apartment. At the Omala end of the range the same
budget corresponds to roughly twice that, and at the Leivatho end to appreciably
less.

Two adjustments belong on top of that arithmetic. Acquisition costs of 7 to 10
percent come off the buying power before anything else. And land, outbuildings and
condition move a real property well away from any per-square-metre average, in both
directions: a stone house needing full renovation and a finished villa with a pool
do not sit on the same line however similar their floor areas look.

## The winter question

This is the part an August viewing trip cannot show you, and it decides how well
people get on with the island.

Ferry timetables thin out. A share of restaurants and services close for the season.
The pool of available builders, electricians and plumbers shrinks, which lengthens
and sometimes prices up any work you commission between November and March. And a
property is harder to show to a buyer in winter, which matters at resale.

None of that is a reason not to buy on Kefalonia. It is a reason to see it in
February first, and to hear how the answer to "what is it like here in winter"
changes depending on who you ask.

## The Golden Visa position

Kefalonia sits in the €400,000 tier rather than the €800,000 prime zone. A single
residential property at or above €400,000, on one title deed, with at least
120 square metres of main usable area certified by a Greek engineer, qualifies. The
[tier guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) sets out the zones, and
the tier follows the cadastral parcel rather than the postal address.

The restriction matters more here than in most places. A qualifying property may only
be let on leases of twelve months or longer, and short-term tourist letting is
prohibited for the life of the permit. On a seasonal island where the letting model
is a large part of why people buy, that means the permit case and the holiday-income
case cannot both be run on the same house. Decide which one the purchase is for
before you shortlist, not after.

## What to check on this island specifically

The general [due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) applies
everywhere. Four things deserve extra attention on Kefalonia.

**Building legality on older stone stock.** Extensions, terrace enclosures and
converted outbuildings are common and are exactly what the engineer's legality
certificate will not cover if they were built without a permit.

**Inheritance chains.** Island properties frequently sit in undivided family
ownership across several heirs, some of whom live abroad. Every one of them signs.

**Access and services.** Whether the road is adopted, where the water comes from,
and whether mains drainage reaches the plot. Inland plots at the cheaper end of the
price range are cheaper partly for these reasons.

**Seasonal contractor availability.** If the purchase needs work, the timetable is
set by when trades are on the island rather than by the scope.

On costs, the transfer tax on a resale is an effective 3.09 percent of the taxable
value, the [full stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/) runs 7 to 10
percent of the price, and the
[transfer tax guide](/guides/greece-property-transfer-tax-fma/) explains why the
taxable value can exceed the price you agreed. If you intend to let, the
[rental income scale](/guides/greece-rental-income-tax-non-resident/) changed on
1 January 2026 and now runs to 45 percent with only a flat 5 percent deduction.
