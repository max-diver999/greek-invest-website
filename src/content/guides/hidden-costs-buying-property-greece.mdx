**Quick answer:** The known costs of buying property in Greece, FMA transfer tax, notary, lawyer, land registry, total 7 to 10% above the purchase price. But most buyers hit an additional 2 to 4% in costs they did not plan for: VAT on agent fees, engineer survey overruns, power of attorney paperwork, inherited ENFIA arrears, cadastre checks, and first-year ownership costs. On a €400,000 property, those extras can exceed €12,000.

The [complete cost of buying property in Greece](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-greece/) covers FMA transfer tax at 3.09%, notary fees, land registry charges, and the standard agent commission. This guide goes one level deeper, into the costs that do not appear in standard fee tables but that experienced buyers, lawyers, and agents see on almost every transaction.

## Why the standard "7 to 10%" figure understates real costs

Every reputable guide to Greek property quotes the 7 to 10% acquisition rule, and it is accurate as far as
it goes. It captures FMA transfer tax at 3.09%, notary fees at 0.8% to 1.2%, lawyer fees at 1% to 1.5%,
land registry at 0.475% to 0.65%, and agent commission at 2% to 2.5%.

What it misses is a second layer, and the reason it misses it is structural rather than careless. Every
item in that list is a percentage of the price, so the rule can express all of them as one percentage. The
second layer is not: some of it is charged on a different base, some is triggered by who you are rather
than by what you buy, and some does not arrive until months after the keys do. A single percentage cannot
carry any of that, so the convention drops it.

Buyers who budget precisely 8% above the asking price arrive at completion 2 to 3% short. On a €500,000
property, 2% is €10,000, which is enough to create genuine financing stress at exactly the moment when
options are narrow. On a €400,000 purchase the full stack reaches €36,768, or 9.19%. A non-EU buyer
purchasing remotely in a border zone reaches 13% to 15%.

The categories below are not edge cases. Most of them appear on most Greek transactions, and they are
grouped here by when they hit your account rather than numbered arbitrarily, because cash-flow timing is
what actually catches people out.

## The 24% that rides on top of every professional fee

Greek VAT at 24% applies to professional services, and agent commission is a professional service. This
is the single largest omission in most budgets, because the commission itself is always quoted and the VAT
on it rarely is.

| Purchase price | Commission rate | Base commission | VAT at 24% | Real cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| €250,000 | 2% | €5,000 | €1,200 | €6,200 |
| €400,000 | 2% | €8,000 | €1,920 | €9,920 |
| €800,000 | 2.5% | €20,000 | €4,800 | €24,800 |

The same 24% applies to your lawyer, your notary and your engineer. When a firm quotes "1.2% notary", ask
whether that is before or after VAT: the difference on an €800,000 deed is over €2,000 on that line alone.
A quote that is silent on VAT is a quote that will grow.

## Costs the property itself triggers

An engineer's survey runs €300 to €800 for an apartment and €600 to €1,200 for a house or villa. That is
the quoted fee. The variable that matters sits behind it: older stock, and island stock built before 1983
in particular, frequently carries unauthorised additions — a room built without permit, a veranda
extension, a converted basement. Those must be regularised under a legalisation law or demolished before a
transfer can proceed.

Regularisation fines (πρόστιμα τακτοποίησης) scale with the square metres involved and the planning zone.
A 20 m² unauthorised veranda in a coastal zone runs €2,000 to €4,000 in fines plus the engineer's time to
process the file. Sellers usually settle this themselves once it is found, so the money is often not
yours — but the delay is, and four to eight weeks is the minimum. If you are paying interest on bridge
finance, that delay has a price even when the fine does not. The
[engineer certificate guide](/guides/greece-property-engineer-certificate/) covers what the survey
actually inspects.

The title work sits alongside it. A standard cadastre search costs €50 to €150; the twenty-year ownership
history that proper due diligence requires costs €200 to €500 in lawyer time. Where the cadastre turns up
boundary disputes, pending proceedings or incomplete registration, resolution runs from several hundred to
several thousand euros and adds weeks. The
[due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/) walks the full sequence.

## Costs your own passport and location trigger

This group is invisible to anyone comparing headline percentages, because it depends entirely on the
buyer rather than the building.

Buying remotely means a power of attorney, and a POA is four separate charges: foreign notarisation at
€50 to €200 depending on the country, an apostille fee at €30 to €100, sworn translation in Greece at €150
to €300, and courier at €40 to €80, because the original has to be in Greece before the deed date. Call it
€300 to €700 per POA, higher outside the Hague Convention where consular legalisation is added. If the
transaction outruns the POA's validity, the whole cost repeats.

Documents follow the same pattern. Sworn translation runs €40 to €80 per page, and a typical buyer pack —
passport, marriage certificate, two financial statements — is 15 to 25 pages, so €600 to €1,500 before
anything unusual. AFM registration is often inside a lawyer's retainer; quoted separately it is €100 to
€300, and a Greek bank account adds €50 to €150 in opening fees. The
[AFM and bank account guide](/guides/greece-afm-tax-number-property/) sets out the document sequence.

Then there is geography. Under Law 1892/1990 a non-EU national buying in a designated border zone —
which includes the Dodecanese, so Rhodes and Kos, along with much of the northern frontier and several
islands — needs clearance from the Ministry of National Defence before the purchase can complete. EU
nationals are exempt entirely. The application itself is not the expense; the timeline is. It runs on its
own committee schedule, adds months rather than weeks, and it has to be coordinated against the Golden
Visa file if you are running one, because the two processes have separate clocks and neither waits for the
other. Budget legal time for that coordination rather than a fee, and see the
[Golden Visa lawyer cost guide](/guides/greece-golden-visa-lawyer-cost/) for how non-EU complexity moves
the retainer.

## Costs that arrive after the keys do

The deed is not the end of the spending, and this group is the one buyers genuinely do not see coming
because it lands when the transaction feels finished.

Registering the transfer carries a mutation fee — the administrative charge for recording the change of
ownership — separate from the 0.475% to 0.65% land registry line inside the standard stack. It runs €80 to
€200 for a straightforward apartment and more for plots, multiple parties, or anything carrying mortgages,
servitudes or caveats. It should be itemised; if it is buried in a fee line, ask for the breakdown.

Utilities transfer at €50 to €150 per connection in administrative fees plus any provider deposit, and a
property that has stood vacant needs a connection inspection at €100 to €250 per utility before supply is
restored. Individually trivial, collectively €300 to €700.

Insurance is not legally compulsory on a cash purchase, which is precisely why it gets left out of
budgets, and a mortgage lender will require it regardless. Expect €200 to €600 a year for a standard
apartment and more for a villa or anything with a pool. In an active seismic zone the line that matters is
earthquake cover, and it is excluded from the cheapest policies — check it explicitly rather than
assuming.

ENFIA is the timing trap. It is assessed as of 1 January, so completing in August makes you liable for the
following year's bill, arriving the January after you move in, when the purchase budget is closed. On a
€400,000 property in a sought-after coastal zone that is €800 to €2,000. Separately, where your total
Greek holdings exceed €300,000 in objective value, supplementary ENFIA applies to the excess and activates
immediately on the purchase that crosses the threshold. The
[ENFIA guide](/guides/enfia-property-tax-greece/) covers both components and the payment schedule.

## The Practical Rule
The standard 7 to 10% figure is a useful starting point, not a complete budget. For any Greek property purchase, build your budget from the bottom up: itemise every cost category against your specific buyer profile (EU or non-EU, in-person or remote, border zone or not, with or without Golden Visa), then add a 2% contingency for overruns in the engineer survey, translation, and administrative categories. Buyers who do this arrive at completion with money to spare; buyers who rely on the headline figure frequently do not.

## Unveiling the Hidden Costs on a €400,000 Purchase

| Expense Category | Rate applied | Actual Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FMA property transfer tax | 3.09% of €400,000 | €12,360 |
| Estate agent commission | 2% + 24% VAT | €9,920 |
| Notary fee | 1.2% + 24% VAT | €5,952 |
| Lawyer fee | 1.0% + 24% VAT | €4,960 |
| Land registry / cadastre registration | 0.475% + 24% VAT | €2,356 |
| Engineer survey and certificate | €500 + 24% VAT | €620 |
| Power of attorney, apostille and sworn translation | Fixed | €400 |
| AFM registration, bank account opening, courier | Fixed | €200 |
| **Total payable above the purchase price** | **9.19%** | **€36,768** |

In this scenario, the buyer must prepare an additional **€36,768** to cover the transaction stack. A common pitfall is underestimating the estate agent commission or notary fees, which are often quoted net of the 24% Greek VAT. 

Additionally, if the buyer requires a mortgage, the bank will charge a mortgage registration fee (typically 1.2% of the loan value) and a mandatory property valuation fee (€300 to €500). Non-resident buyers must also factor in the cost of appointing a local tax representative and opening a Greek bank account, which carries minor annual maintenance and transfer fees.

## Transaction Cost Mitigation Checklist

1. **Objective Value vs Contract Price:** Verify the property's official "objective value" (αντικειμενική αξία). The 3.09% transfer tax is calculated on the higher of the contract price or the objective value. If the objective value is higher than your purchase price, your tax bill will be higher than expected.
2. **VAT vs Transfer Tax on New-Builds:** New-build properties in Greece are subject to a 24% VAT. However, the government has suspended this VAT through 2026, meaning new-builds currently pay the same 3.09% transfer tax as resales, saving buyers up to €80,000 on a €400,000 purchase.
3. **Independent Legal Representation:** Avoid using the developer's or agency's in-house lawyer. While they may offer a discount, an independent lawyer is critical to ensure that prior mortgages, tax liens, and planning violations are fully cleared before your funds are released.

## Buyer scenarios


The same 7% to 10% headline stack lands very differently on three buyers. The cash buyer of older regional stock pays in legalisation and condition; the Golden Visa buyer at a tier boundary pays through the objective value; and the remote buyer pays in apostilles, translations and the power of attorney. Only the third of those is predictable in advance.
Which hidden costs actually bite depends on what you are buying and how you are financing it.

**The cash buyer of an older regional apartment.** The exposure here is legalisation and condition rather than tax. Outstanding regularisation fines from earlier amnesty rounds transfer with the property, the building reserve may be underfunded, and an engineer survey frequently turns up unpermitted works that must be regularised before the Electronic Building Identity can be issued. Budget beyond the 7% to 10% headline stack.

**The Golden Visa buyer at the tier boundary.** The costs that hurt are the ones charged on the higher of contract price or objective value. If the objective value exceeds the agreed price, transfer tax rises accordingly, and a purchase modelled at exactly €400,000 plus 7% can breach the budget without the price changing at all. Get the objective-value calculation in writing before the preliminary agreement.

**The remote buyer completing by power of attorney.** Add apostille and certified translation costs for every document produced outside Greece, plus the notary's fee for the power of attorney itself. These are small individually and routinely omitted from the estimate a buyer is given, and they arrive at the least convenient point in the timetable.
