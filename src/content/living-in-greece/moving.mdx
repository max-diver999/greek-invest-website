**Quick answer:** moving to Greece as a non-EU national means obtaining a residence
permit, because visa-free presence is capped at 90 days in any rolling 180-day period
across the Schengen area. Choose the permit by income source: a pension points to the
financially independent person route, remote employment to the digital nomad route, a
qualifying property purchase to the Golden Visa. Settle the tax question first,
because crossing 183 days of presence generally makes you Greek tax resident and
brings worldwide income into scope. Arrival month therefore matters as much as
paperwork.

Most guides to moving here are written by someone selling the lorry, the visa or the
tax return, and each covers its own leg well. What none of them does is put the whole
move on one timeline. That matters because two separate clocks are running from the
day you land, they are counted differently, and the interaction between them decides
things that cannot be undone later.

## The two clocks

**The 90-day ceiling.** A non-EU national may be present in the Schengen area for
90 days in any rolling 180-day period, under Article 6 of the Schengen Borders Code.
It is area-wide, so days in Italy or Spain count against the same allowance. It is
rolling, so it cannot be reset by a weekend at home. And it is a ceiling on visiting,
not a status: you cannot arrange a life on it.

**The 183-day trigger.** Presence beyond 183 days in a calendar year generally makes
you Greek tax resident under Article 4 of Law 4172/2013, bringing worldwide income
into the Greek net unless a preferential regime or a treaty says otherwise.

| | 90-day ceiling | 183-day trigger |
|---|---|---|
| What it governs | Whether you may be present at all | Whether Greece taxes your worldwide income |
| Counted over | Rolling 180 days, Schengen-wide | Calendar year, Greece only |
| Reset by | Time, not travel | 1 January |
| Consequence of crossing it | Overstay, with entry consequences | Greek tax residence, for that whole year |

They pull in opposite directions. The 90-day ceiling pushes you to lodge a permit
application early, while the 183-day trigger may make an early arrival expensive.
Someone moving in September with a large one-off gain landing in December has a real
decision to make, and it is available only before the move.

## The move in four phases

**Phase one, before you go.** Decide the permit route, take the tax advice, and start
the document chain. Foreign documents will need apostilles under the Hague Convention
and certified translations, and that chain runs three to four weeks when nothing goes
wrong. Obtain a Greek tax number, the AFM, which a lawyer can do under power of
attorney without you being present.

**Phase two, the first weeks.** Open a Greek bank account, which is the step that
most often sets the pace, and secure health insurance that meets the permit's cover
requirement rather than the one you already carry. Lodge the permit application while
you are lawfully present.

**Phase three, waiting.** Biometrics, then the card. Plan in months. Keep the 90-day
count in view if the application was lodged close to the limit, because the allowance
continues to run down.

**Phase four, settling.** Utility transfers, social security registration where the
route requires it, and the first Greek tax filing. None of these offices shares
information with the others, so expect to produce the same documents repeatedly.

## Which permit, decided by what funds you

| If your income is | The route | What it turns on |
|---|---|---|
| A foreign pension | Financially independent person | Regular proven pension income, health cover |
| Remote work for a non-Greek employer | Digital nomad | Contracts outside Greece, income floor |
| Savings and investments | Financially independent person | Demonstrable passive income, health cover |
| A qualifying property purchase | Golden Visa | The threshold for the location |
| A Greek employer | Work permit | Job offer and a quota position |

The Golden Visa is the route that gets the most attention and it is worth being clear
about what it is. Under Law 5100/2024 the qualifying investment is €800,000 in a
single residential property in the prime zone, €400,000 elsewhere, and €250,000 where
a building is converted from another use or restored as a listed property. The
application fee is €2,000 for the main applicant plus €150 for each adult family
member. It grants residence without requiring you to live in Greece, which is
excellent if you want optionality and beside the point if you want a home. The
[Golden Visa hub](/golden-visa/) covers the conditions in full.

## The tax decision that comes before the removal van

Two regimes change the arithmetic, and both are elected rather than granted.

**Article 5B, foreign pensioners.** A flat 7 percent on worldwide income for a fixed
period, for a foreign pensioner transferring tax residence to Greece. The scope is
what makes it unusual: foreign-source income generally, not the pension alone. The
[7 percent regime guide](/guides/greece-7-percent-pension-tax-retirees/) sets out the
eligibility test.

**Article 5A, non-domiciled.** A flat €100,000 a year on foreign-source income
whatever its size, with entry conditional on investing at least €500,000 in Greek
real estate, a business or securities. Cheaper than the ordinary scale only above a
high income, and worse below it. The
[non-dom guide](/guides/greece-non-dom-tax-regime-5a-guide/) shows where the crossover
falls.

If you will let a property here, note that the rental income scale changed on
1 January 2026 under Law 5246/2025: 15 percent on the first €12,000, 25 percent to
€24,000, 35 percent to €35,000, and 45 percent above, with only a flat 5 percent
deduction for repairs against gross rent.

## Moving from the UK

A British citizen has been a third-country national since the end of the transition
period, and the practical consequences are specific rather than general. Free movement
does not apply, the 90-day ceiling does, and a residence permit is required to live
here. Driving licence exchange, healthcare entitlement and pension arrangements each
run on their own rules rather than following automatically from the permit.

British citizens who were lawfully resident in Greece before the cut-off hold
Withdrawal Agreement rights, which is a materially better position than a new
applicant and worth confirming rather than assuming. The
[post-Brexit position for UK buyers](/guides/brexit-uk-buyers-greece-property/)
covers where the property side differs.

## Moving from the United States

The starting position is the same third-country basis, with two American-specific
complications worth naming early.

US citizens remain subject to US filing on worldwide income regardless of where they
live, so becoming Greek tax resident creates two filing obligations rather than
replacing one with the other. The treaty and the foreign tax credit mechanics decide
what is actually paid, and that is a question for a preparer who handles both sides.

Second, the compliance reporting that follows from holding foreign accounts starts
the moment the Greek bank account opens. The
[Golden Visa guide for US citizens](/guides/greece-golden-visa-us-citizens/) covers
the residence side.

## What actually delays a move

| What delays it | Why | What shortens it |
|---|---|---|
| Greek bank account | Non-resident compliance review, and it recurs | Start before you arrive, prepare source-of-funds evidence |
| Apostille and translation chain | Three to four weeks, and a single wrong detail restarts it | Have the Greek lawyer specify the exact wording first |
| Health insurance | The policy must meet the permit's cover requirement | Check the requirement before buying a policy |
| Appointment availability | Biometrics slots, not the decision | Lodge early in the 90-day window |
| Documents from a third country | Each jurisdiction has its own apostille route | Start the slowest one first |

Notice what is not on that list: the decision itself. Applications that meet the
criteria are granted. What costs people months is assembling a file that the office
will accept.

The practical consequence is that a move is a critical-path problem rather than a
volume-of-work problem. Almost every item above can be started in parallel, and
almost nobody does, because each one looks small until it turns out to gate the next.
Mapping the chain once, on paper, before anything is submitted, is worth more than
any single piece of advice about which permit to choose.

## The registrations nobody warns you about

The permit is one document. Living here needs several more, and each is issued by a
different office that will not act until the one before it exists.

| Registration | What it unlocks | What it needs first |
|---|---|---|
| AFM, the tax number | The permit file, a bank account, a tenancy, a purchase | Passport and proof of address |
| Bank account | Rent payments, utilities, the permit's proof of means | AFM, and documented source of funds |
| AMKA, the social security number | Public healthcare access, employment, some school enrolments | Residence permit or a qualifying status |
| Utility accounts in your name | Proof of address for everything afterwards | Tenancy or deed, plus the AFM |
| Driving licence exchange | Legal driving beyond the recognition period | Residence registration, and it varies by issuing country |
| School enrolment | Places for children | AMKA in most municipalities, plus apostilled school records |

Two of these catch people out reliably.

**AMKA is not automatic.** It follows the residence permit rather than arriving with
it, and public healthcare access follows the AMKA. Anyone assuming the permit itself
carries health cover is planning a gap, which is one reason the permit application
requires private insurance in the first place.

**The driving licence has a clock of its own.** A foreign licence is recognised for a
limited period after you become resident, and the exchange rules differ depending on
which country issued it. EU licences exchange straightforwardly. Licences from
outside the EU may require a test. This is worth checking in month one rather than
in month eleven, because the recognition period expires whether or not you noticed.

The pattern across all six rows is the same: nothing here is difficult, and
everything here is sequential. A move that runs late almost always ran late because
one document was started after the document that depended on it.

## Two movers, two sequences

**A retired UK couple with a private pension, moving in spring.** The financially
independent person route, with the 7 percent election checked before departure. A
spring arrival puts them over 183 days in the same calendar year, which is what they
want if the election is favourable. Renting for the first year lets them see an island
in February before buying.

**An American software engineer working remotely, moving in October.** The digital
nomad route. An October arrival keeps presence under 183 days in the arrival year, so
Greek tax residence begins the following January and the two tax positions are easier
to separate in year one. The Greek bank account is started from the US in August.

## After the move

The residence permit is a beginning rather than a conclusion, and the routes lead
different places. Time spent as a lawful resident counts toward long-term status and
eventually toward naturalisation, but the counting rules differ by route and by how
much of each year you actually spend in Greece. The
[path from Golden Visa to citizenship](/guides/greece-citizenship-after-golden-visa/)
sets out where that leads for the investment route, which is the one where the
distinction between holding a permit and living somewhere matters most.
