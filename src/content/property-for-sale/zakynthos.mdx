**Quick answer:** Zakynthos asks about €2,889 per square metre across the province on
October 2025 portal data, up 10.39% in a year, with Elatio at roughly €3,507 and
Artemisio at roughly €1,997. That sits above Kefalonia and below Corfu among the
Ionian islands. The island falls in the €400,000 Golden Visa tier. Two things decide
a purchase here that do not decide one elsewhere: which half of the island you are in,
and whether the plot sits inside the National Marine Park zoning around Laganas Bay.

## One island, two names, and why it costs you money

Zakynthos is the Greek name. Zante is the Venetian and English one. Both are in
current use, and the split runs straight through the property market.

Search demand divides between them almost evenly, and so do listings. Agents who
market to a British audience often use Zante; Greek portals and Greek agents use
Zakynthos. The consequence is that a buyer searching one spelling sees part of the
market and does not know what they are missing.

| If you search | You tend to reach |
|---|---|
| Zante | UK-facing agents and portals, holiday-let oriented stock |
| Zakynthos | Greek portals and local agents, wider stock including the interior |

The practical instruction is short: run both, every time, and treat any single search
as half a market. This is not a quirk of one island. It applies to Corfu and Kerkyra
too, and to Cephalonia and Kefalonia, but it is sharpest here because the English
name is the older and more commercially entrenched one.

## What the island asks, by municipality

|---|---:|---|
| Elatio | about €3,507 | Indomio, October 2025 |
| Zakynthos main town | about €2,982 | Indomio, May 2025 |
| Province average | about €2,889 | Indomio, October 2025 |
| Artemisio | about €1,997 | Indomio, October 2025 |

Every figure above comes from Indomio, a Greek property portal, and is an asking
price taken from live listings rather than a recorded transaction price. Asking prices
in the province rose 10.39% year on year to the October 2025 reference period, and the
main town carried a steeper rise still over its own window.

That source status is not a footnote. Portal averages describe what sellers want;
central bank indices describe what buyers paid. The
[market overview](/property-for-sale/) sets out why the two measures answer different
questions and when they diverge, and the Bank of Greece publishes the transaction
series quarterly for anyone who wants to check the other half.

Against the neighbours, Zakynthos sits in the middle of the Ionian. It is more
expensive than [Kefalonia](/property-for-sale/kefalonia/), which averages a little
under €2,400 island-wide, and cheaper than Corfu, where the Kassiopi area asks about
€4,698. The ranking follows tourism depth rather than landscape.

## The island splits in two, and the halves are different products

This is the single most useful thing to understand before shortlisting here.

**The south, around Laganas and the bay.** A developed, high-volume tourism economy
with the strongest letting demand on the island and the shortest season concentrated
into it. Property here is bought predominantly as a letting asset. It is also the half
where the marine park zoning applies.

**The main town and the eastern coast.** The year-round island. Services, the port,
the administrative functions, and a resident population that does not leave in
October. Prices near €2,982 per square metre reflect that this is where the island
actually works in February.

**The interior and the north.** Agricultural, quieter, and where the €1,997 end of the
range lives. Cheaper because it is further from the letting demand and the services,
not because the land is poor. For a buyer who wants space and does not need a yield,
the budget goes furthest here.

Someone who visits Laganas in August and buys in the interior, or vice versa, has
bought a different island from the one they came for. The drive between them is short
and the two markets behave nothing alike.

## The zoning question nobody puts in a listing

The National Marine Park covers Laganas Bay and associated areas, and exists to
protect loggerhead turtle nesting sites. Protected status brings planning, building
and use restrictions that vary by zone.

None of this appears on a property listing, and none of it is something an agent is
obliged to volunteer. What it means practically is that for any property in or near
the southern bay, the zoning position is a search to run before an offer, alongside
the ordinary title and legality checks. A restriction on building, on access or on how
a property may be used changes what you have bought.

This is not a reason to avoid the south of the island. It is a reason to establish the
position in writing rather than assume it, and it is the one Zakynthos-specific item
that belongs on top of the standard
[due diligence sequence](/guides/due-diligence-greece-property/). The
[cadastre check guide](/guides/greece-property-cadastre-check/) covers how to read the
registered parcel, which is where zoning questions start.

## Letting, and the restriction that catches Golden Visa buyers

Zakynthos has one of the more developed short-term letting markets in the Ionian,
which is exactly why the Golden Visa restriction bites hard here.

A qualifying property may only be let on leases of twelve months or longer, and
short-term tourist letting is prohibited for the life of the permit. On an island
where the letting model most foreign buyers have in mind is the summer holiday let,
that means the permit case and the income case cannot run on the same house. The
[short-term rental rules](/guides/short-term-rental-greece-rules/) cover the
registration requirements that apply to ordinary lets.

For an ordinary purchase with no permit attached, the letting question is commercial
rather than legal: a heavily seasonal market concentrates income into a short window,
and occupancy across the year matters more than the peak nightly rate.

## Access, and what it does to a property market

Two things decide how liquid an island property is: how many people can reach it
easily, and for how many months of the year.

Zakynthos has its own airport with direct seasonal charter and scheduled service from
northern Europe, and ferry links to the mainland at Kyllini. In summer that produces
one of the better-connected islands in the Ionian, which is a large part of why the
letting market developed as it did and why asking prices sit about 20% above
Kefalonia.

Out of season the picture narrows. International routes thin or stop, and the island
runs on domestic connections. For an owner this affects three separate things:
occupancy outside the peak, how quickly a property can be viewed by a buyer in
winter, and how long any works take once specialist trades have to come across.

The pattern to notice is that access and price move together across the Ionian.
Corfu has the most airlift and the highest prices, Zakynthos sits in the middle on
both, and Kefalonia has less of each. Where a buyer lands in that ranking should
follow how much they intend to use the property and how soon they might sell it,
rather than which coastline photographs best.

## What a budget actually buys here

Translating an asking price into a property makes the range concrete. At the province
average of about €2,889 per square metre, a €300,000 budget corresponds to roughly
104 square metres of built area before costs. In Artemisio at about €1,997 the same
budget corresponds to roughly 150 square metres, and in Elatio at about €3,507 to
roughly 86.

Two adjustments belong on top. Acquisition costs come off buying power before
anything else, and the [full cost stack](/guides/hidden-costs-buying-property-greece/)
sets out what they consist of. And land, condition and outbuildings move a real
property well away from any per-square-metre average in both directions: a stone
house needing rewiring and a finished villa with a pool do not sit on the same line
however similar their floor areas look.

The other thing worth pricing in is time. On a market where asking prices moved
10.39% in twelve months, the gap between viewing and completing is not neutral, and a
purchase that takes five months is being made in a different market from the one that
was viewed.

## Risks and red flags on this island

**Zoning in and around the marine park.** Establish it in writing before an offer.

**Building legality on older stock.** Extensions and enclosed terraces built without
permits are common across the Ionian and are what the engineer's certificate is for.
Without it a sale cannot complete.

**Undivided inheritances.** Island properties frequently sit in family ownership
across several heirs, and every one of them signs.

**Buying the wrong half.** An August viewing in the south does not describe the
interior, and a winter viewing in the town does not describe Laganas in July.

**Reading a 10.39% annual rise as a forecast.** It describes asking prices in a
twelve-month window on a thin market. It is not a prediction and it is not a
transaction figure. Sellers can raise asking prices faster than buyers agree to pay
them, and on a small island a handful of ambitious listings moves an average.

## Who the buyers here actually are

Understanding the buyer pool matters at resale more than at purchase, because it
decides who you will eventually be selling to.

Zakynthos has drawn British and northern European buyers for decades, concentrated
heavily on the letting side. That produces a deeper resale market than Kefalonia has,
and it also produces a more correlated one: when the letting economics change, a large
share of the buyer pool reprices at the same time. A market built on one use case
moves together.

The counterweight is the year-round island around the main town, where the buyer is a
resident or a long-stay owner rather than an investor, and where demand is less tied
to what happened to summer bookings. That segment is smaller and steadier.

For a buyer, the question that follows is not which segment is better but which one
they are actually joining. A house bought for summer letting in the south is priced
by, and will be sold into, the letting market. A house in the town is not. The two
behave differently in a soft year, and the difference shows up at exit rather than at
entry, which is when it is too late to choose.

## Buyer scenarios

**A UK buyer taking a €220,000 house in the interior.** Below the Golden Visa
threshold, so letting stays unrestricted. The controlling risks are title chain and
building legality rather than zoning. Budget acquisition costs on top and treat the
letting income as a bonus rather than as the case for the purchase.

**A buyer taking a €400,000 property in the south as a Golden Visa file.** The
€400,000 tier applies, the property must be a single dwelling on one deed with at
least 120 square metres of main usable area, and the letting restriction rules out
the summer model the location is best suited to. Check zoning before anything else.

**A buyer wanting year-round use rather than income.** The main town and the eastern
coast, at around €2,982 per square metre, where services run through the winter. Costs
more per square metre than the interior and delivers a materially different life.
