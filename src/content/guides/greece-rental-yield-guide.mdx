## Why Greece Rental Yield Numbers Mislead You


The gross yields quoted in listings and agency reports are annual rent divided by asking price, before a single Greek cost is subtracted. Three deductions do most of the damage, and together they typically turn a 6.5% gross yield into a 3.5 to 4.5% net one:
1. Greek rental income tax. Greece taxes rental income at progressive rates: 15% on the first €12,000 a year, 25% on €12,001 to €24,000, 35% on €24,001 to €35,000 and 45% above that, under the scale Law 5246/2025 introduced for 2026. Individuals may deduct a flat 5% of gross rent for repairs and maintenance in place of actual costs; nothing else is deductible under the standard regime. One exemption can remove the charge entirely: a three-year residential lease concluded by 31 December 2026 on a home of up to 120 m² that was vacant for three years or previously short-let pays no income tax on that rent for three years. See the [non-resident rental income tax guide](/guides/greece-rental-income-tax-non-resident/) for the conditions and the clawback. A €10,000/year rent stream is not €10,000 in your account.

2. ENFIA property tax. Greece's unified real estate tax (ENFIA) is charged annually on assessed (objective) property values, which are typically below market value. Rates range from approximately 0.1% to 0.8% depending on zone, floor, age and surface area. A €150,000 Athens apartment might carry an assessed value of €80,000 to €100,000 and an annual ENFIA bill of €80 to €800.

3. Operational costs. Management fees, OTA commissions (for STR), cleaning, maintenance, vacancy, insurance and accountancy all extract a further 25 to 40% of gross rent before tax.

The framework in the next section lets you model realistic net returns from any quoted gross yield.

## Greece Net Yield Framework: Full Expense Table


The framework is subtraction, not estimation: take gross rent, remove tax, ENFIA, management, vacancy, maintenance, insurance, accountancy and communal charges, then divide what is left by the all-in purchase price rather than the headline price. A long-term let in Greece loses roughly 30 to 40% of gross rent to those lines. A short-term let loses 45 to 60% once OTA commission, the closed season and the higher wear rate are counted.
Use this table as a starting model. Replace with your actual quoted rent, management contract and ENFIA assessment before making any purchase decision.

| Expense Category | LTR Rate | STR Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rental income tax | 15% / 25% / 35% / 45% by band | Same progressive scale | Flat 5% repairs deduction; nothing else offsets |
| ENFIA (property tax) | 0.10 to 0.8% of assessed value | Same | Assessed value usually below market; check catastro |
| Management / letting fee | 8 to 12% of gross rent | 18 to 25% incl. OTA commission | STR includes Airbnb/Booking.com commission |
| Vacancy allowance | 8 to 12% of annual rent | 20 to 35% (seasonal gap) | Crete STR season ~5 to 6 months usable |
| Maintenance & repairs | 1.0 to 1.5% of property value/year | 1.5 to 2.0% (higher wear from STR) | Budget higher for older stock |
| Buildings insurance | 0.15 to 0.25% of property value | 0.20 to 0.3% (STR premium) | STR policies cost more; not all standard policies cover commercial use |
| Accountant / tax filing | €500 to 1,000/year | €700 to 1,200/year | Non-resident filings typically at upper end |
| Strata / communal fees | €600 to 2,400/year | Same | Blocks and gated complexes; check before signing |

Crete STR delivers the best net yield at 4.40% because gross rent is high enough to absorb the heavier operating stack and still return meaningfully. Capital growth assumptions are not included, your total return picture changes significantly if Athens appreciates 5 to 8% per year (as it has 2022 to 2025).

## Why Net Yield Decays Faster Than Gross in Greece

Greek rental income tax is charged on rent, not on profit. Under the standard regime an individual
landlord sets a flat 5% repairs allowance against gross rent and nothing else — not the management
fee, not the insurance, not the accountant, not a euro of the maintenance actually spent. That single
design choice is why the gross-to-net gap here is wider than in markets that tax net profit, and why
it widens with time: as a building ages its costs rise and its tax bill does not fall.

A €150,000 Athens apartment let at 5.4% gross makes the arithmetic concrete.

| Line | Annual (€) |
|---|---|
| Gross rent, 5.4% of €150,000 | +8,100 |
| Rental income tax, 15% on gross less the 5% repairs allowance | −1,154 |
| ENFIA | −150 |
| Management, 10% of rent | −810 |
| Vacancy allowance, 10% | −810 |
| Maintenance reserve, 1% of value | −1,500 |
| Buildings insurance | −400 |
| Accountancy for a non-resident filing | −600 |
| **Left after deductions** | **+2,676** |
| **Net yield on purchase price** | **1.78%** |

A 5.4% headline becomes 1.78%. On better stock with tighter vacancy and no separate accountancy the
same property might reach 3.0 to 3.5% net, and that is the realistic top of the range rather than a
base case. Two habits follow from the table. Divide by the all-in purchase price, not the headline
price, or the tax and fees you paid to acquire the asset vanish from the return. And treat the
maintenance reserve as a cost you have already incurred rather than one you might avoid.

## Why No Greek Net Yield Can Be Guaranteed

Greek law does not permit a developer or an agent to guarantee a fixed net rental return, so any
marketing that promises one is describing a commercial structure rather than a legal entitlement. In
practice a "6% net guaranteed" offer is one of three things: a purchase price inflated to fund the
subsidy out of your own premium, a gross figure presented as a net one, or a rental pool that passes
risk to whoever buys in last.

The test is cheap. Ask for the assumptions in writing — the occupancy rate used, which fees are
excluded, how ENFIA is allocated, what tax rate is assumed. A guarantee that survives that request is
usually a guarantee that has stopped being one.

## The yield you are quoted is a one-bedroom yield

Almost every headline Greek yield figure describes a one-bedroom apartment. Almost every
Golden Visa property is a three-bedroom, because the permit requires 120 square metres of
main usable area. Those are not the same investment, and the gap between them is larger
than the gap between any two Greek cities.

| Where | Unit | Gross yield |
|---|---|---:|
| Patisia, Athens | 1-bedroom | 8.0% |
| Kipseli, Athens | 1-bedroom | 7.3% |
| Central Athens | 1-bedroom | 6.0% |
| Voulgari-Ntepo-Martiou, Thessaloniki | 1-bedroom | 6.0% |
| Kolonaki-Lykavittos, Athens | 3-bedroom | 3.8% |
| Toumpa, Thessaloniki | 3-bedroom | 3.2% |

Source: Global Property Guide neighbourhood series, November 2025.

Read the two Thessaloniki rows together. The same city, the same survey, the same month:
6.0% on a one-bedroom and 3.2% on a three-bedroom. The three-bedroom earns a little over
half. Athens shows the same shape, with Kolonaki's three-bedroom stock at 3.8% against
Patisia's one-bedroom at 8.0%.

The reason is simple and it is not going away. Rent per square metre falls as flats get
bigger, because the tenant pool for a large flat is smaller and its rent is set by family
budgets rather than by how many people can be fitted into a room. Purchase price per
square metre does not fall nearly as fast.

**What this means for a Golden Visa buyer.** The 120 square metre rule puts you in the
three-bedroom column by law. Any yield quoted to you from the one-bedroom column is a
return you are not permitted to earn on the qualifying property. When an agent quotes
Kipseli at 7.3% for a Golden Visa purchase, the number is real and the application of it
is not. [The Riviera versus centre comparison](/compare/athens-riviera-vs-athens-center-investment/)
works this through at the €800,000 threshold.

Underwrite from the size class you will actually own.

## Athens: Two Completely Different Rental Markets


Athens splits into a working-class long-let ring — Kypseli, Patisia, Peristeri, Nikaia — where €1,200 to €2,000/sqm entry prices produce 6.0 to 7.5% gross, and a coastal and prime market from Glyfada to Vouliagmeni where €3,500 to €8,000+/sqm produces 3.8% to 4.5% gross and 2.0 to 2.8% net. The city-wide average of 5.43% gross (GPG 2025) describes neither of them. Golden Visa buyers are pushed into the second market by Attica's €800,000 threshold, which is why the yield they end up with rarely resembles the yield they researched.
Athens is not a single market. The city hosts at least two structurally different yield environments, and conflating them is the most common investor mistake.

### Working-Class LTR: The Yield Engine

Neighbourhoods like Kypseli, Patisia, Peristeri, Galatsi, Nikaia and parts of Piraeus have been the engine of Athens rental yield for the past five years. Here is what drives the numbers:

Supply shortage. Athens housing stock has not grown meaningfully in over a decade. New construction is concentrated in premium zones. Working-class areas are under-served by new supply, which keeps vacancy low and pushes up rents relative to entry prices.

Price entry point. Apartments in Kypseli and Patisia can still be acquired for €1,200 to €2,000/sqm, meaning a 60 sqm property costs €72,000 to €120,000. Monthly rents for these properties run €600 to €900, producing gross yields of 6.0 to 7.5%.

Tenant stability. Long-term employment-based tenants dominate these areas. Turnover is lower than city-centre STR and vacancy loss is easier to model.

Gentrification trajectory. Kypseli in particular has gentrified substantially since 2018, with cafes, co-working spaces and creative businesses reshaping tenant demographics. Early buyers in 2019 to 2021 have seen capital growth of 35 to 60% in addition to yield.

The catch: property quality varies enormously. Unrenovated 1970s stock needs €15,000 to €40,000 of immediate capex, which must be factored into your effective entry cost and yield calculation.

### Athenian Riviera: Capital Growth, Not Yield

Glyfada, Vouliagmeni, Varkiza and the coastal strip running to Sounio are a different investment thesis. Entry prices per sqm are €3,500 to €8,000+. Gross rental yields run 3.8% to 4.5%. Net yields land around 2.0 to 2.8%.

Why do buyers accept these returns? The Riviera investment case is capital appreciation, not income yield. The area attracts high-net-worth Greeks and international buyers who rent long-term and pay premium rates, but property value growth (higher gross revenue annually in peak years) is the primary driver of total return.

If your strategy is income yield, the Riviera is not optimised for you. If your strategy is total return (capital growth plus yield), the Riviera competes with high-end European coastal markets.

### Athens STR Moratorium: What It Means for You

The Greek government imposed a short-term rental moratorium on Athens city centre through the end of 2026. The moratorium blocks new STR licences within the designated central zone. Specifically:

- No new Airbnb/STR registrations within the moratorium boundary
- Existing licenced operators continue legally
- The moratorium does not affect properties outside the zone (suburbs, Piraeus, Glyfada)
- Status after end-2026 is not yet confirmed, policy may extend or be modified

Investor implication: If you buy inside the moratorium zone expecting STR income, you cannot obtain an STR licence under current policy. Any listing on Airbnb or Booking.com without a valid licence is illegal and subject to fines up to €50,000. Do not rely on guidance that this rule is loosely enforced, enforcement has tightened.

Properties in suburbs, Piraeus and the Riviera are outside the moratorium zone but still require a valid STR licence through the MITAT registry.

## Thessaloniki: Greece's Most Underrated Yield Market

Thessaloniki is measured at 4.4% gross LTR on mid-range property while trading at a 30 to 40% discount to Athens per sqm in comparable neighbourhoods. That combination produces favourable return-on-capital metrics that many Athens-focused analyses miss.

What drives demand:

- Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, the largest university in Greece by student population. Student rental demand is structural and year-round.
- Business and trade hub, Thessaloniki is northern Greece's commercial centre, drawing corporate tenants and professionals.
- Infrastructure improvement, the metro system expansion (opening in phases from 2024) is reshaping neighbourhood desirability.

Best areas by investor profile:

- Kalamaria, south coast suburb, professionals, 5.0 to 5.5% gross LTR
- Toumba, mid-range residential, students and families, above the city's measured 4.4% average
- City centre / Aristotelous, higher STR potential where licenced, 5.0 to 6.0% gross LTR or 6.5 to 8% gross STR
- Panorama, upscale hillside area, lower yield (3.5 to 4.5%) but strong capital growth

Capital growth: Thessaloniki has lagged Athens on capital growth but is catching up. The metro expansion and improved transport links are beginning to reprice certain neighbourhoods. A 3 to 5% annual growth trajectory is broadly cited by local agents, versus Athens at 5 to 9% in prime areas.

## Crete: The STR Yield Leader: If You Have a Licence

Crete is the most cited high-yield market in Greece for a reason. Licensed STR operators in tourist-facing locations report gross yields of a higher figure on purchase price, occasionally higher on well-positioned properties in Chania harbour or the Elounda resort strip.

The gross-to-net compression on Crete STR:

Crete's yield story is compelling on gross but requires careful net modelling:

- Season length: The effective STR season runs May to October (approximately 5 to 6 months). For the remaining 6 to 7 months, occupancy is low or near-zero in most non-urban areas. Your 10% gross yield is built on a concentrated seasonal burst, not year-round income.
- OTA commission: Airbnb and Booking.com take 15 to 20% of booking value. A 10% gross yield with 20% OTA commission on a 6-month season is mathematically different from a 10% gross yield on year-round LTR.
- STR management complexity: Most foreign investors cannot self-manage. A local STR management company on Crete typically charges 18 to 25% all-in (including cleaning coordination, guest communication, maintenance call-outs). This is substantially higher than LTR management at 8 to 12%.
- Higher maintenance: STR properties face higher wear rates. Budget 1.5 to 2% of property value per year versus 1 to 1.5% for LTR.
- STR licence requirement: All STR properties in Greece must hold a valid MITAT registration. Crete municipalities have begun tightening enforcement. Operating without a licence risks fines of €10,000 to €50,000 per violation.

Net yield reality check (Crete STR, €200,000 property):
- Gross rent: €20,000 (10% gross)
- OTA commission (20%): -€4,000
- Management fee (22% of net after OTA): -€3,520
- Seasonal vacancy (30% of potential): implicit in above gross figure
- Maintenance (1.8%): -€3,600
- ENFIA: -€400
- Insurance (STR): -€500
- Accountant: -€800
- Income tax (15% on first €12k, 35% on balance): -€2,100
- Net rental income: ~€5,080 / Net yield: ~2.5%

This model differs from the worked example above because we're being stricter about the income tax calculation on a higher rental income stream. The actual outcome is sensitive to how your tax affairs are structured and your residency status, a local tax advisor is essential.

The Crete conclusion: Crete STR can deliver 4 to 6% net yield under favourable conditions (licensed property, efficient management, strong occupancy). At the same time, many investors enter at 10% gross and are surprised by 2.5 to 3.5% net. Underwrite Crete STR conservatively.

## Other Markets: Patra, Kavala and the Islands

### Patra

Greece's third-largest city, Patra, averages approximately 4.81% gross rental yield. A university city (University of Patras) with steady student demand, Patra offers lower entry prices than Athens or Thessaloniki (typically €800 to €1,400/sqm in residential areas). Net yields model to approximately 2.0 to 2.5%. Capital growth is slower than Athens, Patra is an income play for budget-constrained investors, not a growth story.

### Kavala

Kavala, the northeastern coastal city, averages approximately 3.47% gross yield, the weakest of the major Greek cities in GPG data. Smaller population, limited economic growth drivers and a thin buyer market on exit make Kavala difficult to recommend purely on yield. Local buyers dominate; international investor liquidity is poor.

### Mykonos and Santorini

These premium island markets earn about $26,400 a year per Mykonos listing and about €41,000 per Santorini listing on measured data, but entry prices are among Europe's most expensive (€5,000 to €15,000/sqm and above for premium stock). Absolute annual income can be high, but net yield as a percentage of a €2 to 5M property is modest. The investment case here is capital preservation, lifestyle use and total return, not income yield optimisation.

### Rhodes and Corfu

Mid-tier tourist islands offer STR gross yields of 7 to 9%, with entry prices more accessible than Mykonos (€1,500 to €3,500/sqm for mid-range stock). The STR operational requirements, seasonal compression and management costs apply equally. Net yields of 3 to 5% are achievable for well-run licensed STR operations.

## Short-term rental figures are revenue, not yield

The same problem that makes a quoted long-let yield a one-bedroom yield makes a quoted
short-let yield worse, because the short-let data is not published as a yield at all.

What is measured, and published, is revenue per listing:

| Market | Active listings | Average annual revenue | Occupancy | Average daily rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Crete | 33,148 | about $16,100 | 62% | $237 |
| Athens | 19,055 | about $17,100 | 60% | $116 |
| Mykonos | n/a | about $26,380 | 38.7% | $379 |
| Santorini | n/a | about €41,000 | 69% median | €161 |

Source: AirDNA market data as reported July 2026. Nationally the average daily rate was
€178.8 and revenue per available rental €115.4, up 11% year on year, while active listings
fell about 2.5%.

**Revenue is not yield, and the gap is a unit size.** Revenue is published per listing.
Prices are published per square metre. Converting one to the other requires the floor area,
which nothing pairs with the revenue. Run the Cretan figure across plausible sizes at the
island's €2,329 per square metre:

| Unit | Implied gross revenue yield |
|---|---:|
| 45 m² studio | about 14% |
| 60 m² apartment | about 11% |
| 90 m² house | about 7% |
| 140 m² house | about 5% |

The answer moves by a factor of three, and every published STR yield percentage has quietly
picked one of those rows for you without saying which.

**And it is gross booking revenue.** Platform commission, management, cleaning, linen,
utilities that run year-round against a season that does not, licensing and insurance all
come out before tax does. The expense stack later on this page is the part that decides
whether a headline number survives.

**For a Golden Visa buyer the whole question is moot twice over.** Short-term letting the
qualifying property is banned outright. And the 120 square metre rule would put that
property in the bottom row of the table above even if it were allowed. On both the long-let
and the short-let side, the size class a residency buyer is required to own is the one that
earns least.

## STR vs LTR: The Full Decision Framework

| Factor | LTR (Long-term Rental) | STR (Short-term / Airbnb) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross yield | 4.40% national, 5.43% Athens (measured) | Not published as a yield; see the revenue figures above |
| Net yield | 2.0 to 3.5% | 2.5 to 5.5% |
| Management complexity | Low | High |
| Vacancy risk | Low (1 tenant, long lease) | High (seasonal gaps) |
| STR licence required | No | Yes (MITAT registration) |
| GV property compatibility | Yes | No |
| Athens centre moratorium | Not affected | Blocked for new licences |
| Income predictability | High | Low |
| Operational presence | Low (quarterly inspections) | High (ongoing guest management) |
| Tax treatment | Progressive 15 to 35% | Same, but higher income = higher bracket |

When to choose LTR:
- You want predictable income without management overhead
- The property qualifies for or is purchased under Golden Visa
- You are buying inside the Athens STR moratorium zone
- You do not want to maintain an active management operation

When to choose STR:
- You have access to quality local management with STR track record
- The property is outside the moratorium zone with a valid or obtainable licence
- You can model net returns realistically (not just gross) and the deal still works
- You accept seasonal income volatility

## Golden Visa Properties and the STR Prohibition

The rule is clear: Properties purchased under the Greek Golden Visa program (€250,000 to €800,000 qualifying threshold depending on zone) cannot be used for short-term tourist rentals. Listing a Golden Visa property on Airbnb, Booking.com or any STR platform is a violation of the residency permit conditions.

What this means in practice:
- If your investment thesis requires STR income to make the numbers work, do not use a Golden Visa-qualifying structure for that property
- Long-term rentals (standard 12-month leases) are permitted for Golden Visa properties
- The restriction applies regardless of whether you actually list the property, the intent to use for STR is the issue

If you want both Golden Visa and STR income:
- Structure a portfolio: one GV-qualifying property held for LTR or personal use, a separate non-GV property for STR income
- Consult a lawyer who specialises in both GV compliance and STR licensing, these two areas intersect in ways that general advisors miss

## What Destroys Your Actual Return
1. Buying on gross yield without an expense model. The most common mistake. A 7% gross yield in a listing does not mean 7% net income. Build your own expense table before offer.

2. Acquiring an unlicensed STR property and assuming you can licence it. STR licences in Greece depend on municipal approvals and do not automatically survive a change of owner: a property first registered after 31 December 2024 generally loses its number on transfer. Not all properties qualify. In the Athens moratorium zone, new licences are blocked. In Crete, some municipal areas have imposed density caps. Verify licence status or eligibility before signing a purchase contract.

3. Missing the ENFIA assessment on the specific property. ENFIA is not uniform. Two apartments in the same building can have different assessments based on floor, surface and zone classification. Request the actual ENFIA bill from the current owner, not a generalised estimate.

4. Underestimating renovation and capex. Greece has significant stock of 1970s to 1980s era construction that requires electrical rewiring, plumbing upgrades and structural remediation. A €120,000 apartment requiring €30,000 of renovation is a €150,000 effective entry cost, recalculate your yield accordingly.

5. No local management in place before completion. Investors who close a property without a vetted management arrangement in place frequently spend their first 3 to 6 months finding a manager instead of earning rent. That vacancy is a real cost.

6. Currency drag for non-euro investors. Greece operates in EUR. If your home currency is USD, GBP, RUB or AED, exchange rate moves directly affect your effective return in home currency terms. A 5% net yield in EUR can become 2% or 8% in home currency depending on FX direction over a 5-year hold.

7. Ignoring exit liquidity. Smaller cities (Kavala, Patra) and some island markets have thin resale buyer pools for international investors. If your exit requires a non-Greek buyer, the buyer universe is smaller and time-to-sale is longer. Athens and Thessaloniki have significantly deeper liquidity.

8. Using STR income projections from peak season only. A property that earns €3,000 in August and €800 in November should not be modelled as €3,000 x 12. Build your yield model on annual occupancy, not peak month performance.

## Insider Tips: What Local Operators Know

Athens emerging neighbourhoods move faster than the data. Global Property Guide yield data lags local market movements by 6 to 12 months. Neighbourhoods like Votanikos, Sepolia and parts of Neos Kosmos are being repriced right now. Local agents with live transaction data will give you a more accurate picture than any published index.

The working-class LTR play is not fully arbitraged. Despite several years of media coverage, there is still significant variation in asking prices in Athens periphery. Vendors in areas like Peristeri and Nikaia price based on local comparables without awareness of international yield benchmarks. Patient buyers with cash can still find 7 to 8% gross yield properties at reasonable asking prices.

Renovation quality matters enormously for STR. Two properties on the same Crete street with the same licence can earn €15,000 or €25,000/year depending on how well they are fitted out. Airbnb's algorithm favours properties with higher review scores, which are directly correlated with fit-out quality and responsiveness. A €20,000 renovation investment can deliver €3,000 to €5,000 additional annual STR revenue.

LTR rents in Athens are still rising. The 2023 to 2025 period saw Athens rents increase 15 to 25% in many working-class neighbourhoods. Rent growth is compressing yields somewhat for new buyers (prices have risen faster than rents) but is rewarding existing landlords with improving income on original cost. If you underwrite at today's rent and hold five years, your yield-on-cost may be substantially higher.

ENFIA assessed values are being revised. The Greek government has been updating objective property values to better reflect market prices. Some properties may see ENFIA bills increase over the coming years as assessments are revised upward. Model this in your long-term hold analysis.

Tax treaty review is essential. Greece has double taxation agreements with numerous countries. Depending on your country of tax residence, Greek rental income tax may be offset against home-country tax obligations or may result in additional liability. This is a conversation to have with an accountant before, not after, purchase.

## How to Use This Guide to Underwrite a Specific Property

1. Obtain the asking price and current or projected monthly rent from the agent.
2. Calculate gross yield: (annual rent ÷ purchase price) × 100.
3. Apply the expense table above, using your specific management quote, the actual ENFIA bill (request from vendor), and your accountant's estimate of Greek income tax for your situation.
4. Calculate net yield: net annual income ÷ purchase price.
5. Compare net yield against alternatives: European bonds, other property markets, your home market.
6. Factor in capital growth assumptions based on the specific neighbourhood trajectory: not the Greek national average.
7. If net yield plus projected capital growth delivers a compelling total return versus alternatives, proceed to due diligence.
8. Before completing, verify STR licence status (if STR strategy), Golden Visa compatibility (if applicable), and commission a legal title search through a qualified Greek lawyer.
