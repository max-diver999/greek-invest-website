**Quick answer:** The Grandline is a fourteen-unit SECLAND scheme in Marousi, north Athens, marketed
from about €300,000 with typologies running from roughly 48 m² floor apartments to garden residences
near 224 m². Marousi is Attica, so a Golden Visa file needs €800,000 on one deed and 120 square metres
of certified main usable area. The consequence is that the Golden Visa line runs horizontally through
this building: most stacks fail one test or both, and only the largest typology can pass either.
This is a reading of public material, not a valuation and not a recommendation.

SECLAND does not pay Greek Invest and we sell nothing in this building. It is
reviewed here because a from-price and a residency threshold are routinely read as the same number, and
this building is the clearest example on the site of why they are not.

## The Golden Visa line runs horizontally through this building

Most schemes qualify or they do not. The Grandline does both, and which one applies depends on which
floor a unit sits on.

SECLAND's typologies span roughly 48 square metres to 224 square metres inside a single fourteen-unit
building. The Attica threshold is €800,000 on one deed with 120 square metres of certified main usable
area. Put those together and the building divides:

| Typology | Approximate enclosed area | Against the 120 m² test | Against the €800,000 test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor apartments, 1st to 2nd | ~58 m² | Fails | Fails at the from-price band |
| Larger floor stacks | up to ~162 m² | Can pass if certified | Depends entirely on the quoted deed price |
| Penthouse | ~58 m² enclosed plus large terraces | Fails: terraces are excluded | Priced as a premium unit regardless |
| Garden residences, ground | ~224 m² | Passes comfortably if certified | Only if the deed reaches €800,000 |

The penthouse is the instructive line. It is the most expensive kind of unit in most buildings and it is
the one least likely to qualify here, because what makes it expensive is outdoor area and what the
certificate counts is enclosed area. A buyer who assumes the top of the price list is the safest route to
a permit has the logic exactly backwards in this building.

## A from-price of €300,000 is accurate marketing and irrelevant to a residency file

SECLAND markets The Grandline from about €300,000. That figure is not misleading about the building; it
is true of the small typologies. It is misleading only if a reader carries it into a Golden Visa
question, where it has no bearing at all.

The threshold is tested on the notarial consideration recorded on one deed. €300,000 is not €800,000 and
no combination of units closes the gap: two adjoining apartments cannot be added together to reach either
the price or the area, because the rule is one property on one title. A buyer at the from-price is
buying a north Athens apartment, which may be an entirely sensible purchase, and is not buying a route to
residence.

This is the most common misreading on developer material anywhere in Attica, and it is worth being
explicit that it is a reading error rather than a marketing trick. The
[Golden Visa property tiers](/guides/greece-golden-visa-property-tiers-2026/) page sets out how the
threshold is actually assessed.

## What Marousi is, and what it is not

Marousi is north Athens: corporate headquarters, the Olympic complex, Metro Line 3, established
residential streets and international schools within reach. It is not central Athens and it is not the
Riviera, and it does not price like either.

For a Golden Visa asset the relevant characteristic is the tenant. Marousi lets to professionals and to
families who need the schools and the Metro, on twelve-month terms, all year, and twelve-month terms
are the only letting a qualifying deed may carry, since short-term letting of that deed is prohibited
for the life of the permit. Unlike central districts, Marousi never depended on tourist letting, so the
prohibition removes an income the district was not structured around in the first place.

What Marousi does not offer is the resale depth of the centre. The buyer pool for a north-suburb
apartment is real and stable and narrower than Kolonaki's, which matters at exit rather than at
purchase.

## Three sibling schemes, one price signal, and much less to check

Alongside this building SECLAND sells The Regal, Olympia Living and Heya Residence, and the four share
one number: a price-from signal near €250,000 to €300,000. Read against the €800,000 Attica floor, that
number is a statement about who the buyer is, not a discount on the permit.

The Regal is worth naming because so little about it is checkable. SECLAND publishes no municipality, no
unit count, no typology sizes and no delivery quarter for it, which means neither Golden Visa test can be
applied to it at all from public material. That is not an accusation of anything; it is the reason The
Grandline, with a published typology table, is the scheme in this portfolio a buyer can actually assess.
Ask for the building permit number and the horizontal-property table for any of the four before treating
a price-from figure as a price.

## The documents that settle eligibility here

Because this building's answer differs by unit, the due diligence is unit-level and document-led:

1. **The horizontal-property table** (πίνακας οριζόντιων ιδιοκτησιών), which states each unit's area and
   its share of the common parts.
2. **A certified enclosed area** for the specific unit, referenced to the Electronic Building Identity,
   with balconies, terraces, storage and parking itemised separately: the
   [120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) explains why that separation is the
   whole test.
3. **A written deed price for that unit**, not a band and not a from-price.
4. **Confirmation that it is one title**, since a garden residence sold with a separate storage title is
   two properties.
5. **The tax treatment**: 24% VAT as a first delivery or 3.09% transfer tax, confirmed by your own
   lawyer, which on an €800,000 deed is a difference of well over €150,000.
6. **Stage-payment security**, because Greek [off-plan practice](/guides/off-plan-property-greece-guide/)
   provides none by default, and a Q4 2026 delivery still leaves instalments exposed.

## The horizontal line splits the resale market too

The eligibility line running through this building does not disappear once you own. It reappears at
resale, because the sub-threshold units and the qualifying ones sell to different people.

A 48 square metre unit at a list price well under the Attica floor competes for Greek owner-occupiers and
domestic investors in a deep, liquid, price-sensitive market. A unit that clears €800,000 on one deed and
certifies 120 square metres competes for a much smaller pool of foreign residency buyers, and that pool's
size is set by policy rather than by the Athens market. Price the two halves of this building as two
different assets, because on exit that is what they are.

## Who this building suits

A buyer who wants a north Athens apartment and is choosing on the apartment. The from-price typologies
are priced for that buyer and the location supports them.

A Golden Visa buyer should approach the same building from the opposite end and with a specific unit in
mind: the ground-floor garden residences, at a deed price that reaches €800,000, with the enclosed area
certified in advance. That is a narrow target inside a fourteen-unit scheme, and a buyer who cannot
secure it should compare against schemes where the price test cannot fail, the
[Athens €800K areas guide](/guides/athens-golden-visa-800000-areas/) maps where that budget goes further
on size.
