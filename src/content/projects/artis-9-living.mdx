## The delivery date on this scheme has already passed

Solena marketed ARTIS 9 Living with a January 2026 handover. That date is behind us. Everything a buyer
normally has to take on trust in an off-plan purchase — whether the permit holds, whether the finish
matches the render, whether the developer completes on time — has, on this scheme, already resolved one
way or the other.

That makes ARTIS the only project reviewed on this site where the honest first question is not "what
could go wrong" but "what happened". It is a question with an answer, and the answer is obtainable
without a lawyer: go to Artis Street and look at the building.

## What a passed handover date changes about the due diligence

On a scheme still in construction, due diligence is a set of contractual protections you negotiate in
advance: a bank guarantee over instalments, a longstop date with a penalty attached, an annexed
specification schedule. Those clauses exist because nobody can inspect a building that is not there.

Once the marketed date is in the past, the same list becomes a set of factual enquiries:

| Off-plan question | What it becomes after the date passes |
|---|---|
| Will the permit hold? | Was the completion certificate issued, and when |
| Will the finish match the render? | Walk the unit and compare it to the schedule |
| Will delivery slip? | It either did or it did not; ask for the current handover date in writing |
| Are my instalments protected? | How much has already been paid by earlier buyers, and against what security |
| Is the developer solvent? | Check the GEMI filing, not the brochure |

A slipped date is not by itself evidence of a problem, and the [off-plan property guide](/guides/off-plan-property-greece-guide/) sets out how Greek stage payments are normally structured. Greek construction runs late for ordinary
reasons — permit revisions, materials, subcontractor sequencing — and a scheme of thirteen units has
little slack when one trade falls behind. But a slipped date that the developer has not restated in
writing is a different matter, and a buyer entering now is entering after other buyers have already
committed money into a timetable that did not hold.

## Thirteen units means the shared costs divide thirteen ways

Scheme size is not a detail on a boutique building, it is the denominator of every future bill. A
€40,000 façade repair or lift replacement divides across thirteen apartments at roughly €3,000 each; the
same work on a ninety-unit block is closer to €450 a door.

That cuts both ways and it is worth being clear about which way it cuts here. Small schemes give an
owner real influence in the building's assembly and a neighbour set small enough to reach agreement
quickly. They also concentrate every capital cost, and they make one non-paying owner a material problem
rather than a rounding error. For a Golden Visa buyer who will not live in the building and will let it
on twelve-month terms, that concentration is a running cost to model, not a lifestyle question.

Ask for the building regulation, the κανονισμός, and the current common-charge budget before the
preliminary agreement. On a scheme that has reached or passed completion, both documents exist.

## A++ energy is a rent argument, not a marketing one

Solena markets ARTIS at A++ energy class with smart-home fitting. On a Golden Visa asset that
specification matters more than it would on an owner-occupied home, for a specific reason: the only
income the permit allows is a twelve-month residential lease, and the person paying the heating bill
under such a lease is the tenant.

An A++ building in central Athens lets to a tenant whose winter running costs are a fraction of those in
the 1970s stock that surrounds it. In a district where the competing supply is older apartments with
poor insulation and individual boilers, that is a genuine letting advantage and it shows up as shorter
void periods rather than as higher rent. It is also the part of the specification that is hardest to
retrofit and easiest to verify: the energy performance certificate is a document, and it should be
annexed.

## Ampelokipi lets to people who work, not to people who visit

Artis Street sits in Ampelokipi, on the embassy side of central Athens and within walking distance of
Kolonaki. The district's tenant base is offices, hospitals, embassies and the Metro Line 3 corridor —
people who need to be in Athens on Tuesdays in February.

That matters because of what a Golden Visa asset may and may not earn. Short-term tourist letting of the
qualifying property is prohibited nationwide under Law 5100/2024 for the life of the permit, and the
practical cost of that ban depends entirely on what the alternative was. On a Cycladic island the ban
removes the only real income. In Ampelokipi it removes an income stream the district was never
structured around: this is not a tourist quarter, and the twelve-month professional tenant the permit
requires is the tenant the street already houses.

Note carefully that Ampelokipi is not inside the central short-term-rental moratorium, which applies to
the 1st, 2nd and 3rd districts of Athens. That is a separate restriction from the Golden Visa ban and it
affects non-qualifying stock in Plaka, Koukaki, Exarcheia and Kolonaki. The distinction is worth holding
because the two rules are often conflated, and the
[Athens short-term rental moratorium](/guides/athens-short-term-rental-moratorium-2026/) sets out which
districts are actually covered.

## What the €800,000 threshold does to a thirteen-unit central scheme

Ampelokipi is Attica, so the Golden Visa floor is €800,000 on a single deed with 120 square metres of
main usable area certified on the Electronic Building Identity. Solena does not publish a unit price
list for ARTIS, which means the qualifying question cannot be answered from public material at all: a
buyer has to obtain a written quote per stack, with the deed price and the certified enclosed area
stated separately.

Two failure modes follow from that and both are common in central boutique stock. Smaller two-bedroom
layouts in a thirteen-unit building frequently sit under 120 enclosed square metres however well they
are finished, and no price negotiation fixes a size shortfall. And a unit that clears 120 square metres
in a central location will usually be quoted well above €800,000, at which point the buyer should ask
what the premium is buying, because the permit does not care.

## What to establish before paying anything

1. The current handover position in writing, with a date, since the marketed one has passed.
2. The completion certificate if the building is finished, or the revised permit position if it is not,
   and a [snagging inspection](/guides/snagging-inspection-greece-new-build/) before the final payment.
3. Certified main usable area per unit, excluding balconies, referenced to the Electronic Building
   Identity — the [120 m² rule](/guides/greece-golden-visa-120-square-meter-rule/) explains what the
   certificate counts.
4. The energy performance certificate supporting the A++ claim, annexed to the contract.
5. The building regulation and the common-charge budget for a thirteen-unit assembly.
6. Whether the sale attracts 24% VAT as a first delivery or 3.09% transfer tax, confirmed by your own
   lawyer.
